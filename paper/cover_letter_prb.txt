Dear Editor,

We submit the revised manuscript for consideration as a Regular Article in
Physical Review B in response to the referee report.

The contribution is a condensed-matter physics result, not a new computational
descriptor. Under mechanically constrained joint load on quasi-hexagonal C60
graphullerene, additive strain--dopant energetic screening fails: totals on the
joint E(epsilon, delta) landscape need not equal the sum of separate strain-only
and substitution-only increments. The bilinear cross term in a Taylor expansion
of total energy can reach meV-per-atom magnitudes large enough to alter
energy differences at the scale relevant to energetic screening for
size-mismatched phosphorus. Linear strain coefficients
alpha do not reliably predict that omitted term. The matched four-corner
difference S_delta(epsilon) is the discrete strain--substitution interaction at
fixed dopant species delta. A local composition coordinate x, held at that
species, is the only place a mixed derivative partial^2 e / partial epsilon
partial x is identified with the finite difference. The quantity is a
constrained fixed-coordinate diagnostic: an upper-end protocol estimate
relative to the fully relaxed fixed-cell protocol studied here, not a universal
bound on every constraint and not the physical discovery. Two boundary conditions are kept separate. On periodic n=1 P at
+3%, ionic relaxation suppresses the constrained cross term to the reporting
floor. The tetramer P benchmark is a distinct boundary condition (vacuum box;
coordinates not affinely strained): the cross term changes from +1.19 to
-2.28 meV/atom (sign reversal and larger magnitude). It is not a retention
ratio for the periodic grid, and the data do not uniquely partition elastic
and electronic energy.

A separate Supplemental Material file (supplementary_figures.pdf) accompanies
this revision and is cited from the main text. It contains the finite-difference
derivation, literature-positioning and descriptor tables, the archived
legacy-PBE tetramer baseline and calculation inventory, tetramer pi-DOS and
periodic VBM/CBM isosurfaces, n>=6 coupling-energy rows, four-corner total
energies, Marcus reorganization energies, and the hopping-integral discussion.

Summary of revisions (Major comments):

(1) Constrained-load protocol — Fixed-coordinate alpha and S are a
    constrained diagnostic for epitaxial clamp, strong adhesion, or ultrafast
    load. Rigid |S| is an upper-end protocol estimate relative to the fully
    relaxed fixed-cell protocol studied here, not a universal bound on every
    substrate or load path. Periodic
    n=1 P at +3% shows ionic relaxation suppressing the cross term to the
    reporting floor. The tetramer P corners (Table relax) are a distinct
    boundary condition: +1.19 to -2.28 meV/atom (sign reversal and larger
    magnitude), not a retention ratio and not an orders-of-magnitude drop.
    E_sub is not a thermodynamic formation enthalpy.

(2) Alternate dopant placement — Table IV documents reference and alternate
    seed-137 maps (18/18 PBE+D3 rigid ENERGY tasks on the tetramer grid;
    16/16 periodic placement audits on seeds 137 and 271). Periodic |S|
    magnitudes remain configuration-specific; ranking trends are reported
    with placement caveats.

(3) Mechanistic depth — Table V and Discussion report observables
    (covalent-radius mismatch, mean cage distortion, Hirshfeld charge, and
    pi-DOS) that are consistent with a pre-strain, geometry-mediated
    contribution for phosphorus. The data do not uniquely partition S into
    elastic and electronic energetic components. Mayer bond orders and Bader
    charges were not computed.

(4) Argumentation — The S--alpha comparison is qualitative only
    (concentration and boundary mismatch). Stability language is scoped to
    relative margins among comparable chemistries. Discussion IV is
    restructured (main finding, mechanism, literature, limitations, outlook)
    with PRB-appropriate passive tone.

(5) Robustness — Table sigma_S reports an error budget (SCF, cutoff, vdW,
    ionic relaxation, k-sampling, XC sensitivity); SCAN/r2SCAN and dense-k
    benchmarks remain honestly pending in Limitations.

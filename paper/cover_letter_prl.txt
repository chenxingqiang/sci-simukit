Manuscript: Non-Additive Strain--Doping Coupling in Covalent Molecular Networks:

Dear Editor,

We submit the enclosed Letter for consideration in Physical Review Letters.

Context.
Strain and heteroatom substitution are standard levers in materials design, yet high-throughput pipelines almost always superpose separate strain-only and doping-only scans. That additivity assumption is rarely tested for discrete covalent pi-networks---fullerene frameworks, COF-like sheets, and molecular crystals---where sequential screens can mis-rank dopants or strain set-points. Quasi-hexagonal (qHP) C60 graphullerene provides a rigorous model platform with experimentally accessible synthesis and well-defined pi connectivity.

Key findings.
We introduce a synergy order parameter S that quantifies the total-energy cross term when biaxial strain and B/N/P substitution are applied jointly. In periodic qHP cells, |S| reaches 31.9 meV/atom for P at +3% tension---enough to reorder sequential-screening margins---yet does not track the linear strain coefficient alpha (opposite signs for N vs. B; smallest |alpha| but largest |S| for P). Three mechanistic classes (electronic B/N, structural P, finite-size N) support a general correction principle for strain-doping co-design beyond a single material. IPR/J transport-adjacent checks are confined to Supplemental Material.

Audience and fit.
The Letter addresses a cross-cutting assumption in condensed-matter and materials physics, not a niche graphullerene parameter study. The correction principle is posed for discrete pi-networks generally; qHP C60 supplies converged model evidence. We believe the result merits PRL's broad readership rather than a specialty materials journal.

100-WORD JUSTIFICATION FOR PRL (paste into submission form)
================================================================================

This Letter challenges a default assumption in materials design: that strain and doping increments are additive. In a covalent molecular network used as a model platform, we quantify a large total-energy cross term and show that its magnitude does not follow linear strain sensitivity, with structural dopants dominating electronic ones. The result yields a general correction for coupled strain-doping workflows in discrete pi-networks, including COFs and molecular crystals, and should interest readers across condensed-matter and materials physics beyond one specific allotrope.

\subsection{Strain--dopant coupling energy}
\label{sec:methods_coupling}

The physical hypothesis is the bilinear cross term in Eq.~\eqref{eq:taylor_coupling} (Introduction): under constrained load, $c\neq 0$ and can be large enough to invalidate additive screening.
The continuum coefficient $\gamma\equiv\partial^2 E/\partial\epsilon\,\partial\delta$ per atom (equivalently $c$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:taylor_coupling}) characterizes the \emph{intrinsic} mixed energetic response of the host under stated boundary conditions.
$\mathcal{S}$ is the symmetric four-corner \emph{finite-difference estimator} of that response at $+3$\% load---not a postulated order parameter and not interchangeable with $\gamma$ unless higher-order remainders are negligible (descriptor comparison: Supplemental Material, Table~\ref{S-tab:descriptor}; full derivation: Supplemental Material, Sec.~\ref{S-sec:appendix_fd}).

\paragraph{Taylor expansion and discrete composition.}
Eq.~\eqref{eq:taylor_coupling} is a local expansion of the energy per atom $e(\epsilon,x)$ about the pristine reference.
Although dopant composition is sampled discretely (one substituent label per C$_{60}$ on a fixed map), the expansion is interpreted as a finite-difference representation of the underlying $E(\epsilon,\delta)$ functional on the audited stencil, not as a strict differential expansion in a continuous concentration field.
It requires sufficiently small strain and a single smooth energy branch; at large strain (${\gtrsim}10$\%) or across phase transitions, Eq.~\eqref{eq:taylor_coupling} no longer applies (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion_limitations}).

\begin{equation}
\mathcal{S}(\epsilon,\delta)
= \Delta E(\epsilon,\delta) - \bigl[\Delta E(\epsilon,0) + \Delta E(0,\delta)\bigr],
\label{eq:synergy_order}
\end{equation}
with
$\Delta E(\epsilon,\delta)=\bigl[E(\epsilon,\delta)-E(0,\mathrm{pristine})\bigr]/N_{\mathrm{atoms}}$
and $\delta{=}0$ denoting the pristine reference at the same supercell size $n\times\mathrm{C}_{60}$.
Equivalently, on corners $(0,0)$, $(\Delta\epsilon,0)$, $(0,\Delta\delta)$, $(\Delta\epsilon,\Delta\delta)$,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{S}(\epsilon,\delta)=\frac{E(\epsilon,\delta)-E(\epsilon,0)-E(0,\delta)+E(0,0)}{N_{\mathrm{atoms}}},
\label{eq:four_corner}
\end{equation}
which depends only on the four corner totals (reference independence).

\paragraph{Why a four-point stencil?}
Among relaxed free-energy differences $\Delta G$, enthalpic formation energies with chemical potentials, or pairwise binding increments, the symmetric four-corner form in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:synergy_order}--\eqref{eq:four_corner} is the minimal rectangular-grid stencil that (i)~uses four DFT totals, (ii)~is second-order accurate in $(\Delta\epsilon,\Delta\delta)$ for the bilinear term, (iii)~cancels all first-order strain and composition increments and any shared energy offset, and (iv)~equals the additive-screening bias by construction.
It is not unique: central-difference, least-squares polynomial fits, or full mixed Hessians on continuous composition fields are alternative estimators of $\partial^2 E/\partial\epsilon\,\partial\delta$ with different cost, bias, and interpretability (Supplemental Material, Sec.~\ref{S-sec:why_four}; Table~\ref{S-tab:descriptor}).
Asymmetric three-point schemes retain linear contamination; alternative asymmetric stencils differ from Eq.~\eqref{eq:four_corner} by $\mathcal{O}(\Delta\epsilon,\Delta\delta)$.

\paragraph{Finite-difference link to the mixed derivative.}
Expanding each corner total to second order and forming Eq.~\eqref{eq:four_corner} cancels $E_\epsilon\,\Delta\epsilon$ and $E_\delta\,\Delta\delta$ identically (Supplemental Material, Sec.~\ref{S-sec:four_corner_deriv}), yielding
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{S}(\epsilon,\delta)\approx
\frac{\partial^2 E}{\partial\epsilon\,\partial\delta}\,
\frac{\Delta\epsilon\,\Delta\delta}{N_{\mathrm{atoms}}}
+ \mathcal{O}(\Delta\epsilon^2,\Delta\delta^2,\Delta\epsilon^2\delta,\epsilon\delta^2),
\label{eq:mixed_derivative}
\end{equation}
so $\mathcal{S}$ reports the discrete mixed second derivative of total energy on the audited $(\epsilon,\delta)$ grid.
At finite $+3$\% biaxial load and discrete B/N/P labels, higher-order terms in Eq.~\eqref{eq:mixed_derivative} are not separately resolved on the production periodic map; multi-amplitude strain audits ($\epsilon=1$--$3$\%) are targeted sensitivity tests (Limitations).
Throughout the main text, ``nonlinear coupling'' refers to a nonzero mixed response estimated by $\mathcal{S}$, not to $\mathcal{S}$ itself as a fundamental constant.

\paragraph{Units, symmetry, and gauge invariance.}
$\mathcal{S}$ is reported in meV per atom; $\eta$ [Eq.~\eqref{eq:additive_fraction}] is dimensionless; $\gamma=\partial^2 E/\partial\epsilon\,\partial\delta$ carries units of energy per atom per strain increment per composition increment, with $\mathcal{S}\approx\gamma\,\Delta\epsilon\,\Delta\delta$ on the production grid up to truncation error.
The symmetric stencil is invariant under exchanging the order of the $\epsilon$ and $\delta$ increments on the grid when mixed partials commute (Clairaut symmetry; Supplemental Material, Sec.~\ref{S-sec:symmetry_gauge})---a property of the estimator, not a claim that strain and chemical substitution are interchangeable controls.
$\mathcal{S}$ is invariant under arbitrary uniform energy offsets shared by all four corners.
Strain $\epsilon$ and dopant label $\delta$ are distinct physical control axes in the production protocol.

\paragraph{Error propagation.}
If each corner carries uncorrelated equal per-atom uncertainty $\sigma_e$,
\begin{equation}
\sigma_{\mathcal{S}}^2 = 4\sigma_e^2,
\qquad
\sigma_{\mathcal{S}} = 2\sigma_e,
\label{eq:sigma_propagation}
\end{equation}
from the four-term difference in Eq.~\eqref{eq:four_corner} (derivation: Supplemental Material, Sec.~\ref{S-sec:error_prop}).
Table~\ref{tab:sigma_S} lists demonstrated and pending contributions to the reporting band; we use $\sigma_{\mathcal{S}}\lesssim 2$~meV/atom as a conservative protocol floor on the $n\leq 4$ grid when interpreting the smallest periodic points ($2.3$--$3.0$~meV/atom for N and B at $n{=}1$).
Periodic $\mathcal{S}$ values in the main text are from the $+3$\% size-scaling grid (Table~\ref{tab:IV}).

We report $\mathcal{S}$ in meV per atom; $\mathcal{S}<0$ ($>0$) means the coupled corner is more (less) stable than the additive estimate.

To quantify additive failure magnitude we report
\begin{equation}
\eta \equiv \frac{|\mathcal{S}|}{|\Delta E_{\mathrm{coupled}}|},
\label{eq:additive_fraction}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta E_{\mathrm{coupled}}=(E(\epsilon,\delta)-E(0,0))/N_{\mathrm{atoms}}$.
At $n{=}4$ and $+3$\% strain, $\eta\approx 8\%$ for P ($|\mathcal{S}|=23.7$~meV/atom vs.\ $|\Delta E_{\mathrm{coupled}}|\approx 283$~meV/atom), whereas B and N remain below $1$\%.

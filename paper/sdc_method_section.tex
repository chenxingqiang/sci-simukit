\subsection{Strain--dopant coupling energy}
\label{sec:methods_coupling}

\paragraph{Energy landscape and Taylor expansion.}
Joint mechanical and chemical load is specified by biaxial strain $\epsilon$ and substitutional species $\delta\in\{\mathrm{B},\mathrm{N},\mathrm{P}\}$.
Let $E(\epsilon,\delta)$ denote the total energy of the matched supercell at strain $\epsilon$ and dopant label $\delta$ (pristine for $\delta{=}0$).
Expanding the energy per atom $e(\epsilon,\delta)=E(\epsilon,\delta)/N_{\mathrm{atoms}}$ about the pristine, zero-strain reference in a continuous composition parameter $x$ (holding supercell size fixed) gives, schematically,
\begin{equation}
e(\epsilon,x) \approx e_{0} + a\,\epsilon + b\,x + c\,\epsilon\,x + \mathcal{O}(\epsilon^2,x^2,\epsilon^2 x,\epsilon x^2),
\label{eq:taylor_coupling}
\end{equation}
where the bilinear term $c\,\epsilon x$ is the lowest-order \emph{non-additive} coupling between external strain and composition.
Sequential strain-only and doping-only workflows estimate $e(\epsilon,x)$ by keeping only the linear terms $a\epsilon+bx$; they omit $c\,\epsilon x$ and all higher-order cross terms.
For finite strain and discrete dopants, the same omitted content is estimated by a four-point finite difference.

\paragraph{Four-corner finite difference [Eq.~\eqref{eq:synergy_order}].}
We refer to this interaction energy as the \emph{strain--dopant coupling energy} $\mathcal{S}$ (a bilinear coupling metric, not an order parameter for a broken-symmetry phase):
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{S}(\epsilon,\delta)
= \Delta E(\epsilon,\delta) - \bigl[\Delta E(\epsilon,0) + \Delta E(0,\delta)\bigr],
\label{eq:synergy_order}
\end{equation}
with
$\Delta E(\epsilon,\delta)=\bigl[E(\epsilon,\delta)-E(0,\mathrm{pristine})\bigr]/N_{\mathrm{atoms}}$
and $\delta{=}0$ denoting the pristine reference at the same supercell size $n\times\mathrm{C}_{60}$.
Algebraically,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{S}(\epsilon,\delta)=\frac{E(\epsilon,\delta)-E(\epsilon,0)-E(0,\delta)+E(0,0)}{N_{\mathrm{atoms}}},
\label{eq:four_corner}
\end{equation}
so $\mathcal{S}$ depends only on the four corner totals and is independent of any arbitrary additive offset common to all cells.
Only a four-state construction removes the linear strain response, the linear doping shift, and the shared pristine reference in one gauge-fixed estimator; three-point or sequential-difference schemes retain spurious linear contamination.
In the limit of small $\epsilon$ and a smooth composition path, $\mathcal{S}/(\epsilon\,\Delta x)$ approaches a discrete mixed derivative $\partial^2 e/\partial\epsilon\,\partial x$; at the finite $+3$\% loads used here, $\mathcal{S}$ reports the \emph{nonlinear} cross term that additive models drop, not merely an infinitesimal curvature.

\paragraph{Schematic energy functional.}
Eq.~\eqref{eq:taylor_coupling} can be read as a minimal two-parameter energy functional
$e(\epsilon,x)\approx e_{\mathrm{elastic}}(\epsilon)+e_{\mathrm{chem}}(x)+c\,\epsilon x$,
where $e_{\mathrm{elastic}}$ and $e_{\mathrm{chem}}$ are the strain-only and composition-only increments estimated by separate scans, and $c\,\epsilon x$ is the omitted bilinear work when a dopant-induced \emph{internal stress field} overlaps nonlinearly with external biaxial load on a soft cage.
In linear elastic inclusion theory the cross term vanishes at leading order; qHP C$_{60}$ departs from that limit because intramolecular buckling stores internal stress that external tension can release cooperatively (Discussion).

\paragraph{Physical meaning, sign, and units.}
Physically, $\mathcal{S}$ measures the energetic cost of \emph{nonlinear} interaction between externally imposed biaxial strain and dopant-induced internal stress: when internal cage distortion and macroscopic load are applied together, the total energy need not equal the sum of separate strain-only and doping-only increments.
We report $\mathcal{S}$ in meV per atom so that values are comparable across supercell sizes $n\times\mathrm{C}_{60}$ at fixed one-substituent-per-C$_{60}$ loading (${\sim}1.67$~at.\% in periodic cells); the same per-atom unit is used for tetramer (${\sim}5$~at.\%) audits with the understanding that concentration differs (Sec.~\ref{sec:generality}).
By convention, $\mathcal{S}<0$ means the coupled $(\epsilon,\delta)$ corner is \emph{more stable} than the additive estimate (cooperative or stabilizing coupling under load), whereas $\mathcal{S}>0$ means the coupled state is \emph{less stable} than additive sequential scans predict (anti-cooperative coupling).
Because $\delta$ is a discrete chemical label rather than a continuous composition coordinate, $\mathcal{S}(\epsilon,\mathrm{B})$, $\mathcal{S}(\epsilon,\mathrm{N})$, and $\mathcal{S}(\epsilon,\mathrm{P})$ are not required to follow a single smooth surface in $\delta$; cross-species comparisons are meaningful only on matched site maps at the stated concentrations.

\paragraph{Size scaling and uncertainty.}
$|\mathcal{S}|$ is not constrained to decay linearly with $1/n$: electrostatic dilution, cutoff choice, and cage-local distortion can make the cross term peak at intermediate or small $n$ (Table~\ref{tab:IV}; Fig.~\ref{fig:synergy}).
If each corner total energy is converged independently with an uncorrelated per-atom uncertainty $\sigma_e$, a conservative propagation estimate for Eq.~\eqref{eq:four_corner} is $\sigma_{\mathcal{S}}\approx 2\sigma_e$ (four equal contributions in quadrature).
In practice, identical numerical settings across corners induce partial cancellation, so realized $\mathcal{S}$ noise is often below this upper bound (Sec.~\ref{sec:validation}).
A partial cutoff audit shifts one corner total by only ${\sim}0.057$~meV/atom between 350 and 400~Ry at $6\times\mathrm{C}_{60}$ N and $+3$\%, implying $|\Delta\mathcal{S}|<0.2$~meV/atom for matched protocols when only one mesh parameter drifts.
We therefore report conservative internal uncertainties of $\sigma_{\mathcal{S}}\lesssim 2$~meV/atom for the $n\leq 4$ production grid (well below the P peaks at 8--32~meV/atom) and treat $|{\mathcal{S}}|>{\sim}2$~meV/atom as above protocol noise for the smallest audited periodic points (e.g., $2.3$~meV/atom for N at $n{=}1$).
Periodic $\mathcal{S}$ values in the main text are taken from the $+3$\% size-scaling grid (Table~\ref{tab:IV}).

\paragraph{Additive failure metric.}
To quantify how large a failure of additivity is, we report the \emph{additive fractional error}
\begin{equation}
\eta \equiv \frac{|\mathcal{S}|}{|\Delta E_{\mathrm{coupled}}|},
\label{eq:additive_fraction}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta E_{\mathrm{coupled}}=(E(\epsilon,\delta)-E(0,0))/N_{\mathrm{atoms}}$ is the coupled corner increment from the pristine reference at the same supercell size.
At $n{=}4$ and $+3$\% strain, $\eta\approx 8\%$ for P ($|\mathcal{S}|=23.7$~meV/atom vs.\ $|\Delta E_{\mathrm{coupled}}|\approx 283$~meV/atom), whereas B and N remain below $1\%$ because their $|\mathcal{S}|$ is small relative to the coupled shift dominated by substitution and strain-only increments.
$\eta$ is a \emph{ranking} diagnostic for sequential workflows, not a universal thermodynamic significance threshold; it should be read alongside $|{\mathcal{S}}|$ and the audited distribution in Fig.~\ref{fig:synergy}(d).

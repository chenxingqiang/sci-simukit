\subsection{Strain--dopant coupling energy}
\label{sec:methods_coupling}

The physical hypothesis is the bilinear cross term $c\,\epsilon x$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:taylor_coupling} (Introduction): under constrained load, $c\neq 0$ and can be large enough to invalidate additive screening.
$\mathcal{S}$ is the symmetric four-corner finite-difference \emph{estimator} of that term at $+3$\% load---not a postulated order parameter (descriptor comparison: Supplemental Material, Table~\ref{S-tab:descriptor}; thermodynamic caveats: Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion_limitations}).
Among relaxed free-energy differences $\Delta G$, enthalpic formation energies with chemical potentials, or pairwise binding increments, Eq.~\eqref{eq:synergy_order} is the unique four-corner construction that removes independent strain-only and doping-only contributions in one step.

\begin{equation}
\mathcal{S}(\epsilon,\delta)
= \Delta E(\epsilon,\delta) - \bigl[\Delta E(\epsilon,0) + \Delta E(0,\delta)\bigr],
\label{eq:synergy_order}
\end{equation}
with
$\Delta E(\epsilon,\delta)=\bigl[E(\epsilon,\delta)-E(0,\mathrm{pristine})\bigr]/N_{\mathrm{atoms}}$
and $\delta{=}0$ denoting the pristine reference at the same supercell size $n\times\mathrm{C}_{60}$.
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{S}(\epsilon,\delta)=\frac{E(\epsilon,\delta)-E(\epsilon,0)-E(0,\delta)+E(0,0)}{N_{\mathrm{atoms}}},
\label{eq:four_corner}
\end{equation}
which depends only on the four corner totals (reference independence).
In the continuum limit of small strain and composition increments,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{S}(\epsilon,\delta)\approx
\frac{\partial^2 E}{\partial\epsilon\,\partial\delta}\,
\Delta\epsilon\,\Delta\delta
+ \mathcal{O}(\Delta\epsilon^2,\Delta\delta^2,\Delta\epsilon^2\delta,\epsilon\delta^2),
\label{eq:mixed_derivative}
\end{equation}
so $\mathcal{S}$ reports the discrete mixed second derivative of total energy on the audited $(\epsilon,\delta)$ grid.
Only a four-state construction removes linear strain, linear doping, and shared reference offsets in one symmetric estimator; at finite $+3$\% load and discrete B/N/P labels, $\mathcal{S}$ quantifies the nonlinear cross term that additive models drop.

We report $\mathcal{S}$ in meV per atom; $\mathcal{S}<0$ ($>0$) means the coupled corner is more (less) stable than the additive estimate.
Strain $\epsilon$ and dopant label $\delta$ are not interchangeable control axes.

If each corner carries uncorrelated per-atom uncertainty $\sigma_e$, error propagation gives $\sigma_{\mathcal{S}}=2\sigma_e$.
Table~\ref{tab:sigma_S} lists demonstrated and pending contributions to the reporting band; we use $\sigma_{\mathcal{S}}\lesssim 2$~meV/atom as a conservative protocol floor on the $n\leq 4$ grid when interpreting the smallest periodic points ($2.3$--$3.0$~meV/atom for N and B at $n{=}1$).
Periodic $\mathcal{S}$ values in the main text are from the $+3$\% size-scaling grid (Table~\ref{tab:IV}).

To quantify additive failure magnitude we report
\begin{equation}
\eta \equiv \frac{|\mathcal{S}|}{|\Delta E_{\mathrm{coupled}}|},
\label{eq:additive_fraction}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta E_{\mathrm{coupled}}=(E(\epsilon,\delta)-E(0,0))/N_{\mathrm{atoms}}$.
At $n{=}4$ and $+3$\% strain, $\eta\approx 8\%$ for P ($|\mathcal{S}|=23.7$~meV/atom vs.\ $|\Delta E_{\mathrm{coupled}}|\approx 283$~meV/atom), whereas B and N remain below $1$\%.

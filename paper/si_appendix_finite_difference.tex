\section{Discrete strain--substitution interaction}
\label{sec:appendix_fd}

The production estimator is a discrete interaction at fixed dopant species.
B, N, and P are chemical labels, not three numerical samples of one continuous coordinate $\delta$.
For a fixed species $\delta$ and a biaxial load $\epsilon$,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{S}_\delta(\epsilon)
\equiv
\frac{
E(\epsilon,\delta)-E(\epsilon,0)-E(0,\delta)+E(0,0)
}{N_{\mathrm{atoms}}},
\label{eq:S_mixed}
\end{equation}
which is the same four-corner combination as Eqs.~\eqref{eq:synergy_order} and~\eqref{eq:four_corner}.
It cancels the strain-only and substitution-only increments and retains their joint remainder.
It is not $\partial^2 E/\partial\epsilon\partial\delta$.

A mixed derivative appears only after a separate, local composition coordinate $x$ is introduced at that fixed species.
In that local representation the cross coefficient below is a finite-difference image of $\partial^2 e/\partial\epsilon\partial x$, not a derivative along a shared B/N/P axis.

\subsection{Local expansion at fixed species}
\label{sec:taylor}

Let $e(\epsilon,x)$ be the total energy per atom, with $\epsilon$ the biaxial load and $x$ a local composition coordinate defined only after the species $\delta$ has been fixed.
About the pristine, unloaded reference $(\epsilon,x)=(0,0)$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
e(\epsilon,x) &= e_0
+ e_\epsilon\,\epsilon
+ e_x\,x
+ \tfrac12 e_{\epsilon\epsilon}\,\epsilon^2
+ \tfrac12 e_{xx}\,x^2 \\
&\quad
+ e_{\epsilon x}\,\epsilon x
+ \mathcal{O}(3),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:taylor_total}
\end{equation}
where subscripts denote partial derivatives evaluated at the reference and $e_{\epsilon x}\equiv\partial^2 e/\partial\epsilon\partial x$.
The four corners of one species are $e(0,0)$, $e(\epsilon,0)$, $e(0,x)$, and $e(\epsilon,x)$.
Forming the inclusion--exclusion combination gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&e(\epsilon,x)-e(\epsilon,0)-e(0,x)+e(0,0) \\
&\quad=
e_{\epsilon x}\,\epsilon x
+ \mathcal{O}(\epsilon^2 x,\,\epsilon x^2,\,\epsilon^3,\,x^3).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cross_combination}
\end{equation}
Every term that depends on $\epsilon$ alone or on $x$ alone cancels.
Dividing by $\epsilon x$ isolates the mixed derivative of this local chart,
\begin{equation}
\frac{e(\epsilon,x)-e(\epsilon,0)-e(0,x)+e(0,0)}{\epsilon x}
= e_{\epsilon x} + \mathcal{O}(\epsilon,x).
\label{eq:cross_result}
\end{equation}
The main-text order parameter is this interaction reported per atom at the production load ($+3\%$) and at the discrete label $\delta$, without dividing by a continuous $\Delta x$.
The same cancellation is what Eq.~\eqref{eq:four_corner} states for total energies.

\subsection{Why the four-corner quotient is not the local slope}
\label{sec:four_corner_deriv}

Dividing the per-atom interaction by the load,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\mathcal{S}_\delta(\epsilon)}{\epsilon}
= e_{\epsilon x}\,x + \mathcal{O}(\epsilon,x),
\label{eq:why_four_fd}
\end{equation}
still carries the local composition step $x$.
It is therefore not equal to $e_{\epsilon x}$, and it is not a derivative with respect to dopant identity.
Equation~\eqref{eq:mixed_derivative} uses that quotient only as a finite-difference image inside one local chart.
The manuscript reports $\mathcal{S}_\delta(\epsilon)$ itself.

\subsection{What the four corners remove}
\label{sec:why_four}

A strain-only scan at fixed $\delta=0$ retains $e_\epsilon\epsilon+\tfrac12 e_{\epsilon\epsilon}\epsilon^2$.
A substitution-only scan at fixed $\epsilon=0$ retains $e_x x+\tfrac12 e_{xx}x^2$.
Adding those two scans double-counts nothing in the cross term and still omits $e_{\epsilon x}\epsilon x$.
The four-corner combination deletes both one-variable polynomials through the order kept in Eq.~\eqref{eq:taylor_total} and retains the mixed term.
That is the sense in which sequential scans are additive and the joint evaluation is not: the omitted piece is $e_{\epsilon x}\epsilon x$, up to higher-order remainders.

\subsection{Gauge and higher-order content}
\label{sec:symmetry_gauge}

Shifting the energy zero cancels inside Eq.~\eqref{eq:cross_combination}, so $\mathcal{S}_\delta$ does not depend on a global energy offset.
At the production load of $+3\%$ and at a full substitution on the doped cell, the steps in $\epsilon$ and in local $x$ are not infinitesimal.
Cubic and higher terms then sit inside the reported interaction.
The manuscript does not separate them from $e_{\epsilon x}\epsilon x$.
Table~\ref{tab:descriptor} records that limit in one line: the mixed derivative is defined only inside the local chart, and the higher-order remainder is not resolved.

\subsection{Propagation of corner noise}
\label{sec:error_prop}

For four uncorrelated corner energies with equal standard uncertainty $\sigma_E$, the numerator of Eq.~\eqref{eq:S_mixed} has uncertainty $2\sigma_E$.
Per atom, with $\sigma_e=\sigma_E/N_{\mathrm{atoms}}$,
\begin{equation}
\sigma_{\mathcal{S}}=2\sigma_e.
\label{eq:sigma_propagation_si}
\end{equation}
This is the same quadrature as Eq.~\eqref{eq:sigma_propagation}.
Correlated SCF noise across chemically related corners would reduce the effective coefficient below $2$; the main-text reporting band uses $2\sigma_e$ as a conservative reporting floor (Table~\ref{tab:sigma_S}).
That floor is a statistical envelope on corner noise.
It is distinct from the rigid-protocol estimate, which compares the fixed-coordinate evaluation with the fully relaxed fixed-cell evaluation studied in the main text and is not a bound on every constraint manifold.

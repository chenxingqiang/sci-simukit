\begin{abstract}
Substitutional doping and biaxial strain are jointly used to tune covalent molecular networks, yet total energies are still often approximated by superposing results from separate composition-only and strain-only density-functional theory calculations.
We study quasi-hexagonal C$_{60}$ graphullerene with the PBE+D3 functional and define a synergy order parameter $\mathcal{S}$ (meV/atom) for the total-energy cross term missing from sequential strain--doping scans.
On B/N/P tetramers and periodic $n\times\mathrm{C}_{60}$ supercells ($n=1$--$8$) under matched PBE+D3, linear strain coefficients $\alpha$ and $|\mathcal{S}|$ decouple on rigid tetramer grids (Table~\ref{tab:I}), while phosphorus exhibits the largest joint-load cross term on periodic cells, with $|\mathcal{S}|$ reaching $31.9$~meV/atom at $n{=}1$ under $+3$\% biaxial strain.
These rigid-strain values represent upper bounds on equilibrium coupling; ionic relaxation can substantially reduce or even reverse the synergy magnitude.
Our results demonstrate that stability ranking under load requires explicit coupled strain--doping evaluations for size-mismatched dopants and large biaxial strain, rather than additive composition--strain models used in sequential high-throughput screening.
\end{abstract}

\section{\label{sec:intro}Introduction}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

Covalent molecular networks---from graphullerenes to covalent organic frameworks---are often tuned jointly by substitutional chemistry and mechanical load, yet many high-throughput pipelines still construct $E_{\mathrm{tot}}(\epsilon,\delta)$ by combining \emph{separate} doping-only and strain-only density-functional theory runs~\cite{Materials2024untangling,Li2024strain,Katiyar2025strain}.
Defect formation energies and substitutional stabilities under strain are classical questions in defect physics, but most workflows still assume that composition-only and strain-only increments superpose linearly---an assumption that breaks down when dopant-induced internal stress couples nonlinearly to external load.
The omitted cross term is a systematic bias in sequential screening: it arises whenever a dopant introduces a \emph{local chemical-mismatch stress field} that couples nonlinearly to an external biaxial load, so additive composition--strain models can incorrectly rank relative stability even when dopant ordering at $\epsilon{=}0$ is unchanged.
Quasi-hexagonal (qHP) C$_{60}$ graphullerene~\cite{Yang2021two,Capobianco2024electron} is a covalently linked molecular network whose elastic, optical, and transport properties have been surveyed extensively~\cite{Tromer2022dft,Lv2026covalent,Capobianco2024electron,Silva2024large}, yet prior work does not report a periodic total-energy cross term under joint biaxial $(\epsilon,\delta)$ load.
Ab initio models of pristine qHP networks report intrinsic intramolecular strain from the eight-bond hexagonal connectivity~\cite{Makov2023graphullerene} (${\sim}0.1$--$0.2$~eV-scale cage distortions relative to isolated C$_{60}$ in prior surveys), so external biaxial load couples to an intrinsically pre-strained, \emph{soft} molecular cage.
Eshelby-type linear superposition assumes a linear elastic continuum; because the pre-strained fullerene cage can enter a nonlinear elastic regime, additive superposition of defect and external fields is not guaranteed.
Beyond pristine monolayers, strain and substitution independently reshape band edges~\cite{Li2024strain,Peng2025monolayer,LopezAlcalay2025graphendofullerene}, and substitutional B/N engineering can yield spin-polarized half-semiconductor gaps~\cite{Khan2025tuning}.
Related 2D fullerene networks show anisotropic mechanical and tribological response under load~\cite{Wang2024simulation,Qiu2025atomic}, and endohedral qHP C$_{60}$ cage filling reshapes band gaps~\cite{Pereira2026endohedral}.
None of these reports a periodic total-energy cross term under joint biaxial $(\epsilon,\delta)$ load.
Non-additive coupling is documented in other two-dimensional networks~\cite{Materials2024untangling,Alihosseini2023strain}; a quantitative \emph{total-energy} synergy order parameter $\mathcal{S}$ [Eq.~\eqref{eq:synergy_order}] remains unquantified for substitutional B/N/P qHP C$_{60}$ tetramers and $n=1$--$8$ supercells.

We address three questions on this \emph{class} of covalent molecular networks (qHP C$_{60}$ as the validation platform): (1)~how donor vs.\ acceptor dopants produce distinct mechanical strain responses and $\pi$-electron filling under the same PBE+D3 protocol; (2)~how large the omitted non-additive cross term $|\mathcal{S}|$ is under joint load; and (3)~whether $|\mathcal{S}|$ scales with dopant--host size mismatch or originates from $\pi$-electron nonlinearities.
We define $\mathcal{S}$ on periodic PBE+D3 grids at $+3$\% biaxial strain and compare matched-functional tetramer $\alpha$ and $\mathcal{S}_{+3\%}^{\mathrm{tet}}$ on an independent placement map (Table~\ref{tab:I}) to periodic $\mathcal{S}(n)$.
The largest periodic $|\mathcal{S}|$ at fixed coordinates (up to $31.9$~meV/atom for P at $n{=}1$) does not track tetramer $|\alpha|$ monotonically---\emph{decoupling} means that linear $\alpha$ cannot quantitatively predict cross-term magnitude or sign.
Local dopant distortion and global biaxial strain therefore interact nonlinearly in total energy, so relative stability margins from $\epsilon=0$ substitution energies or strain-only increments may not hold under coupled loading.

\section{\label{sec:methods}Computational Methods}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Electronic structure method}

All total-energy calculations use CP2K~\cite{cp2k2025} with PBE~\cite{Perdew1996generalized}, Grimme DFT-D3 (Becke--Johnson damping)~\cite{Grimme2011effect}, Goedecker--Teter--Hutter (GTH) pseudopotentials, and the DZVP-MOLOPT-SR-GTH atom-centered basis.
We adopt PBE+D3 rather than the Koopmans-compliant rVV10 protocol of Ref.~\citenum{Capobianco2024electron} to enable large-scale $(\epsilon,\delta)$ grids; PBE underestimates absolute gaps, but $\mathcal{S}$ is a same-functional total-energy difference so systematic gap errors largely cancel.
Plane-wave cutoffs are 400~Ry for $n\leq 4$ and 350~Ry for $n\geq 6$; a single-point 400~Ry control at $6\times\mathrm{C}_{60}$ N and $+3$\% differs by ${\sim}0.057$~meV/atom from the 350~Ry energy at fixed geometry.
Unless noted otherwise, the SCF threshold is $10^{-6}$~$E_{\mathrm{h}}$.

\subsection{Strain and doping models}
\label{sec:notation}
Biaxial strain is defined as $\epsilon=(a-a_0)/a_0\times 100\%$, where $a$ ($a_0$) is the strained (pristine equilibrium) in-plane lattice constant; dopant species $\delta\in\{\mathrm{B},\mathrm{N},\mathrm{P}\}$.
The synergy order parameter is the calligraphic symbol $\mathcal{S}$ [Eq.~\eqref{eq:synergy_order}], in meV per atom.
$\pi$-projected densities of states are written $\pi$-DOS.

Biaxial strain scales the in-plane lattice vectors of the pristine equilibrium cell at fixed fractional coordinates (rigid-strain protocol).
Reported $\alpha$ and $\mathcal{S}$ under this protocol are upper bounds on equilibrium magnitudes, because ionic relaxation can lower the energy through structural reconstruction (Sec.~\ref{sec:validation}).

Tetramer models ($4\times\mathrm{C}_{60}$) substitute B, N, or P at nominal ${\sim}5$~at.\% on a fixed pseudorandom carbon-site map; configurations are not thermodynamically optimized.
Periodic supercells place one substituent per C$_{60}$ (${\sim}1.67$~at.\%) on a fixed production site map shared across $n=1$--$8$.
Linear strain sensitivity $\alpha=\mathrm{d}E/\mathrm{d}\epsilon$ (meV per 1\% biaxial strain) is obtained from rigid PBE+D3 tetramer grids; positive $\alpha$ means tensile strain increases the total energy.
The substitution energy difference $\Delta E_{\mathrm{sub}}=(E_{\mathrm{doped}}-E_{\mathrm{pristine}})/n_{\mathrm{dopants}}$ is not a thermodynamic formation enthalpy (no elemental chemical potentials).

At $\epsilon{=}0$, B doping results in a nearly closed $\pi$ gap ($E_g\approx 0.03$~eV), N doping pins the Fermi level inside the conduction band ($E_g\approx -0.14$~eV), and P remains gapped ($E_g\approx 0.05$~eV); tensile strain narrows the pristine gap (${\sim}23$~meV/\% on the three available strain points in the dataset).

\subsection{\label{sec:strain_response}Mechanical strain sensitivity}

Pristine tetramers are strain-insensitive ($|\alpha|\approx 1$~meV/\% on the verified PBE+D3 grids).
Rigid PBE+D3 $\alpha$ on the reference placement map gives $+58.7$, $-297.5$, and $+456.0$~meV/\% for B, N, and P [Fig.~\ref{fig:main}(b)]; these share the dopant-site map with the periodic $\mathcal{S}$ audit.
On the independent alternate placement (Table~\ref{tab:I}), the same protocol gives $+21.4$, $+45.4$, and $+989.6$~meV/\%---$\alpha$ exhibits strong dopant-placement dependence while $\mathcal{S}_{+3\%}^{\mathrm{tet}}$ remains on the order of $10$~meV/atom for all three dopants.
The largest periodic $|\mathcal{S}|$ at $n{=}4$ and $+3$\% strain is $23.7$~meV/atom for P (Table~\ref{tab:IV}), while $|\alpha_P|$ on the alternate tetramer map is placement-sensitive---consistent with $\alpha$--$|\mathcal{S}|$ decoupling.
Ranking dopant stability solely by zero-strain substitution energies can be misleading under applied strain for close competitors.
\input{tables/tab_V.tex}
Table~\ref{tab:III} links mechanical spread to \emph{tetramer} local geometry (${\sim}5$\% nominal doping, multiple sites): P shows negligible strain-induced change in the mean nearest-neighbor C--C distance ($\Delta\bar{d}\approx 3\times 10^{-5}$~\AA\ vs.\ ${\sim}8.5\times 10^{-3}$~\AA\ for B at $+3$\% load), indicating a negligible uniform stretching of nearest-neighbor bonds rather than absence of cage buckling.
Complementary Hirshfeld charges on the periodic $n{=}1$ B/N/P strain paths probe charge redistribution along the same load path.

\subsection{\label{sec:synergy}Non-additive strain--doping coupling}

Figure~\ref{fig:main}(c) tracks protocol $\mathcal{S}(n)$ at $+3$\% biaxial strain.
At $n=4$ under the fixed periodic production map (Table~\ref{tab:IV}; two independent $n{=}4$ placement maps preserve P$>$B$>$N), $\mathcal{S}=-6.2$~meV/atom (B), $+3.0$~meV/atom (N), and $-23.7$~meV/atom (P); P shows the largest magnitude despite $\alpha$--$|\mathcal{S}|$ decoupling on the verified PBE+D3 tetramer map (Table~\ref{tab:I}).
On the alternate tetramer map (Table~\ref{tab:I}), P still carries the largest $\mathcal{S}_{+3\%}^{\mathrm{tet}}$ ($+41.4$~meV/atom) while $|\alpha|$ is placement-sensitive ($+989.6$~meV/\%); decoupling means linear $\alpha$ cannot predict cross-term magnitude or sign.
For $n\leq 4$ at matched 400~Ry cutoffs, $|\mathcal{S}|$ reaches $31.9$~meV/atom for P at $n=1$ and remains on the order of $10$~meV/atom for B and P at $n=2$--$4$ (Table~\ref{tab:IV}).
Within $n\leq 4$, $|\mathcal{S}|$ is not monotonic in $n$ (e.g., B: $8.4\rightarrow 2.2\rightarrow 6.2$~meV/atom at $n{=}1,2,4$), reflecting finite-cell dopant--dipole interactions and periodic image effects rather than an asymptotic size limit; we report protocol values at each supercell without extrapolating $\mathcal{S}_\infty$.
Rows with $n\geq 6$ (350~Ry) appear in Supplemental Material Table~S1.
Negative $\mathcal{S}$ (B, P at $n=4$) means coupled tension \emph{stabilizes} the doped cell relative to the additive estimate; positive $\mathcal{S}$ (N at $n=4$) means sequential screens \emph{overestimate} stability under load.

\subsubsection{Ionic-relaxation checkpoint}
\label{sec:relax_checkpoint}
Table~\ref{tab:II} benchmarks four fixed-cell geometry-optimization corners on the tetramer $P$-substitution grid (pristine and $P$ at $\epsilon{=}0$ and $+3$\%).
For the pristine tetramer, relaxed total energies at $\epsilon{=}0$ and $+3$\% are $-1363.9551$ and $-1363.9550$~$E_{\mathrm{h}}$ ($\Delta E\approx 1.2\times10^{-4}$~$E_{\mathrm{h}}$).
The $P$-doped corners give $E(P,\epsilon{=}0)=-1374.4415$ and $E(P,\epsilon{=}3\%)=-1374.4615$~$E_{\mathrm{h}}$.
Matched-functional PBE+D3 rigid single-points at relaxed coordinates yield $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{rigid}}^{\mathrm{PBE+D3}}=+1.19$~meV/atom versus $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{relaxed}}=-2.28$~meV/atom (sign not preserved; $|\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{relaxed}}|/|\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{rigid}}^{\mathrm{PBE+D3}}|\approx 1.9$; Table~\ref{tab:II}).
Matched-functional fixed-cell geometry optimization on the periodic $n{=}1$ P grid (PBE+D3; four corners) gives $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{rigid}}=-31.9$~meV/atom versus $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{relaxed}}\approx +0.1$~$\mu$eV/atom ($|\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{relaxed}}|/|\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{rigid}}|\lesssim 10^{-5}$), so ionic relaxation nearly eliminates the rigid periodic cross term in this cell.

\section{\label{sec:discussion}Discussion}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Literature context}
Relative to strain-only, doping-only, mobility, or mechanical studies in qHP and related fullerene networks~\cite{Capobianco2024electron,Li2024strain,Khan2025tuning,Wang2024simulation,LopezAlcalay2025graphendofullerene,Pereira2026endohedral,Qiu2025atomic,Peng2025monolayer,ShaikhPeng2025thermal,Shi2023strainC60,Makov2023graphullerene,Lv2026covalent,Materials2024untangling,Santra2024strain,Tromer2022dft}, the distinction here is a periodic \emph{total-energy} audit of joint biaxial $(\epsilon,\delta)$ load [Eq.~\eqref{eq:synergy_order}] (Table~\ref{tab:IV}).
Foundational DFT surveys of qHP C$_{60}$ monolayers emphasize anisotropic elastic moduli and optical gaps~\cite{Tromer2022dft} rather than matched four-corner total-energy grids under joint substitutional and mechanical load.
Strain-engineering roadmaps for generic two-dimensional materials~\cite{Katiyar2025strain} likewise foreground band-edge and mobility levers under separate mechanical or chemical protocols.
Graphendofullerene derivatives report Curie temperatures tunable through \emph{either} biaxial strain or electrostatic doping applied in isolation~\cite{LopezAlcalay2025graphendofullerene}, reinforcing that joint $(\epsilon,\delta)$ total-energy corners are not implicit in sequential protocol designs.
In other 2D hosts, substitutional defects can raise or lower formation energies under biaxial strain depending on dopant--host size mismatch~\cite{Santra2024strain}; spin-resolved impurity engineering on qHP graphullerene instead targets half-semiconductor gaps~\cite{Khan2025tuning}; we instead quantify the omitted additive cross term $\mathcal{S}$ in total energy.

\subsection{Origins of $\alpha$--$\mathcal{S}$ decoupling}
\label{sec:stress_coupling}
Substitutional B, N, or P can be viewed as embedding a \emph{localized internal strain field} $\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{int}}$ into an already distorted qHP cage baseline~\cite{Makov2023graphullerene}.
Table~\ref{tab:III} quantifies the chemical-mismatch scale by covalent-radius excess $\Delta r_{\mathrm{cov}}$ ($+7$, $-6$, and $+30$~pm for B, N, and P vs.\ C) and by mean dopant--C distance $\bar{d}$ at $\epsilon{=}0$.
External biaxial strain $\epsilon_{\mathrm{ext}}$ is a uniform macroscopic load on the periodic cell.
Sequential high-throughput models implicitly assume that responses to $\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{int}}$ and $\epsilon_{\mathrm{ext}}$ superpose linearly; $\mathcal{S}$ is the total-energy evidence that this superposition fails when both fields are present.
In the Eshelby picture, a small substitutional defect in a linear elastic continuum produces a strain field that superposes with external load; qHP C$_{60}$ falls outside this linear elastic regime because the cage is \emph{soft} and pre-strained~\cite{Makov2023graphullerene}, so second-order defect--elastic work can exceed the increment predicted by linear $\alpha\times\epsilon$ scaling alone.

N introduces excess $\pi$ electrons that strengthen bonding under tensile strain (negative $\alpha$), whereas B creates electron deficiency and antibonding $\pi^\ast$ character that rises with lattice expansion (positive $\alpha$).
Quantitatively, the $\epsilon{=}0$ gap ordering ($E_g^{\mathrm{N}}<E_g^{\mathrm{B}}<E_g^{\mathrm{P}}$ within PBE; Sec.~\ref{sec:electronic}) and the dopant-resolved $\pi$-DOS stack in Fig.~\ref{fig:pdos} (N states at $E_F$; B near-gap; P gapped) align with donor vs.\ acceptor $\pi$-electron counting, while total-energy $\alpha$ spans ${\sim}970$~meV/\% between B and P on the verified PBE+D3 alternate tetramer map (Table~\ref{tab:I}).
$\alpha$--$\mathcal{S}$ \emph{decoupling} does not assert anticorrelation between $|\alpha|$ and $|\mathcal{S}|$: on the alternate PBE+D3 map (Table~\ref{tab:I}), P has both the largest $|\alpha|$ and the largest $\mathcal{S}_{+3\%}^{\mathrm{tet}}$.
The claim is that linear strain slopes from a one-parameter scan cannot quantitatively predict four-corner cross-term magnitude or sign because $\alpha$ is placement-sensitive at ${\sim}5$~at.\% loading whereas $|\mathcal{S}|$ remains on the order of $10$~meV/atom on matched-functional tetramer and periodic audits.

The tetramer linear coefficient $\alpha$ and the periodic cross term $\mathcal{S}$ probe different energy partitions: $\alpha$ is the slope of total-cell energy under uniform lattice expansion at ${\sim}5$\% multi-site doping, whereas $\mathcal{S}$ is the omitted four-corner cross term per atom at ${\sim}1.67$~at.\% under periodic boundary conditions.
They therefore need not share sign even for the same element---a negative $\alpha$ (tension lowers total energy in a strain-only scan) can coexist with positive $\mathcal{S}$ (the coupled corner is less stable than an additive model predicts), as for N at $n{=}4$.
Any N sign flip between $n=6$ and $n=8$ lies outside the matched-cutoff $n\leq 4$ core grid and is excluded from design rules.

\subsection{Geometric vs.\ electronic coupling mechanisms}
\label{sec:mechanistic}
For \emph{size-mismatched} P, the dopant introduces compressive local misfit in the cage ($\Delta r_{\mathrm{cov}}{=}+30$~pm) while tensile $\epsilon_{\mathrm{ext}}$ partially relieves that misfit without proportionally stretching nearest-neighbor bonds ($\Delta\bar{d}\approx 3\times 10^{-5}$~\AA\ at $+3$\%; Table~\ref{tab:III}).
The resulting negative $\mathcal{S}$ (cooperative stabilization relative to the additive estimate) is consistent with nonlinear defect--elastic coupling: external strain couples to \emph{pre-existing cage buckling} rather than to uniform bond stretching.
P carries the largest covalent-radius excess and pre-existing cage distortion at $\epsilon{=}0$, yet shows the smallest strain-induced change in mean nearest C distance and the smallest site-to-site spread at $+3$\% ($\sigma(\bar{d})\approx 4\times 10^{-4}$~\AA\ vs.\ ${\sim}2\times 10^{-2}$~\AA\ for B/N; Table~\ref{tab:III}), yielding the largest $|\mathcal{S}|$ at $n{=}4$ ($23.7$~meV/atom; Table~\ref{tab:IV}).
For B and N, $|\Delta r_{\mathrm{cov}}|\lesssim 10$~pm and $\Delta\bar{d}$ tracks $\epsilon_{\mathrm{ext}}$ more uniformly (${\sim}8.5\times 10^{-3}$~\AA\ at $+3$\%), so $\mathcal{S}$ is set primarily by electronic nonlinearity---donor vs.\ acceptor $\pi$-electron counting and Fermi-level pinning---rather than by large-amplitude cage reshaping.
At $n{=}4$ the coupled correction for P exceeds the rigid per-atom strain increment from tetramer $\alpha$ by about two orders of magnitude ($|\mathcal{S}|\gg \alpha\times 3\%/N_{\mathrm{atoms}}$), even though $|\alpha|$ ranks P as the weakest strain responder on the tetramer grid.

For the matched-functional tetramer $P$ rigid grid, Grimme D3 dispersion energies at the four corners give $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{vdW}}\approx +0.33$~meV/atom---orders of magnitude below $|\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{rigid}}^{\mathrm{PBE+D3}}|\approx 1.2$~meV/atom (Table~\ref{tab:II})---so vdW non-additivity does not drive the reported cross terms.
On the periodic $n{=}1$ P grid, Hirshfeld charge on P decreases from $+0.063\,e$ at $\epsilon{=}0$ to $+0.056\,e$ at $+3$\% while tetramer $\Delta\bar{d}$ for P remains below $10^{-4}$~\AA\ at $+3$\% load (Table~\ref{tab:III}).
B and N Hirshfeld charges shift from $+0.119\,e$ and $+0.069\,e$ at $\epsilon{=}0$ to $+0.111\,e$ and $+0.057\,e$ at $+3$\% (${\sim}7$\% and ${\sim}18$\% fractional $\Delta q/q$, vs.\ ${\sim}11$\% for P).
Thus N shows the largest fractional charge response yet the smallest $|\mathcal{S}|$ at $n{=}4$, whereas P combines a moderate $\Delta q$ with vanishing $\Delta\bar{d}$ and the largest cross term---charge redistribution alone does not rank $|\mathcal{S}|$; geometric pre-strain remains necessary for the P channel.

Together, these observations suggest a taxonomy for covalent molecular networks: \emph{electronic} donor/acceptor control of $\alpha$; \emph{structural} local-distortion coupling to external strain (P); and \emph{finite-size} electrostatic dilution (N at large $n$).

\subsection{General design rules for covalent networks}
\label{sec:generality}
Using $|\Delta r_{\mathrm{cov}}|$ from Table~\ref{tab:III} as a single mismatch coordinate, periodic $|\mathcal{S}|$ spans $2.3$--$8.4$~meV/atom for B and N ($|\Delta r_{\mathrm{cov}}|\lesssim 10$~pm) but reaches $31.9$~meV/atom for P ($|\Delta r_{\mathrm{cov}}|{=}30$~pm; Table~\ref{tab:IV}).
N breaks a naive ``$|\mathcal{S}|\propto(\Delta r)^2$'' law at comparable mismatch to B because its cross term is electronic (positive $\mathcal{S}$ at $n{=}4$) rather than geometric.
The data support a \emph{two-regime} design rule: $|\Delta r_{\mathrm{cov}}|\gtrsim 20$~pm flags the geometric-nonlinear regime where coupled audits are mandatory (P); $|\Delta r_{\mathrm{cov}}|\lesssim 15$~pm leaves $|\mathcal{S}|$ at few-meV/atom under $+3$\% load but still permits sign conflicts between $\alpha$ and $\mathcal{S}$ (B, N).
These thresholds are calibrated on qHP C$_{60}$; rigid graphene-like hosts may require higher mismatch before geometric nonlinearity dominates.

At $|\Delta r_{\mathrm{cov}}|\lesssim 10$~pm and $+3$\% biaxial load, $|\mathcal{S}|\lesssim 10$~meV/atom on the $n{=}1$ audit grid; sequential pipelines may be adequate for coarse ranking but not for close $E_{\mathrm{sub}}$ competitors.
Pristine tetramers show $|\alpha|\approx 1$~meV/\%, so sub-1\% loads keep linear strain increments small; at $+3$\% and above, coupled corners for size-mismatched dopants are advisable.
qHP C$_{60}$ combines interfullerene covalent links with soft intramolecular cages and intrinsic intramolecular strain~\cite{Makov2023graphullerene}, amplifying local-distortion coupling relative to rigid 2D lattices; extrapolation to graphene-like hosts requires separate benchmarks.
Tetramer $\alpha$ and $\mathcal{S}_{+3\%}^{\mathrm{tet}}$ are not interchangeable with periodic $\mathcal{S}(n)$ because concentration (${\sim}5$\% vs.\ ${\sim}1.67$~at.\%) and boundary conditions differ; non-additivity is assessed only through Eq.~\eqref{eq:synergy_order} on matched four-corner grids.

At $n{=}4$ and $+3$\% strain the coupled correction is $|\mathcal{S}|=23.7$~meV/atom for P vs.\ $6.2$~meV/atom for B---sequential additive estimates differ by approximately a factor of four in the load-dependent margin under the same PBE+D3 periodic protocol.
Where $\mathcal{S}<0$ (B and P at $n{=}4$), coupled tension can stabilize doped cells beyond additive predictions; where $\mathcal{S}>0$ (N at $n{=}4$), sequential screens overestimate stability under load.
For B at $n{=}4$, negative $\mathcal{S}$ coexists with positive $\alpha$; for N at $n{=}4$, negative tetramer $\alpha$ and positive $\mathcal{S}$ present a second sign conflict.
Gap closing [Fig.~\ref{fig:main}(a)] does not track $|\mathcal{S}|$, so photonic or transport targets cannot be inferred from total-energy $\mathcal{S}$ alone.

\subsection{Experimental signatures}
\label{sec:exp_signatures}
Three classes of laboratory observables could test the stress-field picture without resolving total energies directly.
\textbf{Raman or IR cage modes:} under joint doping and strain, mode shifts should deviate from the sum of doping-only and strain-only shifts when $|\mathcal{S}|$ is large---especially for P, where tensile load couples to pre-existing cage distortion rather than uniform bond stretching (Table~\ref{tab:III}).
\textbf{Scanning tunneling spectroscopy:} dopant-site local density of states should evolve nonlinearly with strain when Hirshfeld charge redistributes without proportional $\Delta\bar{d}$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:mechanistic}); N pinning near $E_F$ vs.\ gapped B/P provides contrasting signatures.
\textbf{Thermal or chemical stability:} for $\mathcal{S}<0$ (B, P at $n{=}4$), tension should enhance apparent doping stability relative to additive thermochemical estimates; for $\mathcal{S}>0$ (N at $n{=}4$), the opposite bias is expected.
Quantitative links require matched sample chemistry and strain calibration; we quote DFT protocol bounds only.

\subsection{Limitations and outlook}
Main-text $\alpha$ and $\mathcal{S}$ use PBE+D3 under the rigid-strain protocol (Sec.~\ref{sec:notation}); ionic relaxation can reverse or nearly extinguish $\mathcal{S}$ (Table~\ref{tab:II}; periodic $n{=}1$ P).
Periodic $\mathcal{S}(n)$ magnitudes are configuration-specific on a fixed production site map (two independent $n{=}4$ maps preserve P$>$B$>$N; Table~\ref{tab:IV}).
Core claims use matched 400~Ry for $n\leq 4$; $n\geq 6$ production points use 350~Ry.
Table~\ref{tab:III} geometry is a tetramer proxy; the geometric vs.\ electronic channel assignment rests on $\Delta\bar{d}$, $\Delta r_{\mathrm{cov}}$, and Hirshfeld $\Delta q$ rather than a full energy decomposition.
Because $\mathcal{S}$ is a same-functional difference, absolute errors in substitution energy do not affect our conclusion of non-additive coupling.
Future work includes Mayer/Bader strain-path partitioning and placement averaging on periodic cells.

\section{\label{sec:conclusions}Conclusions}

We quantify how substitutional chemistry and biaxial strain couple nonlinearly in covalent molecular networks: the omitted total-energy cross term $\mathcal{S}$ arises when a dopant-induced internal stress field meets an external load, not from a graphullerene-specific numerical artifact.
On qHP C$_{60}$ as the validation platform, donor and acceptor dopants produce distinct $\pi$-electron filling and gap response, with placement-sensitive PBE+D3 $\alpha$ on tetramer maps (Table~\ref{tab:I}), while omitted cross terms reach the meV/atom scale on the periodic grid ($|\mathcal{S}|$ up to $31.9$~meV/atom for P at $n{=}1$ at fixed coordinates; Table~\ref{tab:IV}).
For P, $|\mathcal{S}|$ tracks local cage distortion and mismatch (Table~\ref{tab:III}) rather than tetramer $|\alpha|$.
Two mechanisms emerge---electronic donor/acceptor $\pi$ control (B, N) and geometric local-distortion coupling for size-mismatched P---with a practical mismatch threshold ($|\Delta r_{\mathrm{cov}}|\gtrsim 20$~pm) separating few-meV from tens-of-meV $|\mathcal{S}|$ under $+3$\% load.
Coupled $(\epsilon,\delta)$ screening is advisable for size-mismatched substituents, $+3$\% and larger loads, and close substitution-energy competitors; Raman, STS, and stability trends in Sec.~\ref{sec:exp_signatures} offer experimental tests of the stress-field picture.

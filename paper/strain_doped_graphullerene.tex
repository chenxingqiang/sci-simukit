\begin{abstract}
Under mechanically constrained loading on quasi-hexagonal (qHP) C$_{60}$ graphullerene, measurable deviations from additive strain--dopant energetics arise because frozen elastic incompatibility introduces a bilinear strain--composition interaction that can reach magnitudes comparable to high-throughput screening margins---not because a finite-difference estimator is merely nonzero.
The additive approximation in sequential workflows omits the discrete strain--substitution interaction for each fixed substituent.
Matched four-corner audits on the 400~Ry core ($n{=}1$, $2$, and $4$; PBE+D3, affine biaxial strain with locked dopant sites) show that this term can alter energy differences at the scale relevant to energetic screening for size-mismatched phosphorus.
The representative core value is $|\mathcal{S}|=23.7\pm 2$~meV/atom at $n{=}4$ ($\eta\approx 8\%$, about $12$ times the $2$~meV/atom reporting floor).
Across the audited sizes, P spans $23.7$--$31.9$~meV/atom; the $n{=}1$ value ($31.9\pm 2$~meV/atom) is the strongest constrained coupling and also the weakest $\Gamma$-only sampling support.
Supercells with $n\geq 6$ (350~Ry) are reference-only and are not used for that screening comparison.
Cluster tetramer ``strain'' grids do not affine-scale the molecular coordinates and are not used as strain-physics evidence.
The four-corner difference $\mathcal{S}$ estimates that coupling on the audited grid and is not a new thermodynamic observable.
It is not a charged-defect or polaron-rate estimator.
The $n{=}2$ $\mathcal{S}(n)$ row is reference-limited at the undoped corner, not a sign change of the discrete species interaction.
On the 400~Ry core, only the size-mismatched substituent is numerically resolved well above the protocol floor: $|\mathcal{S}|$ is $12$--$16$ times the $2$~meV/atom reporting band for P ($31.9$~meV/atom at $n{=}1$; $23.7$~meV/atom at $n{=}4$), while N at $n{=}1$ ($+2.30$~meV/atom) sits at that floor and is not assigned separate physical significance. The pattern is consistent with covalent-radius mismatch on this B/N/P map; it is not a unique causal assignment.
Ionic relaxation of periodic $n{=}1$ P suppresses the constrained cross term to the reporting floor ($|\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{relaxed}}|<10^{-3}$~meV/atom). On a separate tetramer boundary condition the same relaxation reverses the sign and increases $|\mathcal{S}|$ ($+1.19$ to $-2.28$~meV/atom). Rigid $|\mathcal{S}|$ is an upper-end protocol estimate relative to the fully relaxed fixed-cell protocol studied here, not a universal bound on every constraint manifold.
The audit signals when sequential additive workflows should be replaced by explicit coupled evaluations on the $E(\epsilon,\delta)$ landscape.
\end{abstract}

\section{\label{sec:intro}Introduction}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

Substitutional doping and mechanical strain are widely used to tune stability and electronic structure in covalent molecular networks~\cite{Katiyar2025strain,Lv2026covalent}.
Sequential screens often add separate strain-only and substitution-only increments, omitting the discrete strain--substitution interaction for each fixed dopant species~\cite{Lee2023localised,Li2024strain,Katiyar2025strain}.
Cluster-expansion Hamiltonians already include composition--strain cross terms through constituent-strain and configurational interactions~\cite{Sanchez1984generalized,Laks1992efficient}; what has been missing for soft fullerene networks is a matched four-corner total-energy audit under simultaneous load.
Expanding the energy per atom $e(\epsilon,x)=E(\epsilon,\delta)/N_{\mathrm{atoms}}$ about the pristine reference gives
\begin{equation}
e(\epsilon,x) \approx e_{0} + a\,\epsilon + b\,x + c\,\epsilon\,x + \mathcal{O}(\epsilon^2,x^2,\epsilon^2 x,\epsilon x^2),
\label{eq:taylor_coupling}
\end{equation}
where $c=\partial^2 e/\partial\epsilon\,\partial x$ is the lowest-order bilinear coupling.
Here $x$ is a local continuous composition coordinate introduced at one fixed species; B, N, and P are not three points on one shared $x$ axis.
The four-corner estimator $\mathcal{S}_\delta(\epsilon)$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:methods_coupling}) is the discrete strain--substitution interaction at $+3$\% biaxial load.
Only after that local coordinate is introduced does $\mathcal{S}_\delta(\epsilon)$ sample $\partial^2 e/\partial\epsilon\,\partial x$.
It is a diagnostic, not a new thermodynamic observable (Supplemental Material, Sec.~\ref{S-sec:appendix_fd})~\cite{SupplementalMaterial}. It is not a charged-defect or polaron-rate estimator.

Finite-size $\mathcal{S}(n)$ entries that fail the undoped-corner SCF gate are reference-limited and must not be read as a dopant-rank trend.

We study quasi-hexagonal (qHP) C$_{60}$ graphullerene~\cite{Hou2022synthesis,Capobianco2024electron,Makov2023graphullerene} as an elastically soft, experimentally accessible host.
Stress-related anisotropy of qHP versus qTP C$_{60}$ has been mapped for optoelectronic device implications~\cite{Wang2024simulation}; that comparison is a uniaxial-stress geometry map, not a four-corner $\mathcal{S}$ on a substitutional B/N/P grid.
Substitutional B, N, and P form a donor, acceptor, and size-mismatch triad spanning both signs of covalent-radius mismatch relative to C ($\Delta r_{\mathrm{cov}}=+7$, $-6$, and $+30$~pm; Table~\ref{tab:geometry}).
Prior DFT work maps strain and doping on separate grids or reports nonlinear electronic response without matched four-corner \emph{total-energy} audits~\cite{Li2024strain,Khan2025tuning,Peng2025monolayer,Capobianco2024electron,Tromer2022dft,Lee2023localised,Silva2024large,Santra2024strain}; a positioning table is in Supplemental Material, Table~\ref{S-tab:lit_positioning}.
A related strain-only map of polymerized C$_{24}$ graphullerene~\cite{Li2024graphullerene} is chemically distinct from the qHP C$_{60}$ lattice used here and likewise omits that four-corner $\mathcal{S}$ audit.
Atomic-scale mechanical and tribological maps of graphullerene~\cite{Qiu2025atomic} likewise do not evaluate a four-corner total-energy $\mathcal{S}$.
A substitutional defect on a pre-strained cage, together with global biaxial load, can store elastic incompatibility that linear $\alpha$ does not capture.
Here we test whether additive totals fail under joint $(\epsilon,\delta)$ load, whether the omitted term matters under mechanically constrained configurations, and whether failure tracks covalent-radius mismatch rather than $\pi$-electron descriptors alone.
The protocol is reported for one host; transferring it requires repeating the same $(\epsilon,\delta,n)$ grid.

\section{\label{sec:methods}Computational Methods}

\subsection{Electronic structure method}

All total-energy calculations use CP2K~\cite{VandeVondele2005quickstep} with Goedecker--Teter--Hutter (GTH) pseudopotentials, the DZVP-MOLOPT-SR-GTH atom-centered basis, and the Gaussian and plane waves (GPW) scheme.

\paragraph{Exchange-correlation and dispersion.}
We use PBE~\cite{Perdew1996generalized} with Grimme DFT-D3 (Becke--Johnson damping)~\cite{Grimme2011effect} for the production dataset.
Hybrids or meta-GGAs (SCAN, r$^2$SCAN) can shift absolute totals by tens of meV per atom in carbon networks; production rankings are therefore framed as \emph{within-PBE+D3} comparisons of $\mathcal{S}$ among B, N, and P under matched load.
The four-corner construction [Sec.~\ref{sec:methods_coupling}] subtracts totals computed with identical XC, basis, dispersion, and numerical settings, so systematic shifts largely cancel when \emph{ranking} dopants at fixed protocol---but cancellation is not assumed without audit: on the isolated-cage tetramer $P$ grid (not a strained covalent network), Grimme-D3 non-additivity is $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{vdW}}\approx +0.33$~meV/atom (Table~\ref{tab:sigma_S})---a cluster-protocol bound, not a strain-physics decomposition of periodic $\mathcal{S}$.
No meta-GGA $\mathcal{S}$ values are reported (Table~\ref{tab:sigma_S}; Limitations).

\paragraph{Plane-wave mesh and Brillouin-zone sampling.}
Plane-wave cutoffs are 400~Ry for $n\leq 4$ and 350~Ry for $n\geq 6$ (REL\_CUTOFF 50~Ry; QS EPS\_DEFAULT $10^{-10}$~$E_{\mathrm{h}}$).
Comparisons at fixed $n$ always use the same cutoff.
Production supercells contain 60--480 atoms with in-plane lattice constants up to ${\sim}44$~\AA\ at $n{=}8$; inputs use $\Gamma$-only sampling.
Because $\mathcal{S}$ is a second-order difference on the \emph{same} supercell, uniform $k$-point or cutoff shifts that co-drifts all four corners largely cancel in the four-corner estimator; sensitivity must be audited on $\mathcal{S}(\mathrm{cutoff})$ and $\mathcal{S}(k)$, not on bare totals alone.
A partial cutoff audit at $6\times\mathrm{C}_{60}$ N and $+3$\% shifts one corner by only ${\sim}0.057$~meV/atom between 350 and 400~Ry, implying $|\Delta\mathcal{S}|<0.2$~meV/atom when only the mesh drifts (Table~\ref{tab:sigma_S}).
Dense-$k$ ($2\times 2\times 1$) four-corner benchmarks on $n{=}1$ B/N/P at $+3$\% are not included in the production set (Table~\ref{tab:sigma_S}; Limitations); the smallest reported $|\mathcal{S}|$ values ($2.3$--$3.0$~meV/atom for N and B at $n{=}1$) are therefore interpreted with explicit $\Gamma$-only caution.

\paragraph{SCF convergence and four-corner precision.}
Unless noted otherwise, the SCF threshold is $\mathrm{EPS\_SCF}=10^{-6}$~$E_{\mathrm{h}}$ in inner and outer orbital-transformation loops (MAX\_SCF 300; OUTER\_SCF MAX 30).
The $2\times\mathrm{C}_{60}$ cells are the exception: they cycled at $10^{-6}$ and were converged at $10^{-5}$~$E_{\mathrm{h}}$, which is why their pristine reference lies off the size-converged plateau and why we flag that row as reference-limited (Table~\ref{tab:sigma_S}; Sec.~\ref{sec:synergy}).
Because the four-corner stencil of Eq.~\eqref{eq:four_corner} includes that pristine total, the $n{=}2$ row is reference-limited at the undoped corner rather than by a sign change of the discrete species interaction.
The $n{=}8$ row is likewise reference-limited at the undoped corner: the undoped $+3$\% strain cost sits well above the $n{=}1/4/6$ plateau even though that cell uses the same $350$~Ry production cutoff as $n{=}6$, so the offset is not a cutoff-class change and is not reported as a size-trend sign change.
Charged-state workflows in the Supplemental Material tighten this to $10^{-7}$~$E_{\mathrm{h}}$; selected stiff tetramer corners may use $10^{-5}$~$E_{\mathrm{h}}$ when outer SCF cycling persists at $10^{-6}$, which stays inside the ${\sim}2$~meV/atom reporting floor of Table~\ref{tab:sigma_S}.
Because $\mathcal{S}$ is built from four independently converged totals [Eq.~\eqref{eq:synergy_order}], uncertainty is propagated as $\sigma_{\mathcal{S}}=2\sigma_e$ for uncorrelated per-atom corner errors $\sigma_e$ [Eq.~\eqref{eq:sigma_propagation}; Supplemental Material, Sec.~\ref{S-sec:error_prop}] (Table~\ref{tab:sigma_S}).
We use $\sigma_{\mathcal{S}}\lesssim 2$~meV/atom as the conservative reporting band on the $n\leq 4$ production grid.

\paragraph{Spin, charge, and mechanical boundary conditions.}
Substitutional periodic cells are charge-neutral closed-shell Kohn--Sham calculations (no spin polarization in the production $\mathcal{S}$ grid); Hirshfeld strain-path audits on $n{=}1$ B/N/P use spin-polarized KS only where odd electron counts require it (Sec.~\ref{sec:methods_population}).
We do not apply compensating background charge or charged supercells in the $\mathcal{S}$ audit: doping is modeled as neutral substitution.

\subsection{Strain and doping models}
\label{sec:notation}
Biaxial strain is $\epsilon=(a-a_0)/a_0\times 100\%$, with $a$ ($a_0$) the strained (equilibrium) in-plane lattice constant; $\delta\in\{\mathrm{B},\mathrm{N},\mathrm{P}\}$ labels the substituent and is not a continuous coordinate shared by B, N, and P.
$\mathcal{S}$ is defined in Sec.~\ref{sec:methods_coupling}; $\pi$-projected densities of states are written $\pi$-DOS.
Periodic $n\times\mathrm{C}_{60}$ cells place one substituent per cage (${\sim}1.67$~at.\%) and affine-scale both lattice vectors and Cartesian coordinates; that protocol supplies every $\mathcal{S}(n)$ value in this paper.
Isolated-cage tetramer clusters ($4\times\mathrm{C}_{60}$, ${\sim}5$~at.\% substitution) are not a covalently networked monolayer: labeled tetramer ``strain'' grids change only the vacuum box and do not affine-scale the molecular coordinates, so they are not used as strain-physics evidence.
The substitution energy $\Delta E_{\mathrm{sub}}=(E_{\mathrm{doped}}-E_{\mathrm{pristine}})/n_{\mathrm{dopants}}$ is not a formation enthalpy (no elemental chemical potentials).

\paragraph{Rigid vs.\ relaxed protocols.}
\emph{Rigid strain} on periodic cells scales lattice vectors at fixed fractional coordinates and is the production protocol for $\mathcal{S}(n)$.
It is a constrained fixed-coordinate diagnostic for loads in which ions cannot relax (epitaxial clamp, strong adhesion, or ultrafast load): an upper-end protocol estimate relative to the fully relaxed fixed-cell protocol studied here, not a universal bound on every constraint manifold and not an equilibrium free-energy difference.
\emph{Relaxed strain} uses fixed-cell BFGS at each bilinear corner on the periodic $n{=}1$ P cell before evaluating $\mathcal{S}$ [Fig.~\ref{fig:synergy}(b)].
Force thresholds are $4.5\times10^{-4}$ and $3.0\times10^{-4}$~Ha/bohr (${\sim}0.023$ and ${\sim}0.015$~eV/\AA) at $\epsilon{=}0$ and $+3$\%, tighter than typical 0.02~eV/\AA\ defaults so that near-zero relaxed $|\mathcal{S}|$ is resolved.
On that periodic cell, relaxation suppresses the constrained cross term ($|\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{relaxed}}|<10^{-3}$~meV/atom).
Table~\ref{tab:relax} is a different boundary condition: matched-functional tetramer $P$ changes from $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{rigid}}^{\mathrm{PBE+D3}}=+1.19$~meV/atom to $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{relaxed}}=-2.28$~meV/atom, so the sign is not preserved and $|\mathcal{S}|$ increases.
That tetramer grid does not affine-scale the molecules and is not a retention ratio for the periodic scan.
Table~\ref{tab:placement} records a tetramer seed diagnostic that is likewise not strain physics.
The physically interpreted ionic-relaxation and placement tests are the periodic $n{=}1$ P four-corner set and the periodic $n{=}4$ three-seed set (seeds 42, 137, and 271).

\subsection{\label{sec:strain_response}Mechanical strain sensitivity}

Pristine tetramers yield $\alpha_{\mathrm{pris}}=+1.2\pm 0.1$~meV/\% on the six-point reference grid.

\section{\label{sec:discussion}Discussion}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Main finding}
\label{sec:discussion_main}

Additive screening omits the four-corner cross term $\mathcal{S}$ under constrained joint load.
On the matched 400~Ry core the representative value is $n{=}4$ ($|\mathcal{S}|=23.7\pm 2$~meV/atom). That cross term is large enough to invalidate an additive ranking for P when candidate separations are comparable to $|\mathcal{S}|$ (Table~\ref{tab:sgrid}), while linear $\alpha$ does not rank $|\mathcal{S}|$ [Fig.~\ref{fig:decoupling}(d)].
The three-dopant pattern is selective and illustrative: only P is resolved well above both the protocol floor (${\sim}2$~meV/atom) and the $\eta>{\sim}1$\% flag, B clears the floor but not the $\eta$ flag, and N at $n{=}1$ ($+2.30$~meV/atom) is resolution-limited (Sec.~\ref{sec:synergy}).
That selectivity is consistent with a larger covalent-radius mismatch for P; the present data do not identify a unique cause.
Ionic relaxation of periodic $n{=}1$ P suppresses the constrained cross term ($|\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{relaxed}}|<10^{-3}$~meV/atom).
On the tetramer boundary condition the same relaxation reverses the sign and increases $|\mathcal{S}|$ ($+1.19$ to $-2.28$~meV/atom; Table~\ref{tab:relax}; Fig.~\ref{fig:synergy}(b)).
The pattern is consistent with a geometry-mediated contribution; the data do not partition elastic and electronic energy (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion_limitations}).
We treat $\mathcal{S}$ as numerically resolved only when $|\mathcal{S}|$ exceeds the protocol floor by a clear margin.
The $\eta>{\sim}1$\% flag is a practical marker for coupled re-evaluation on this PBE+D3 map, not a 95\% confidence interval and not a thermodynamic boundary.

\subsection{Mechanism}
\label{sec:mechanistic}

For size-mismatched P, the correlated pattern is (Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism}): $\Delta r_{\mathrm{cov}}{=}+30$~pm together with the largest $|\mathcal{S}|$.
This is a consistency check on the present energetic and structural audits, not a partitioned causal chain.
Two features of the audited set are consistent with a geometry-mediated contribution, not a unique control (Table~\ref{tab:geometry}; Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism}(b,d)).
First, the \emph{sign} tracks the sign of the mismatch: N is the only undersized substituent ($\Delta r_{\mathrm{cov}}{=}-6$~pm) and the only one with $\mathcal{S}>0$, while the oversized B and P ($+7$ and $+30$~pm) both give $\mathcal{S}<0$.
Second, the \emph{rank} of the magnitude follows $|\Delta r_{\mathrm{cov}}|$ at both audited sizes: N $<$ B $<$ P for $|\Delta r_{\mathrm{cov}}|$ ($6<7<30$~pm) and for $|\mathcal{S}|$ at $n{=}1$ ($2.3<8.4<31.9$~meV/atom) and $n{=}4$ ($3.0<6.2<23.7$~meV/atom).
Radius nonetheless fails to fix the proportionality constant: $|\mathcal{S}|_{n=1}/|\Delta r_{\mathrm{cov}}|$ is ${\sim}0.4$~meV/atom per pm for N against ${\sim}1.1$--$1.2$ for B and P, so a single geometric descriptor orders the three substituents without predicting their magnitudes.
With three substitutional labels this is a consistency check on the misfit picture, not a fitted scaling law.
Electronic redistribution does not reproduce either pattern: the fractional Hirshfeld change $|\Delta q/q|$ over $0\to{+}3$\% is largest for N (${\sim}18$\%), intermediate for P (${\sim}10$\%), and smallest for B (${\sim}6$\%), which neither orders nor signs $\mathcal{S}$ [Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism}(a)].
$\pi$-DOS morphology likewise does not order $|\mathcal{S}|$: the substituted cages are indistinguishable in $E_{\mathrm{H-L}}$ at $\epsilon{=}0$ yet $|\mathcal{S}|$ at $n{=}1$ runs from $2.3$ to $31.9$~meV/atom [Figs.~\ref{fig:electronic}(c) and~\ref{fig:decoupling}(d)].
Uniform bond stretch does not track $|\mathcal{S}|$: B shows the largest $\Delta\bar{d}$ at $+3$\% ($+8.5\times 10^{-3}$~\AA) yet an intermediate $|\mathcal{S}|$, whereas P barely stretches ($+3\times 10^{-5}$~\AA) and couples most strongly.
Ionic relaxation of periodic $n{=}1$ P suppresses the constrained cross term; on the tetramer the same relaxation reverses the sign and increases $|\mathcal{S}|$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:synergy}(b)).
Grimme D3 contributes $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{vdW}}\approx +0.33$~meV/atom at tetramer $P$, about $28$\% of the small tetramer $|\mathcal{S}|\approx 1.2$~meV/atom (Table~\ref{tab:sigma_S}).
That decomposition does not indicate that dispersion dominates the larger periodic P signals ($23.7$ and $31.9$~meV/atom).

\subsection{Relation to literature}
\label{sec:discussion_literature}

This is a periodic total-energy audit of joint $(\epsilon,\delta)$ load (Table~\ref{tab:sgrid}), not a strain-only, doping-only, or mobility/gap map~\cite{Capobianco2024electron,Li2024strain,Khan2025tuning,Peng2025monolayer,Lee2023localised}.
Strain-only electronic and optical maps of C$_{24}$ graphullerene~\cite{Li2024graphullerene} likewise omit the matched four-corner $\mathcal{S}$ audit used here for qHP C$_{60}$.
Cluster expansions already encode $c\,\epsilon x$~\cite{Sanchez1984generalized,Laks1992efficient}; the increment is the matched four-corner audit on a soft, pre-strained network with rigid-vs.-relaxed and placement checks (Tables~\ref{tab:relax}--\ref{tab:sgrid}; Supplemental Material, Table~\ref{S-tab:lit_positioning}).
Prior graphullerene studies often relaxed ions first or added separate grids~\cite{Capobianco2024electron,Li2024strain,Khan2025tuning}, which masks constrained-load coupling.
Quasi-harmonic thermal-expansion maps of related fullerene networks~\cite{ShaikhPeng2025thermal} likewise omit a matched four-corner $\mathcal{S}$ audit under a frozen biaxial cell.
Strain-induced band-alignment and mobility maps of 1D/2D C$_{60}$ nanostructures~\cite{Shi2023strainC60} likewise omit that matched four-corner $\mathcal{S}$ audit.
Endohedral doping of the same qHP cages~\cite{Pereira2026endohedral} maps electronic and optical responses of encapsulated guests, not the substitutional B/N/P plus frozen-biaxial four-corner $\mathcal{S}$ protocol used here.
Two-dimensional C$_{70}$ networks have been proposed as synthesizable platforms for electronic band engineering~\cite{Xu2025C70network}; that design axis is cage-allotrope and band-edge selection, not the substitutional B/N/P plus frozen-biaxial four-corner $\mathcal{S}$ audit used here for qHP C$_{60}$.
Strain-induced magnetism and half-metallicity in C$_{20}$ graphullerene monolayers~\cite{Nie2026strainC20} likewise address a magnetic/half-metallic response of a different cage allotrope, not the substitutional B/N/P plus frozen-biaxial four-corner $\mathcal{S}$ audit used here for qHP C$_{60}$.
The four-corner $\mathcal{S}$ is a total-energy cross term on the \emph{neutral} Born--Oppenheimer surface; charged versus neutral DFT formulations that are equivalent for polaron self-interaction corrections~\cite{Falletta2025polaronDFT} do not convert that audit into a Marcus rate or a charged-defect formation energy.

\subsection{Applicability and generality}
\label{sec:applicability}

The four-corner audit applies when local substitutional strain cannot fully relax on a short-range, elastically soft covalent network~\cite{Makov2023graphullerene,Alihosseini2023strain}.
It does not apply to fully relaxed bulk references, metallic or liquid hosts, or high-$T$ regimes where configurational entropy dominates sub-meV differences (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion_limitations}).
Uniaxial load should preserve the stencil but rescale $c$ through elastic anisotropy; that case is not audited.

Laboratory tests need not resolve $\mathcal{S}$: cage-mode Raman/IR or substrate-bending that deviate from separate strain-plus-doping calibrations would signal additive failure, provided chemistry and strain are matched and the comparison is to a constrained, not fully relaxed, state.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion_limitations}

Main-text $\alpha$ and $\mathcal{S}$ use PBE+D3. Periodic production cells are affinely strained at fixed fractional coordinates; cluster tetramer grids change only the vacuum box and are not used as strain physics. Rigid $|\mathcal{S}|$ is an upper-end protocol estimate relative to the fully relaxed fixed-cell protocol studied here, not a universal bound on every constraint manifold. Ionic relaxation of periodic $n{=}1$ $P$ suppresses the constrained cross term ($|\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{relaxed}}|<10^{-3}$~meV/atom). Table~\ref{tab:relax} is a different boundary condition: the tetramer $P$ cross term reverses sign and increases in magnitude ($+1.19$ to $-2.28$~meV/atom) and is not a retention ratio for the periodic scan.
Eq.~\eqref{eq:taylor_coupling} and the four-corner link [Eq.~\eqref{eq:mixed_derivative}] assume small perturbations on a single smooth energy branch; they lose validity at large strain (e.g.\ ${\gtrsim}10$\%) or across structural phase transitions, and the production audits intentionally remain at $+3$\% biaxial load inside the nominally elastic regime. Finer meshes at $\pm 1$, $\pm 2$, and $\pm 4$\%, including $\mathcal{S}_{P}(1\%,2\%,3\%)$ on periodic $n{=}1$, remain outside the production audit.
All totals are 0~K Born--Oppenheimer energies; finite-temperature free energies and configurational entropy remain unexplored and could reorder sub-meV competitors without restoring additivity if bilinear coupling persists at the electronic-structure level.
Production periodic cells use $\Gamma$-only sampling; dense-$k$ and SCAN/r$^2$SCAN four-corner checks are not reported (Table~\ref{tab:sigma_S}).
On the tested production grid, $n{=}1$ is the strongest constrained periodic coupling observed; it is not an intrinsic dilute-limit constant. That cell is also the one for which $\Gamma$-only sampling is least adequate, so the $n{=}1$ magnitude carries the weakest $k$-sampling support in the set. The present data do not establish size independence of $\mathcal{S}$.
Two of the five supercell sizes ($n{=}2$, $8$) have pristine references off the converged plateau by $4$--$5$~meV/atom, so their $\mathcal{S}$ rows are reported but not used for size-trend claims; recomputing those four corners at $\mathrm{EPS\_SCF}=10^{-6}$ and matched cutoff is the cheapest remaining improvement to the audit.
The $n{=}8$ offset is not a cutoff-class change: that supercell shares the $n{=}6$ production cutoff (Methods). Cells with $n{\geq}6$ use 350~Ry and remain Supplemental reference rows; they are not used for mechanistic or asymptotic inference.
Alternate placements (Table~\ref{tab:placement}) bound configuration specificity; Table~\ref{tab:geometry} geometry is a tetramer proxy (${\sim}5$~at.\%) without Mayer/Bader partitioning or matched periodic $n{=}4$ bond statistics.
With only three substitutional labels, the $\alpha$--$|\mathcal{S}|$ map [Fig.~\ref{fig:decoupling}(d)] is a qualitative rank comparison among $n{=}3$ chemistries, not a fitted correlation law or predictive model for linear $\alpha$.
Direct elastic-vs.-electronic energetic decomposition (Mayer bond order, Bader strain partitioning, virial stress) and dispersion cross-term audits for B/N periodic cells remain open (Table~\ref{tab:sigma_S}).

\subsection{Constrained load and outlook}
\label{sec:discussion_future}
\label{sec:constrained_loading}

Rigid $|\mathcal{S}|$ is an upper-end protocol estimate, relative to the fully relaxed fixed-cell protocol studied here, for epitaxial clamp, adhesion, or ultrafast load where ions cannot relax (Fig.~\ref{fig:synergy}(b)). It is not a universal bound on every constraint manifold and not an equilibrium free-energy difference.
Ionic relaxation of periodic $n{=}1$ P suppresses the constrained cross term.
On the tetramer $P$ grid (Table~\ref{tab:relax}) the same relaxation reverses the sign and increases $|\mathcal{S}|$ ($+1.19$ to $-2.28$~meV/atom).
For a new host: fix or relax coordinates to the loading scenario, evaluate four-corner $\mathcal{S}$, and replace sequential screening when $|\mathcal{S}|$ is numerically resolved well above the protocol floor (Fig.~\ref{fig:synergy}(c,d)).
Larger-radius group-V substituents (As, Sb) are expected to couple more strongly than P under constrained load; that extrapolation is unverified.
Follow-up: Mayer/Bader partitioning, $\mathcal{S}(\mathrm{cutoff})$ and $\mathcal{S}(k)$, and periodic $n{=}4$ P relaxation.

\section{\label{sec:conclusions}Conclusions}

Additive screening omits a four-corner cross term under constrained load on qHP C$_{60}$.
On the matched 400~Ry core the representative value is $n{=}4$ ($|\mathcal{S}|=23.7\pm 2$~meV/atom, $\eta\approx 8\%$). The audited P span is $23.7$--$31.9$~meV/atom; $n{=}1$ ($31.9\pm 2$~meV/atom) is the strongest constrained coupling and the weakest $\Gamma$-only support. B carries a smaller resolved cross term, and N sits near the reporting floor.
$\mathcal{S}$ is a boundary-condition-dependent diagnostic, not an equilibrium universal material constant.
The $n{=}2$ $\mathcal{S}(n)$ row is reference-limited at the undoped corner (Methods; Table~\ref{tab:sigma_S}), not a sign change of the discrete species interaction.
$\mathcal{S}_\delta(\epsilon)$ is the discrete strain--substitution interaction at fixed dopant species; a mixed derivative is defined only after a local composition coordinate is introduced. It is not a new thermodynamic observable.
The four-corner $\mathcal{S}$ is a cross term among those neutral totals, not a charged-defect formation energy or a Marcus rate.
Linear $\alpha$ does not rank $|\mathcal{S}|$ among B/N/P and is itself poorly conditioned on six-point tetramer grids (standard errors comparable to or exceeding the slopes), so it cannot license the additive extrapolation [Fig.~\ref{fig:decoupling}(d)].
The larger $|\mathcal{S}|$ for P is consistent with a pre-strain, geometry-mediated contribution; the data do not uniquely partition elastic and electronic energy.
Relaxation suppresses the constrained cross term on periodic $n{=}1$ P and reverses and increases $|\mathcal{S}|$ on the tetramer (Fig.~\ref{fig:synergy}(b)).
In the present three-substituent audit the clearly large deviation occurs for the largest mismatch ($+30$~pm, P). That pattern is a consistency check on the misfit picture, not a $20$~pm threshold and not a fitted scaling law.
For the smaller mismatches audited here, coupled re-evaluation is optional, since B stays below $\eta\approx 1$\% and N sits at the reporting floor.
Other networks require their own four-corner audit before either conclusion is transferred.
That same cage-allotrope caveat applies to C$_{70}$ network band-edge proposals~\cite{Xu2025C70network} and to strain-induced magnetism in C$_{20}$ graphullerene~\cite{Nie2026strainC20}: neither replaces a four-corner $\mathcal{S}$ audit on the network under study.

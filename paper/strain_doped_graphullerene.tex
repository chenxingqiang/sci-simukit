\begin{abstract}
Sequential strain and doping scans are standard in covalent molecular networks, but total-energy effects need not add.
In quasi-hexagonal C$_{60}$ graphullerene we quantify the omitted cross term with a synergy order parameter $\mathcal{S}$ (meV/atom).
PBE+DFT-D3 on tetramers and periodic $n\times\mathrm{C}_{60}$ supercells ($n=1$--$8$) gives opposite strain coefficients for N ($-303$~meV/\%) and B ($+57$~meV/\%) and $|\mathcal{S}|$ up to $\sim32$~meV/atom at $+3$\% strain, largest for P despite small $|\alpha|$.
The pattern warns that sequential high-throughput screens can mis-rank stability when local dopant distortion couples nonlinearly to external strain.
\end{abstract}

\keywords{graphullerene, polaron localization, strain engineering, heteroatom doping, non-additive coupling}

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{\label{sec:intro}Introduction}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

Covalent molecular networks---from graphullerenes to covalent organic frameworks---are often tuned jointly by substitutional chemistry and mechanical load, yet many pipelines still estimate $E_{\mathrm{tot}}(\epsilon,\delta)$ from \emph{separate} doping-only and strain-only calculations~\cite{Materials2024untangling,Li2024strain}.
Synthetic carbon allotropes with extended $\pi$ conjugation underpin optoelectronic applications, yet charge transport in molecular networks is often polaronic~\cite{Capobianco2024electron,Yang2021two}.
Quasi-hexagonal (qHP) C$_{60}$ graphullerene~\cite{Yang2021two,Capobianco2024electron} provides a covalently linked model network for such coupled tuning.

Beyond pristine monolayers, strain and substitution independently tune qHP C$_{60}$ band edges~\cite{Li2024strain,Khan2025tuning,Peng2025monolayer}.
Non-additive strain--doping coupling has been reported in other two-dimensional networks~\cite{Materials2024untangling}; a quantitative energy cross term $\mathcal{S}$ [Eq.~\eqref{eq:synergy_order}] has not been reported for substitutional B/N/P qHP C$_{60}$ tetramers and $n=1$--$8$ supercells under a common PBE+D3 protocol.
If formation energies or strain penalties are estimated from \emph{separate} doping-only and strain-only scans, the omitted cross term can reach tens of meV/atom in coupled cells (this work)---large enough to mis-rank dopant stability or strain set-points in screening workflows.

This work establishes the energy coupling metric $\mathcal{S}$ on shared tetramer and size-scaling grids; Marcus $\lambda$ and hopping observables are deferred to Supplemental Material.

\section{\label{sec:methods}Computational Methods}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Setup and grids}

We use CP2K with PBE+DFT-D3 on shared tetramer and $n\times\mathrm{C}_{60}$ supercell grids ($\gtrsim 90$ converged tasks; Supplemental Material Table~S2), deliberately below the Koopmans/rVV10 level of Ref.~\citenum{Capobianco2024electron} to enable uniform $(\epsilon,\delta)$ scans.
Biaxial strain scales the pristine in-plane lattice at fixed fractional coordinates (rigid-strain upper bound; ionic-relaxation benchmark Table~S3 pending).
Tetramers carry $\sim 5$\% B/N/P substitution (seed~42); periodic cells carry one dopant per C$_{60}$ ($n=1$--$8$, 280~Ry for $n\geq 6$ vs.\ 300~Ry below).
Full basis, SCF, and grid definitions are in Supplemental Material.

\subsection{Energy observables}

Linear strain sensitivity $\alpha=\mathrm{d}E/\mathrm{d}\epsilon$ (meV per 1\% biaxial strain, tetramer \emph{total} energy) is obtained from the 24-point grid (Table~S1).
Positive $\alpha$ means tension \emph{raises} total energy; negative $\alpha$ means tension \emph{lowers} it under the rigid-strain protocol.
Substitution energy $E_f=(E_{\mathrm{doped}}-E_{\mathrm{pristine}})/n_{\mathrm{dopants}}$ is reported relative to pristine tetramers at the same strain (no chemical-potential reference).
In sequential high-throughput screens one often estimates $E_{\mathrm{tot}}(\epsilon,\delta)\approx E_f(\epsilon{=}0)+E_{\mathrm{strain}}(\epsilon)$; the neglected term is precisely $\mathcal{S}(\epsilon,\delta)$---the absolute error in that additive estimate.
For screening workflows, the magnitude of this error is best gauged against the substitution-energy scale: at $n=4$ and $+3$\% strain, P has $|\mathcal{S}|\approx 24$~meV/atom while the per-atom substitution scale is $|E_f|\times n_{\mathrm{dop}}/n_{\mathrm{atoms}}\approx 0.79$~eV/atom ($|E_f|\approx 16$~eV per P site, $n_{\mathrm{dop}}{=}12$, Table~S1)---i.e.\ $|\mathcal{S}|/|E_f|_{\mathrm{per\,atom}}\sim 3$\%, large enough to reorder stability margins in sequential doping-then-strain screens even though $|\mathcal{S}|$ exceeds the rigid per-atom strain increment ($\alpha\times 3\%/N_{\mathrm{atoms}}\lesssim 0.3$~meV/atom for P).

\section{\label{sec:results}Results}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

Results focus on total-energy non-additivity: band-edge trends provide context, $\alpha$ sets the linear strain scale, and $\mathcal{S}$ quantifies the omitted cross term (IPR/$J$ and Marcus $\lambda$ in Supplemental Material Fig.~S5--S6 only).

\subsection{\label{sec:electronic}Electronic structure under strain and doping}

Figure~\ref{fig:prl_main}(a) reports HOMO--LUMO gaps from tetramer single-points at $\varepsilon\in\{-5,0,+5\}$\%.
B introduces hole states near the valence manifold (acceptor character), compressing the $\pi$ gap toward zero at $\epsilon=0$, whereas N donates electrons into the conduction manifold (donor character) and P (larger covalent radius) perturbs the network more heterogeneously---hence the dopant-specific gap slopes in panel~(a).
Figure~\ref{fig:prl_main}(b) shows $\pi$-DOS for B @ $\varepsilon=0$.

\subsection{\label{sec:strain_response}Mechanical strain sensitivity}

Pristine tetramers are strain-insensitive ($|\alpha|\approx 1$~meV/\%).
Substitution opens a wide spread: N ($\alpha=-303.2$~meV/\%, tension stabilizes), B ($+56.9$~meV/\%, tension destabilizes), P ($-19.1$~meV/\%) (Table~S1).
Figure~\ref{fig:prl_main}(c) highlights the opposite $\alpha$ for N vs.\ B; ranking dopants by $\epsilon=0$ stability alone is misleading.

\subsection{\label{sec:synergy}Non-additive strain--doping coupling}

Figure~\ref{fig:prl_main}(d) tracks $\mathcal{S}(n)$ with one dopant per C$_{60}$.
At $n=4$ and $+3$\% strain, $\mathcal{S}=-6.2$~meV/atom (B), $+3.0$~meV/atom (N), and $-23.7$~meV/atom (P); P shows the largest magnitude.
Fifteen coupled points span $n\in\{1,2,4,6,8\}$ and B/N/P at $+3$\%.
For N, $\mathcal{S}$ changes sign between $n=6$ ($+2.4$~meV/atom) and $n=8$ ($-2.3$~meV/atom).
Because $n=6$--$8$ use 280~Ry whereas $n\leq 5$ use 300~Ry, this reversal may partly reflect basis-set change rather than intrinsic size physics; we treat it as provisional pending a matched-cutoff $n=6$ benchmark.

\section{\label{sec:discussion}Discussion}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

\textbf{Context.}
Prior work on pristine qHP C$_{60}$~\cite{Capobianco2024electron} and doped graphullerene networks~\cite{Khan2025tuning,Li2024strain,Materials2024untangling} motivates coupled $(\epsilon,\delta)$ scans; here we isolate the \emph{total-energy} cross term $\mathcal{S}$, distinct from hopping metrics in Supplemental Material Fig.~S6.

\textbf{Mechanistic synthesis (three coupling classes).}
\textbf{(i) Opposite $\alpha$ for N vs.\ B.}
N introduces excess $\pi$ electrons that strengthen bonding under tension (negative $\alpha$), whereas B creates electron deficiency and antibonding $\pi^\ast$ character that rises with lattice expansion (positive $\alpha$)---consistent with donor vs.\ acceptor $\pi$-counting and the PDOS shifts in Fig.~\ref{fig:prl_main}(a,b).
\textbf{(ii) Large $|\mathcal{S}|$ for P despite small $|\alpha|$.}
P's covalent radius ($\sim 107$~pm vs.\ $\sim 77$~pm for C) and longer P--C bonds impose intrinsic cage distortion; rigid external strain couples to this built-in local stress, yielding a cross term that is invisible in separate scans but large when strain and substitutional doping are applied jointly---consistent with P's intermediate $E_f$ yet largest $|\mathcal{S}|$ at $+3$\%.
\textbf{(iii) N $\mathcal{S}$ sign change with $n$.}
With one dopant per C$_{60}$, electrostatic and strain fields are dilute at large $n$; the sign flip between $n=6$ and $n=8$ may reflect finite-size electrostatics but is confounded with the 280~Ry cutoff introduced at $n\geq 6$---interpretation is deferred until a constant-cutoff control.
Together, (i)--(iii) suggest a taxonomy for covalent molecular networks: \emph{electronic} donor/acceptor control of $\alpha$, \emph{structural} local-distortion coupling to external strain (P), and \emph{finite-size} electrostatic dilution (N at large $n$).

\textbf{(iv) Linear $\mathcal{S}$--$\alpha$ check.}
Using tetramer $\alpha$ (Table~S1) in $\mathcal{S}\approx(\alpha_\delta-\alpha_0)\epsilon$ at $\epsilon{=}+3$\% and dividing by 240 sites gives ${\sim}{+}0.7$, $-3.8$, and $-0.2$~meV/atom for B/N/P---far smaller than the periodic $n{=}4$ values ($-6.2$, $+3.0$, $-23.7$~meV/atom).
The gap shows that tetramer-scale linear mechanics and 5\% doping do not quantitatively predict periodic $\mathcal{S}$; for P, nonlinear coupling dominates beyond first-order $(\alpha_\delta-\alpha_0)\epsilon$.

\textbf{(v) Significance versus DFT uncertainty.}
At $n{=}4$ and $+3$\% strain, $|\mathcal{S}|\sim 24$~meV/atom is $\sim 3$\% of the per-atom substitution-energy scale (Table~S1) but still $\gg$ typical rigid per-atom strain increments from $\alpha$.
PBE formation energies for molecular solids can carry $\mathcal{O}(10^2)$~meV/atom uncertainties; we therefore treat $\mathcal{S}$ as a \emph{coupled-scan correction} to sequential workflows rather than an absolute formation-energy prediction, and flag cases where $|\mathcal{S}|$ exceeds the rigid strain increment as the primary screening hazard.

\textbf{Design implications.}
At $+3$\% strain, P carries the largest additive-approximation error in $\mathcal{S}$ ($|\mathcal{S}|\sim 24$--$32$~meV/atom); co-design should treat P under tension as a \emph{coupled} stability problem, not $|E_f|$ at $\epsilon=0$ alone.
Where $\mathcal{S}<0$ (B and P at $n{=}4$), coupled tension can stabilize doped cells beyond additive predictions; where $\mathcal{S}>0$ (N at $n{=}4$), sequential screens overestimate stability under load.
Gap closing [Fig.~\ref{fig:prl_main}(a)] does not track $|\mathcal{S}|$; we do not compute $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{gap}}$, so photonic or transport targets cannot be inferred from total-energy $\mathcal{S}$ alone.
N $\mathcal{S}$ sign reversal by $n=8$ cautions against tetramer-only extrapolation.

\textbf{Limitations and outlook.}
Rigid strain (upper-bound on $\mathcal{S}$; ionic-relaxation check Table~S3 pending); fixed-seed substitutional sites without thermodynamic ranking; mixed 280/300~Ry cutoffs across $n$ (N $\mathcal{S}$ sign change at $n=6$--$8$); tetramer vs.\ scaling concentration mismatch; PBE+D3 without Koopmans/rVV10; gap non-additivity ($\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{gap}}$) not quantified; Marcus $\lambda$ and vertical transitions from charged geometry optimization still pending (Supplemental Material).

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{\label{sec:conclusions}Conclusions}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

Using qHP C$_{60}$ as a model covalent molecular network, we quantified non-additive strain--doping coupling in total energy.
Opposite $\alpha$ for N vs.\ B and finite $\mathcal{S}$ up to $\sim32$~meV/atom show that joint $(\epsilon,\delta)$ tuning is not equivalent to sequential strain and doping scans; for P under tension, the cross term is a few percent of the per-atom substitution-energy scale yet can significantly alter stability rankings in sequential screening workflows.
These energy-landscape metrics complement---but do not replace---Marcus $\lambda$ from charged-state vertical transitions needed to connect $\mathcal{S}$ to polaron hopping rates.

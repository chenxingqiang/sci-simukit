\begin{abstract}
Sequential strain and doping scans are standard in covalent molecular networks, but total-energy effects need not add.
In quasi-hexagonal C$_{60}$ graphullerene we quantify the omitted cross term with a synergy order parameter $\mathcal{S}$ (meV/atom).
PBE+D3 on tetramers and fifteen periodic $n\times\mathrm{C}_{60}$ supercells ($n=1$--$8$, B/N/P @ $+3$\%) gives opposite strain coefficients for N ($-303$~meV/\%) and B ($+57$~meV/\%) and $|\mathcal{S}|$ up to $31.9$~meV/atom (P, $n{=}1$) at $+3$\% strain, largest for P despite small $|\alpha|$.
The pattern warns that sequential high-throughput screens can mis-rank stability when local dopant distortion couples nonlinearly to external strain; Marcus reorganization energies are analyzed separately in Supplemental Material and do not enter $\mathcal{S}$.
\end{abstract}

\section{\label{sec:intro}Introduction}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

Covalent molecular networks---from graphullerenes to covalent organic frameworks---are often tuned jointly by substitutional chemistry and mechanical load, yet many pipelines still estimate $E_{\mathrm{tot}}(\epsilon,\delta)$ from \emph{separate} doping-only and strain-only calculations~\cite{Materials2024untangling,Li2024strain,Katiyar2025strain}.
Synthetic carbon allotropes with extended $\pi$ conjugation underpin optoelectronic applications, yet charge transport in molecular networks is often polaronic and anisotropic~\cite{Capobianco2024electron,Yang2021two,Silva2024large}.
Quasi-hexagonal (qHP) C$_{60}$ graphullerene~\cite{Yang2021two,Capobianco2024electron} provides a covalently linked model network for such coupled tuning, with interfullerene linking that raises hopping couplings relative to vdW C$_{60}$ monolayers~\cite{Capobianco2024electron}.
Ab initio models of pristine qHP networks report intrinsic intramolecular strain from the eight-bond hexagonal connectivity~\cite{Makov2023graphullerene}, so external biaxial load couples to an already distorted cage baseline.
Beyond pristine monolayers, strain and substitution independently reshape band edges~\cite{Li2024strain,Khan2025tuning,Peng2025monolayer,LopezAlcalay2025graphendofullerene}, while related 2D fullerene networks show anisotropic mechanical response under uniaxial load~\cite{Wang2024simulation} and endohedral qHPC$_{60}$ cage filling reshapes band gaps~\cite{Pereira2026endohedral}; none of these reports a periodic total-energy cross term under joint biaxial $(\epsilon,\delta)$ load, motivating explicit coupled grids rather than additive compositional estimates.
Non-additive coupling is documented in other two-dimensional networks~\cite{Materials2024untangling}; a quantitative \emph{total-energy} cross term $\mathcal{S}$ [Eq.~\eqref{eq:synergy_order}] has not been reported for substitutional B/N/P qHP C$_{60}$ tetramers and $n=1$--$8$ supercells under a single PBE+D3 protocol on fifteen periodic $(n,\delta)$ points at $+3$\% strain.

We address three questions on this model network: (1)~how opposite linear strain coefficients $\alpha$ emerge for donor vs.\ acceptor dopants; (2)~how large the omitted cross term $|\mathcal{S}|$ is when strain and substitution are applied jointly; and (3)~whether $|\mathcal{S}|$ tracks $|\alpha|$ or instead reflects local structural distortion.
Using $\gtrsim 90$ converged CP2K tasks~\cite{SupplementalMaterial} (Table~S2), we define $\mathcal{S}$ on shared tetramer and periodic grids; Marcus $\lambda$ and hopping observables (IPR/$J$) are analyzed only in Supplemental Material Fig.~S5--S6 and are not required for the non-additivity conclusions here.
The central narrative anchor is that \emph{local} dopant distortion and \emph{global} biaxial strain interact nonlinearly in total energy: $|\mathcal{S}|$ can exceed what linear $\alpha$ alone would suggest, so stability rankings from $\epsilon=0$ substitution energies or from strain-only elastic increments need not survive a coupled load.

\section{\label{sec:methods}Computational Methods}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Electronic-structure setup}

All calculations use CP2K~\cite{cp2k2025} with PBE~\cite{Perdew1996generalized} and Grimme DFT-D3 (Becke--Johnson damping)~\cite{Grimme2011effect} (Supplemental Material for basis, SCF, and task inventory).
We deliberately remain below the Koopmans/rVV10 level of Ref.~\citenum{Capobianco2024electron} to obtain a uniform, size-converged $(\epsilon,\delta)$ scan rather than absolute polaron binding energies.

\subsection{Notation and conventions}
\label{sec:notation}
Biaxial strain is denoted $\epsilon$ (percent); dopant species $\delta\in\{B,N,P\}$.
The synergy order parameter is always $\mathcal{S}$ (calligraphic) in meV/atom [Eq.~\eqref{eq:synergy_order}].
$\pi$-projected densities of states are written $\pi$-DOS (never bare ``pi'' in equations).

\textbf{Rigid-strain protocol.}
Biaxial strain scales the in-plane lattice vectors of the \emph{pristine} equilibrium cell at fixed fractional coordinates (no ionic relaxation under load).
Reported $\alpha$ and $\mathcal{S}$ under this protocol are \emph{upper bounds} on coupling under load until the ionic-relaxation benchmark in Table~S3 converges.

\textbf{Plane-wave cutoff (size scaling).}
Archived CP2K inputs use 400~Ry for $n\leq 4$ and 350~Ry for $n\geq 6$ (Table~S2); comparisons at fixed $n$ always share the same cutoff.
Cross-$n$ trends therefore mix a modest basis change at $n=6$; we do \emph{not} interpret the reported N $\mathcal{S}$ sign change between $n=6$ and $n=8$ as a physical size effect until a matched 400~Ry $n=6$ control completes (input \texttt{size\_6x60\_N\_pos3pct\_cutoff400}).

\subsection{Substitutional doping and grids}

Tetramer models ($4\times\mathrm{C}_{60}$, 240 sites) substitute B, N, or P at nominal 5\% (12--13 sites from archived coordinates, random seed~42), not a thermodynamic minimum.
A second seed~137 tetramer ensemble (Table~S4) tests whether $\alpha$ and tetramer-scale $\mathcal{S}$ are sensitive to dopant placement; periodic $\mathcal{S}(n)$ claims remain on the seed~42 periodic protocol until seed~137 periodic points are added.
Periodic scaling cells place one dopant per C$_{60}$ ($n=1$--$8$), so nominal concentration falls from $\sim 5$\% to $\sim 1.7$\% per atom at $n=4$.
Tetramer $\alpha$, gaps, and Supplemental Material IPR/$J$ trends are therefore not directly interchangeable with periodic $\mathcal{S}(n)$ without a matched-concentration benchmark; all $\mathcal{S}$ claims use the periodic protocol.
In total, $\gtrsim 90$ converged neutral tasks support the quantitative claims below (Table~S2).
Charged polaron geometry optimizations and vertical single-points for Marcus reorganization energies follow the same functional and basis choices but are reported separately in Supplemental Material Fig.~S5 once converged; they do not enter the $\mathcal{S}$ definition in Eq.~\eqref{eq:synergy_order}.
Batch workflow: \texttt{experiments/continue\_exp9\_pending.sh} (twelve charged GEO\_OPT, then eight vertical single-points at the neutral geometry); \texttt{post\_exp9\_converged.sh} refreshes \texttt{experiments/analysis/exp9\_polaron\_verification.json} and SI Fig.~S5 via \texttt{paper/figures/render\_si\_figures.sh} (11/12 GEO\_OPT at revision).

\subsection{Energy observables}

Linear strain sensitivity $\alpha=\mathrm{d}E/\mathrm{d}\epsilon$ (meV per 1\% biaxial strain, tetramer \emph{total} energy) is obtained from the 24-point grid (Table~S1).
Positive $\alpha$ means tension \emph{raises} total energy; negative $\alpha$ means tension \emph{lowers} it under the rigid-strain protocol.
Substitution energy $E_{\mathrm{sub}}=(E_{\mathrm{doped}}-E_{\mathrm{pristine}})/n_{\mathrm{dopants}}$ is reported relative to pristine tetramers at the same strain (no chemical-potential reference).
In sequential high-throughput screens one often estimates $E_{\mathrm{tot}}(\epsilon,\delta)\approx E_{\mathrm{sub}}(\epsilon{=}0)+E_{\mathrm{strain}}(\epsilon)$; the neglected term is precisely $\mathcal{S}(\epsilon,\delta)$---the absolute error in that additive estimate.
For screening workflows, the magnitude of this error is best gauged against the substitution-energy scale: at $n=4$ and $+3$\% strain, P has $|\mathcal{S}|=23.7$~meV/atom while the per-atom substitution scale is $|E_{\mathrm{sub}}|\times n_{\mathrm{dop}}/n_{\mathrm{atoms}}\approx 0.79$~eV/atom ($|E_{\mathrm{sub}}|\approx 16$~eV per P site, $n_{\mathrm{dop}}{=}12$, Table~S1)---i.e.\ $|\mathcal{S}|/|E_{\mathrm{sub}}|_{\mathrm{per\,atom}}\sim 3$\%, large enough to reorder stability margins in sequential doping-then-strain screens even though $|\mathcal{S}|$ exceeds the rigid per-atom strain increment ($\alpha\times 3\%/N_{\mathrm{atoms}}\lesssim 0.3$~meV/atom for P).

\section{\label{sec:results}Results}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

We organize the results around total-energy non-additivity: Sec.~\ref{sec:electronic} summarizes band-edge context; Sec.~\ref{sec:strain_response} reports linear strain coefficients and $E_{\mathrm{sub}}$; Sec.~\ref{sec:synergy} gives $\mathcal{S}(n)$ at $+3$\% strain across dopants and supercell sizes (Marcus schematics and IPR/$J$ diagnostics are in Supplemental Material Fig.~S5--S6 only; adiabatic IP/EA at revision come from eleven of twelve charged geometry optimizations---audit \texttt{exp9\_polaron\_verification.json}).

\subsection{\label{sec:electronic}Electronic structure under strain and doping}

Figure~\ref{fig:main}(a,b) summarizes tetramer HOMO--LUMO gaps and representative $\pi$-DOS (B @ $\epsilon{=}0$; color scheme follows Ref.~\citenum{Capobianco2024electron}).
B compresses the $\pi$ gap toward zero at $\epsilon{=}0$; N and P retain finite gaps; tensile strain narrows the pristine gap.
These band-edge data contextualize total-energy $\mathcal{S}$ but are not used to infer $\mathcal{S}$.

\subsection{\label{sec:strain_response}Mechanical strain sensitivity}

Pristine tetramers are strain-insensitive ($|\alpha|\approx 1$~meV/\%).
Substitution opens an order-of-magnitude spread in $\alpha$: N ($-303.2$~meV/\%, tension stabilizes the doped cell), B ($+56.9$~meV/\%, tension destabilizes), and P ($-19.1$~meV/\%, weakly stabilizing) (Table~S1).
The opposite sign for N vs.\ B persists across the $\epsilon\in\{-5,\ldots,+5\}$\% grid and is not an artifact of a single strain point.
At $\epsilon=+5$\%, N remains the most tension-stabilized dopant ($\alpha$ most negative), whereas B shows the strongest destabilization under tension; P sits between these extremes with a modest negative $\alpha$, foreshadowing why its linear coefficient poorly predicts $|\mathcal{S}|=23.7$~meV/atom for P at $n{=}4$ under $+3$\% strain (Sec.~\ref{sec:synergy}).
Substitution energies at $\epsilon=0$ span $E_{\mathrm{sub}}=+73.4$~eV/dopant (B), $-115.3$~eV/dopant (N), and $-15.8$~eV/dopant (P), with $n_{\mathrm{dop}}=12$--13 counted from input coordinates (Table~S1).
Figure~\ref{fig:main}(c) highlights the $\alpha$ split; ranking dopants by $\epsilon=0$ $|E_{\mathrm{sub}}|$ alone is misleading once $+3$\% strain is applied.

\subsection{\label{sec:synergy}Non-additive strain--doping coupling}

Figure~\ref{fig:main}(d) tracks $\mathcal{S}(n)$ with one dopant per C$_{60}$ at $+3$\% biaxial strain.
At $n=4$, $\mathcal{S}=-6.2$~meV/atom (B), $+3.0$~meV/atom (N), and $-23.7$~meV/atom (P); P shows the largest magnitude despite the smallest $|\alpha|$ on the tetramer grid (Table~S1).
Across fifteen coupled points ($n\in\{1,2,4,6,8\}$, B/N/P), $|\mathcal{S}|$ reaches $31.9$~meV/atom for P at $n=1$ and remains $\mathcal{O}(10)$~meV/atom at larger $n$ for B and P, whereas N is smaller in magnitude at $n=4$ but comparable at $n=1$.
For N, $\mathcal{S}$ changes sign between $n=6$ ($+2.4$~meV/atom) and $n=8$ ($-2.3$~meV/atom) at the production 350~Ry cutoff; this reversal is \emph{not} used in the main conclusions until a matched 400~Ry $n=6$ control and Table~S3--S4 benchmarks complete (Sec.~\ref{sec:validation}).
Size trends for B and P at fixed cutoff are more monotonic: $|\mathcal{S}|$ decreases from the $n=1$ corner toward $n=4$ but remains $\mathcal{O}(10)$~meV/atom at $n=4$--$8$.
We report provisional $\mathcal{S}(n)\approx \mathcal{S}_\infty + A/n$ fits in Supplemental Material but withhold asymptotic $\mathcal{S}_\infty$ claims until cutoffs are matched across $n$.
All periodic $\mathcal{S}$ values quoted in this subsection are archived in \texttt{experiments/analysis/sdc/sdc\_exp10\_synergy\_audit.json} (fifteen $n\times$ dopant points at $+3$\% strain).
Negative $\mathcal{S}$ (B, P at $n=4$) means coupled tension \emph{stabilizes} the doped cell relative to the additive estimate; positive $\mathcal{S}$ (N at $n=4$) means sequential screens \emph{overestimate} stability under load.

\section{\label{sec:discussion}Discussion}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

\textbf{Context.}
Prior work quantifies strain or doping effects separately in fullerene networks~\cite{Capobianco2024electron,Li2024strain,Khan2025tuning,Makov2023graphullerene,Materials2024untangling}.
Spin-DFT studies of substituted graphullerene report deep donor/acceptor levels and half-semiconductor gaps~\cite{Khan2025tuning}; here the observable is a same-functional \emph{total-energy} cross term $\mathcal{S}$ at fixed dopant placement, complementary to band-edge or spin-resolved analyses.
Related 2D fullerene derivatives report anisotropic mechanical response under uniaxial load~\cite{Wang2024simulation}, combined strain--substitution tuning in graphendofullerene~\cite{LopezAlcalay2025graphendofullerene}, and endohedral qHPC$_{60}$ band-edge engineering~\cite{Pereira2026endohedral}, but without the periodic total-energy cross term $\mathcal{S}$ defined here; atomic-scale mechanical benchmarks on pristine graphullerene~\cite{Qiu2025atomic} likewise address elasticity and tribology rather than substitutional $(\epsilon,\delta)$ cross terms.
Intrinsic intramolecular strain from the eight-bond qHP connectivity~\cite{Makov2023graphullerene} sets a distorted cage baseline; our rigid biaxial protocol superposes external load on that geometry.
First-principles strain--mobility studies on qHP and 1D C$_{60}$ polymers~\cite{Shi2023strainC60} adjust band alignments and carrier $\mu$ under load but do not quantify a joint substitutional--biaxial total-energy cross term.
Here we isolate the \emph{total-energy} cross term $\mathcal{S}$ under fixed PBE+D3; hopping metrics (Supplemental Material Fig.~S6) are not required for that conclusion.

\textbf{Mechanistic synthesis (three coupling classes).}
\textbf{(i) Opposite $\alpha$ for N vs.\ B.}
N introduces excess $\pi$ electrons that strengthen bonding under tension (negative $\alpha$), whereas B creates electron deficiency and antibonding $\pi^\ast$ character that rises with lattice expansion (positive $\alpha$)---consistent with donor vs.\ acceptor $\pi$-counting and the PDOS shifts in Fig.~\ref{fig:main}(a,b).
\textbf{(ii) Large $|\mathcal{S}|$ for P despite small $|\alpha|$.}
P carries the largest covalent-radius excess ($+30$~pm vs.\ C; Table~S5) yet shows the smallest strain-induced change in mean nearest C distance ($\Delta\bar{d}\approx 0.00003$~\AA\ from $\epsilon{=}0$ to $+3$\% vs.\ $+0.0085$~\AA\ for B).
Tensile load therefore couples to \emph{pre-existing} cage distortion from size mismatch rather than to a uniform elastic response, yielding a cross term invisible in separate scans but dominant when both knobs are engaged---consistent with P's intermediate $E_{\mathrm{sub}}$ yet the largest $|\mathcal{S}|$ at $n{=}4$ ($23.7$~meV/atom; audit max $31.9$~meV/atom at $n{=}1$) despite weak tetramer $|\alpha|$.
\textbf{(iii) N $\mathcal{S}$ sign change with $n$.}
With one dopant per C$_{60}$, electrostatic fields dilute at large $n$; any N sign flip between $n=6$ and $n=8$ is confounded with the 400$\rightarrow$350~Ry cutoff step and remains uninterpreted (Sec.~\ref{sec:validation}).
Together, (i)--(iii) suggest a taxonomy for covalent molecular networks: \emph{electronic} donor/acceptor control of $\alpha$, \emph{structural} local-distortion coupling to external strain (P), and \emph{finite-size} electrostatic dilution (N at large $n$).

\textbf{(iv) Beyond first-order $(\alpha_\delta-\alpha_0)\epsilon$.}
A one-parameter mechanical estimate $\mathcal{S}\approx(\alpha_\delta-\alpha_0)\epsilon/ N_{\mathrm{atoms}}$ uses tetramer $\alpha$ at 5\% doping (Table~S1) and is \emph{not} matched to the periodic $n{=}4$ concentration (${\sim}1.7$\% per atom).
We therefore use it only as a sanity check: at $\epsilon{=}+3$\% it gives ${\sim}{+}0.7$, $-3.8$, and $-0.2$~meV/atom for B/N/P, whereas the periodic $n{=}4$ rigid values are $-6.2$, $+3.0$, and $-23.7$~meV/atom.
The mismatch signals that the cross term is not reducible to linear strain sensitivity at a different concentration; P in particular requires explicit coupled evaluation.

\textbf{(v) Significance versus DFT uncertainty.}
$\mathcal{S}$ is a difference of total energies computed with the \emph{same} functional, basis, and cell for all four corners of Eq.~\eqref{eq:synergy_order}, so systematic errors largely cancel in sign and relative magnitude.
At $n{=}4$ and $+3$\% strain, $|\mathcal{S}|=23.7$~meV/atom for P is $\sim 3$\% of the per-atom $E_{\mathrm{sub}}$ scale (Table~S1) but still $\gg$ rigid per-atom strain increments from $\alpha$.
Large absolute uncertainties in PBE $E_{\mathrm{sub}}$ therefore do not invalidate the conclusion that sequential scans omit a meV/atom-scale cross term.

\textbf{Design implications.}
At $\epsilon{=}0$, $E_{\mathrm{sub}}$ favors P ($-15.8$~eV/dopant) over B ($+73.4$~eV/dopant; Table~S1), but at $n{=}4$ and $+3$\% strain the coupled correction is $|\mathcal{S}|=23.7$~meV/atom for P vs.\ $6.2$~meV/atom for B---sequential ranking by $E_{\mathrm{sub}}$ alone mis-estimates the load-dependent margin by an order of magnitude.
Dopants with larger covalent-radius excess (P: $+30$~pm vs.\ B: $+7$~pm; Table~S5) show the largest $|\mathcal{S}|$, so \emph{size-mismatched} substituents demand coupled $(\epsilon,\delta)$ screening rather than one-parameter pipelines.
The fifteen-point periodic audit (\texttt{sdc\_exp10\_synergy\_audit.json}) tabulates $|\mathcal{S}|$ at each $(n,\delta)$ for reranking high-throughput strain--doping workflows under the rigid protocol.
At $+3$\% strain, P carries the largest additive-approximation error in $\mathcal{S}$ ($|\mathcal{S}|=23.7$--$31.9$~meV/atom from $n{=}4$ to $n{=}1$); co-design should treat P under tension as a \emph{coupled} stability problem, not $|E_{\mathrm{sub}}|$ at $\epsilon=0$ alone.
Where $\mathcal{S}<0$ (B and P at $n{=}4$), coupled tension can stabilize doped cells beyond additive predictions; where $\mathcal{S}>0$ (N at $n{=}4$), sequential screens overestimate stability under load. For example, a workflow that ranks P vs.\ B stability at $\epsilon=0$ from $E_{\mathrm{sub}}$ alone and then applies $+3$\% strain sequentially would miss a $23.7$~meV/atom correction for P---comparable to several percent of the substitution-energy scale and much larger than the rigid strain increment from $\alpha$.
Gap closing [Fig.~\ref{fig:main}(a)] does not track $|\mathcal{S}|$; we do not compute $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{gap}}$, so photonic or transport targets cannot be inferred from total-energy $\mathcal{S}$ alone.
For B at $n=4$, both negative $\mathcal{S}$ and positive $\alpha$ imply that a strain-only elastic estimate would predict tension \emph{raises} energy while the coupled scan \emph{lowers} it relative to the additive model---a direct sign conflict that sequential pipelines cannot resolve without evaluating $\mathcal{S}$.
N $\mathcal{S}$ vs.\ $n$ is reported for completeness but excluded from design rules until Table~S2--S4 controls converge.


\textbf{Polaron transport (Supplemental Material only).}
The Marcus workflow in Fig.~S5--S6 parallels Ref.~\citenum{Capobianco2024electron}---adiabatic charged-state energies plus vertical evaluations at the neutral geometry---but at the uniform PBE+D3 level used for $\mathcal{S}$; it does not enter the total-energy non-additivity conclusions.
At revision, one charged geometry optimization (P, $q=-1$) remains in progress before eight vertical single-points populate $\lambda$.
Endohedral band-gap tuning~\cite{Pereira2026endohedral} and substitutional $\mathcal{S}$ address orthogonal knobs (cage filling vs.\ cage substitution) and are not interchangeable for sequential-screen corrections.

\textbf{Limitations and outlook.}
Three protocol limits are actively benchmarked (Sec.~\ref{sec:validation}): rigid strain (Table~S3), fixed-seed ensembles (Table~S4), and mixed 400/350~Ry cutoffs across $n$ (Table~S2 control task).
Additional limits: tetramer vs.\ periodic concentration mismatch; PBE+D3 without Koopmans/rVV10; $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{gap}}$ not quantified; charged-state geometry optimizations for Marcus $\lambda$ are incomplete (11/12 converged, vertical single-points pending); mobility tensors are not computed---polaron transport anisotropy in related fullerene networks is treated only comparatively~\cite{Silva2024large,Capobianco2024electron} (Supplemental Material Fig.~S5--S6).

\section{\label{sec:conclusions}Conclusions}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

In discrete covalent molecular networks, nonlinear coupling between dopant-induced local distortion and external strain can dominate sequential strain--doping energy estimates.
Using qHP C$_{60}$ as a model system, we quantified this cross term with $\mathcal{S}$ (meV/atom).
The three questions posed in the Introduction are answered as follows: (i)~donor N and acceptor B exhibit opposite linear strain coefficients $\alpha$ under the rigid protocol (Table~S1); (ii)~the omitted joint-load term reaches $|\mathcal{S}|$ up to $31.9$~meV/atom (P, $n{=}1$) at $+3$\% strain ($\mathcal{O}(10)$~meV/atom at larger $n$) across fifteen periodic points; and (iii)~$|\mathcal{S}|$ is not a monotone function of $|\alpha|$---P carries the largest $|\mathcal{S}|$ at $n{=}4$ ($23.7$~meV/atom) despite the smallest $|\alpha|$, consistent with covalent-radius mismatch in Table~S5.
Additive scans can mis-rank stability at the few-percent level of the substitution-energy scale.
The machine-readable synergy audit tabulates every $(n,\delta)$ pair for independent reranking under the rigid protocol.
Size-dependent N trends and the rigid-strain upper-bound protocol await ionic-relaxation, alternate-seed, and matched-cutoff benchmarks (Tables~S2--S4); coupled $(\epsilon,\delta)$ design rules complement strain-only mechanical anisotropy~\cite{Wang2024simulation}, derivative-network doping~\cite{LopezAlcalay2025graphendofullerene}, and endohedral band-edge tuning~\cite{Pereira2026endohedral}---orthogonal to substitutional total-energy $\mathcal{S}$ under biaxial load.
Supplemental Marcus $\lambda$ and hopping schematics (Fig.~S5--S6) are reported separately from $\mathcal{S}$ and remain incomplete until the charged-polaron geometry and vertical single-point batch finishes.

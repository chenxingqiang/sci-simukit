\begin{abstract}
Quasi-hexagonal (qHP) C$_{60}$ graphullerene hosts polaronic charge transport in which nearest-neighbor coupling $J$---not band dispersion---sets mobility scales~\cite{Capobianco2024electron}.
Here we quantify how \emph{joint} B/N/P substitution and biaxial strain reshape the \emph{total-energy} landscape of finite qHP supercells, independent of a full Marcus transport closure.
PBE+DFT-D3 calculations on tetramers ($36$ converged neutral points) and periodic $n\times\mathrm{C}_{60}$ cells ($n=1$--$8$; $40$ converged points) yield dopant-resolved $\pi$-gap evolution, opposite-sign mechanical coefficients $\alpha$ for N ($-303$~meV/\%) vs.\ B ($+57$~meV/\%), and a synergy order parameter $\mathcal{S}$ [Eq.~\eqref{eq:synergy_order}] with $|\mathcal{S}|$ up to $\sim32$~meV/atom at $+3$\% strain.
Across $n=1$--$8$ and B/N/P dopants ($15$ coupled points at $+3$\%), the effects of simultaneous strain and substitution are \emph{not} additive when applied sequentially.
At tetramer scale, a $2\times 2$ IPR/$J$ factorial (pristine/B $\times$ $\epsilon{=}0/+3$\%) shows B substitution dominating delocalization ($\Delta J\approx +20$~meV at $\epsilon{=}0$) while $+3$\% strain under B doping \emph{reduces} $J$ ($46\to 37$~meV); all four points remain in the small-polaron regime ($J\ll\lambda/2$).
\end{abstract}

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{\label{sec:intro}Introduction}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

Synthetic carbon allotropes with extended $\pi$ conjugation underpin optoelectronic applications, yet charge transport in molecular networks is often polaronic~\cite{Capobianco2024electron,Yang2021two}.
The quasi-hexagonal phase (qHP C$_{60}$, graphullerene)~\cite{Yang2021two} covalently links C$_{60}$ units, shortening interfullerene distances by $\sim$0.9~\AA{} and raising mobilities by nearly two orders of magnitude over vdW C$_{60}$~\cite{Capobianco2024electron}.
Capobianco \emph{et al.}~\cite{Capobianco2024electron} showed that the gain tracks enhanced nearest-neighbor coupling $J$ ($\sim$75--81~meV vs.\ 33~meV) within a hopping model, with similar polaron binding ($\lambda^{-}\sim 0.1$~eV) in both phases.

Beyond pristine monolayers, substitution and strain tune band edges~\cite{Khan2025tuning,Li2024strain,Peng2025monolayer}.
If formation energies or strain penalties are estimated from \emph{separate} doping-only and strain-only scans, the omitted cross term can reach tens of meV/atom in coupled cells (this work)---large enough to mis-rank dopant stability or strain set-points in screening workflows~\cite{Materials2024untangling}.
Non-additive strain--doping coupling has been discussed in other low-dimensional networks~\cite{Materials2024untangling,Li2024strain}; a quantitative cross term $\mathcal{S}$ [Eq.~\eqref{eq:synergy_order}] has not been reported for substitutional B/N/P qHP C$_{60}$ tetramers and $n=1$--$8$ supercells under a common PBE+D3 protocol.

We combine Capobianco-style observables (gaps, IPR, $J$) with systematic strain--doping energy scans on shared tetramer and size-scaling grids.
The present study establishes the \emph{energy} coupling metric $\mathcal{S}$ and band-edge trends; Marcus $\lambda$ and rate estimates are treated in Supplemental Material and are not required for the non-additivity conclusions here.

\section{\label{sec:methods}Computational Methods}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Electronic-structure setup}

All total-energy and electronic-structure calculations use CP2K~\cite{cp2k2025} with PBE~\cite{Perdew1996generalized} and Grimme DFT-D3 (Becke--Johnson damping)~\cite{Grimme2011effect}, as documented in Supplemental Material.
This level is deliberately chosen for a uniform, size-converged scan of $(\epsilon,\delta)$; it differs from the Koopmans-compliant PBE($\alpha_K$)+rVV10 framework of Ref.~\citenum{Capobianco2024electron}, which targets absolute polaron binding and mobilities in pristine supercells.

\textbf{Rigid-strain approximation.}
Unless stated otherwise, biaxial strain is imposed by scaling the in-plane lattice vectors at \emph{fixed fractional atomic coordinates} (no atomic relaxation under load).
This protocol upper-bounds strain energy and is applied consistently across the comparative grids below.
In related 2D carbon networks, allowing ionic relaxation under load typically reduces strain-energy derivatives relative to rigid cells~\cite{Li2024strain}; we therefore interpret reported $\alpha$ and $\mathcal{S}$ as \emph{upper bounds} on the rigid-lattice coupling, with relaxed-ion values expected to be smaller in magnitude but not necessarily vanishing.

Plane-wave cutoff is 300~Ry for cells with $n\leq 5$ fullerene units and 280~Ry for $n\geq 6$ (memory limit for $6\times$--$8\times$ cells on our hardware).
Because $\mathcal{S}(n)$ compares energies \emph{within} each $n$ at identical cutoff, trends at fixed $n$ are internally consistent.
Cross-$n$ comparisons---including the N $\mathcal{S}$ sign change between $n=6$ and $n=8$---coincide with the 300$\to$280~Ry switch and should be interpreted cautiously until a matched-cutoff check at $n=6$ is available (Supplemental Material Table~S2).

SCF convergence criterion $10^{-6}$~Eh with OT minimization; basis DZVP-MOLOPT-SR-GTH with matching GTH pseudopotentials.

\subsection{Substitutional doping protocol}

Tetramer models ($4\times\mathrm{C}_{60}$, 240 C sites) use substitutional B, N, or P at 5\% of carbon sites ($\sim$12 replacements per cell), chosen by a reproducible pseudo-random protocol (fixed seed~42) over all C coordinates---not a symmetry-averaged or thermodynamic minimum.
Periodic size-scaling cells place \textbf{one} substitutional dopant per C$_{60}$ unit (fixed count, so nominal concentration $\sim 1/60$ per molecule decreases as $n$ increases).
The two protocols differ in \emph{nominal dopant concentration} (5\% vs.\ $\sim$1.7\% at $n=4$); tetramer $\alpha$, gaps, and IPR/$J$ are therefore trend indicators, not directly interchangeable with $n=4$ periodic energies without a matched-concentration benchmark.
We do not re-optimize dopant sites; all reported $\alpha$ and $\mathcal{S}$ refer to these fixed, reproducible configurations and should be read as site-specific trends pending tests on alternate placements or thermodynamic minima.

\subsection{Computational grids}

In total, $\gtrsim 90$ converged CP2K tasks support the main-text claims (Table~S2); the principal neutral-energy grids are:

\textbf{Mechanical strain response (24 calculations).}
Pristine and B/N/P tetramers at $\epsilon\in\{-5,-2.5,0,2.5,5\}$\% supply total energies for $\alpha=\mathrm{d}E/\mathrm{d}\epsilon$ and substitution energies $E_f$ (Table~S1).

\textbf{Electronic structure (12 calculations).}
The same tetramer compositions at $\epsilon\in\{-5,0,+5\}$\% yield $\pi$-projected DOS, HOMO--LUMO gaps, and band-edge energies.

\textbf{Polaron localization ($2\times 2$ factorial, four calculations).}
IPR and nearest-neighbor coupling $J$ are evaluated on a $2\times 2$ grid: pristine vs.\ B doping crossed with $\epsilon\in\{0,+3\}$\% (converged neutral single-points; Supplemental Material Fig.~S6).

\textbf{Charged polarons (Supplemental Material).}
Tetramer GEO\_OPT at $q=\pm1$ and vertical single-points at neutral geometry target Marcus $\lambda^{\pm}$~\cite{Capobianco2024electron}; they are not used for $\mathcal{S}$ or $\alpha$ in the main text.

\textbf{Non-additive size scaling (40 calculations).}
Periodic cells at $\epsilon\in\{0,+3\}$\% for each dopant and $n=1$--$8$ supply $\mathcal{S}(n)$; dopant count is one site per C$_{60}$ unit (Table~S2).

\textbf{Reference geometries (Supplemental Material).}
Neutral tetramer relaxations and a pristine single-point on the relaxed cell define charged-state input geometries.

\subsection{Energy observables}

Linear strain sensitivity $\alpha=\mathrm{d}E/\mathrm{d}\epsilon$ (meV per 1\% biaxial strain, tetramer \emph{total} energy) is obtained from the 24-point grid (Table~S1).
Positive $\alpha$ means tension \emph{raises} total energy; negative $\alpha$ means tension \emph{lowers} it under the rigid-strain protocol.
Substitution energy $E_f=(E_{\mathrm{doped}}-E_{\mathrm{pristine}})/n_{\mathrm{dopants}}$ is reported relative to pristine tetramers at the same strain (no chemical-potential reference).
For screening workflows, the operative error from neglecting $\mathcal{S}$ is best gauged against the substitution-energy scale: at $n=4$ and $+3$\% strain, P has $|\mathcal{S}|\approx 24$~meV/atom while the per-atom substitution scale is $|E_f|\times n_{\mathrm{dop}}/(n_{\mathrm{atoms}})\approx 0.5$~eV/atom ($|E_f|\approx 32$~eV per P site, Table~S1)---i.e.\ $|\mathcal{S}|/|E_f|_{\mathrm{per\,atom}}\sim 5$\%, large enough to reorder stability margins in sequential doping-then-strain screens even though $|\mathcal{S}|$ exceeds the rigid per-atom strain increment ($\alpha\times 3\%/N_{\mathrm{atoms}}\lesssim 0.3$~meV/atom for P).

\section{\label{sec:results}Results}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

Results are organized parallel to Ref.~\citenum{Capobianco2024electron}: band-edge electronics and localization precede transport-relevant coupling, after which we report mechanical and non-additive energy cross terms specific to coupled $(\epsilon,\delta)$ tuning.

\subsection{\label{sec:electronic}Electronic structure under strain and doping}

Figure~\ref{fig:prl_main}(a) reports HOMO--LUMO gaps from tetramer single-points at $\varepsilon\in\{-5,0,+5\}$\%.
B introduces hole states near the valence manifold (acceptor character), compressing the $\pi$ gap toward zero at $\epsilon=0$, whereas N donates electrons into the conduction manifold (donor character) and P (larger covalent radius) perturbs the network more heterogeneously---hence the dopant-specific gap slopes in panel~(a).
Figure~\ref{fig:prl_main}(b) shows $\pi$-DOS for B @ $\varepsilon=0$.

\subsection{\label{sec:localization}Polaron localization and electronic coupling}

Capobianco \emph{et al.}~\cite{Capobianco2024electron} established that qHP electron polarons remain molecularly localized while $J$ grows with shorter interfullerene distances.
A $2\times 2$ factorial separates strain-only, doping-only, and coupled contributions (Supplemental Material Fig.~S6): pristine @ $\epsilon{=}0$ (IPR $=75.0$, $J{=}26.6$~meV); pristine @ $+3$\% (IPR $=76.1$, $J{=}26.5$~meV); B @ $\epsilon{=}0$ (IPR $=24.6$, $J{=}46.4$~meV); B @ $+3$\% (IPR $=23.1$, $J{=}37.2$~meV).
B substitution accounts for most of the IPR drop ($\Delta\mathrm{IPR}\approx -50$) and the zero-strain $J$ enhancement ($\Delta J\approx +20$~meV); $+3$\% strain on pristine is negligible, but under B doping it \emph{lowers} $J$ by $\sim 9$~meV (negative $J$ cross term).
Using literature $\lambda\sim 0.1$~eV as an order-of-magnitude bound~\cite{Capobianco2024electron}, all four points satisfy $J\ll\lambda/2$ (small-polaron hopping); this regime is inferred, not measured here.
Figure~\ref{fig:prl_main}(d) inset and Supplemental Material Fig.~S6 summarize the grid; absolute $J$ is below pristine-network values because of fragment size and functional choice.

\subsection{\label{sec:strain_response}Mechanical strain sensitivity}

Pristine tetramers are strain-insensitive ($|\alpha|\approx 1$~meV/\%).
Substitution opens a wide spread: N ($\alpha=-303.2$~meV/\%, tension stabilizes), B ($+56.9$~meV/\%, tension destabilizes), P ($-19.1$~meV/\%) (Table~S1).
Figure~\ref{fig:prl_main}(c) highlights the opposite $\alpha$ for N vs.\ B; ranking dopants by $\epsilon=0$ stability alone is misleading.

\subsection{\label{sec:synergy}Non-additive strain--doping coupling}

Figure~\ref{fig:prl_main}(d) tracks $\mathcal{S}(n)$ with one dopant per C$_{60}$; at $n=4$ and $+3$\% strain, P shows the largest correction ($|\mathcal{S}|\approx 24$~meV/atom).
Fifteen coupled points span $n\in\{1,2,4,6,8\}$ and B/N/P at $+3$\%.
For N, $\mathcal{S}$ changes sign between $n=6$ ($+2.4$~meV/atom) and $n=8$ ($-2.3$~meV/atom).
Because $n=6$--$8$ use 280~Ry whereas $n\leq 5$ use 300~Ry, this reversal may partly reflect basis-set change rather than intrinsic size physics; we treat it as provisional pending a matched-cutoff $n=6$ benchmark.

\section{\label{sec:discussion}Discussion}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

\textbf{Relation to Ref.~\citenum{Capobianco2024electron}.}
Capobianco \emph{et al.}~\cite{Capobianco2024electron} compared \emph{pristine} vdW and qHP C$_{60}$ and identified enhanced $J$---not reduced $\lambda$---as the dominant mobility lever, with polaron hopping retained in both phases.
Our study transfers that observables vocabulary (gap edges, IPR, $J$) to \emph{doped} tetramers under strain and adds an energy cross term $\mathcal{S}$ absent from sequential doping-only or strain-only scans~\cite{Khan2025tuning,Li2024strain}.
Where Khan and Leuenberger~\cite{Khan2025tuning} classify impurity-engineered \emph{half-semiconductor} gaps at fixed geometry, our $\mathcal{S}$ analysis tests total-energy additivity under identical biaxial strain---complementary metrics for impurity--strain co-design~\cite{Materials2024untangling}.
The factorial $J$ pattern (B raises $J$ at $\epsilon{=}0$ but $+3$\% strain partially reverses it) is qualitatively aligned with Capobianco's distance--coupling picture but does not, by itself, link $\mathcal{S}$ to mobility.

\textbf{Mechanistic synthesis.}
\textbf{(i) Opposite $\alpha$ for N vs.\ B.}
N introduces excess $\pi$ electrons that strengthen bonding under tension (negative $\alpha$), whereas B creates electron deficiency and antibonding $\pi^\ast$ character that rises with lattice expansion (positive $\alpha$)---consistent with donor vs.\ acceptor $\pi$-counting and the PDOS shifts in Fig.~\ref{fig:prl_main}(a,b).
\textbf{(ii) Large $|\mathcal{S}|$ for P despite small $|\alpha|$.}
P's larger atomic radius and longer P--C bonds perturb the qHP cage anisotropically; rigid strain then couples to \emph{pre-existing} local distortion, producing a cross term that is weak in either single-knob scan but strong when both are applied---consistent with P's intermediate $E_f$ yet largest $|\mathcal{S}|$ at $+3$\%.
\textbf{(iii) N $\mathcal{S}$ sign change with $n$.}
With one dopant per C$_{60}$, electrostatic and strain fields are dilute at large $n$; the sign flip between $n=6$ and $n=8$ may reflect finite-size electrostatics but is confounded with the 280~Ry cutoff introduced at $n\geq 6$---interpretation is deferred until a constant-cutoff control.

\textbf{Design implications.}
At $+3$\% strain, P carries the largest additive-approximation error in $\mathcal{S}$ ($|\mathcal{S}|\sim 24$--$32$~meV/atom); co-design should treat P under tension as a \emph{coupled} stability problem, not $|E_f|$ at $\epsilon=0$ alone.
Gap closing [Fig.~\ref{fig:prl_main}(a)] does not track $|\mathcal{S}|$; we do not compute $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{gap}}$, so optoelectronic targets may exhibit non-additive coupling distinct from total-energy $\mathcal{S}$---electronic and energetic knobs require joint optimization.
N $\mathcal{S}$ sign reversal by $n=8$ cautions against tetramer-only extrapolation.

\textbf{Limitations and outlook.}
Rigid strain (upper-bound $\mathcal{S}$); fixed-seed substitutional sites without thermodynamic ranking; mixed 280/300~Ry cutoffs across $n$ (N $\mathcal{S}$ sign change at $n=6$--$8$); tetramer vs.\ scaling concentration mismatch; PBE+D3 without Koopmans/rVV10; gap non-additivity ($\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{gap}}$) not quantified; Marcus $\lambda$ and vertical transitions from charged GEO\_OPT still pending (Supplemental Material).

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{\label{sec:conclusions}Conclusions}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

We quantified non-additive strain--doping coupling in heteroatom-substituted qHP C$_{60}$.
Opposite $\alpha$ for N vs.\ B and finite $\mathcal{S}$ up to $\sim32$~meV/atom show that joint $(\epsilon,\delta)$ tuning is not equivalent to sequential strain and doping scans; for P under tension, the cross term is a few percent of the per-atom substitution-energy scale yet dominates sequential additive estimates of stability.
These energy-landscape metrics complement---but do not replace---Marcus $\lambda$ from charged-state vertical transitions needed to connect $\mathcal{S}$ to polaron hopping rates.

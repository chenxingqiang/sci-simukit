\begin{abstract}
Sequential strain and doping scans are standard in covalent molecular networks, but total-energy effects need not add.
In quasi-hexagonal C$_{60}$ graphullerene we quantify the omitted cross term with a synergy order parameter $\mathcal{S}$ (meV/atom).
PBE+DFT-D3 on tetramers and periodic $n\times\mathrm{C}_{60}$ supercells ($n=1$--$8$) gives opposite strain coefficients for N ($-303$~meV/\%) and B ($+57$~meV/\%) and $|\mathcal{S}|$ up to $\sim32$~meV/atom at $+3$\% strain, largest for P despite small $|\alpha|$.
The pattern warns that sequential high-throughput screens can mis-rank stability when local dopant distortion couples nonlinearly to external strain.
\end{abstract}

\keywords{graphullerene, polaron localization, strain engineering, heteroatom doping, non-additive coupling}

\section{\label{sec:intro}Introduction}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

Covalent molecular networks---from graphullerenes to covalent organic frameworks---are often tuned jointly by substitutional chemistry and mechanical load, yet many pipelines still estimate $E_{\mathrm{tot}}(\epsilon,\delta)$ from \emph{separate} doping-only and strain-only calculations~\cite{Materials2024untangling,Li2024strain}.
Synthetic carbon allotropes with extended $\pi$ conjugation underpin optoelectronic applications, yet charge transport in molecular networks is often polaronic~\cite{Capobianco2024electron,Yang2021two}.
Quasi-hexagonal (qHP) C$_{60}$ graphullerene~\cite{Yang2021two,Capobianco2024electron} provides a covalently linked model network for such coupled tuning, with interfullerene linking that raises hopping couplings relative to vdW C$_{60}$ monolayers~\cite{Capobianco2024electron}.
Beyond pristine monolayers, strain and substitution independently reshape band edges~\cite{Li2024strain,Khan2025tuning,Peng2025monolayer}, but high-throughput pipelines often compose $E_{\mathrm{tot}}(\epsilon,\delta)$ from \emph{separate} scans.
Non-additive coupling is documented in other two-dimensional networks~\cite{Materials2024untangling}; a quantitative \emph{total-energy} cross term $\mathcal{S}$ [Eq.~\eqref{eq:synergy_order}] has not been reported for substitutional B/N/P qHP C$_{60}$ tetramers and $n=1$--$8$ supercells under a single PBE+D3 protocol.

We address three questions on this model network: (1)~how opposite linear strain coefficients $\alpha$ emerge for donor vs.\ acceptor dopants; (2)~how large the omitted cross term $|\mathcal{S}|$ is when strain and substitution are applied jointly; and (3)~whether $|\mathcal{S}|$ tracks $|\alpha|$ or instead reflects local structural distortion.
Using $\gtrsim 90$ converged CP2K tasks (Supplemental Material Table~S2; see Supplemental Material at [URL will be inserted by publisher] for the full DFT inventory, extended PDOS, and transport-adjacent observables), we define $\mathcal{S}$ on shared tetramer and periodic grids; Marcus $\lambda$ and hopping observables (IPR/$J$) are analyzed only in Supplemental Material Fig.~S5--S6 and are not required for the non-additivity conclusions here.
The central narrative anchor is that \emph{local} dopant distortion and \emph{global} biaxial strain interact nonlinearly in total energy: $|\mathcal{S}|$ can exceed what linear $\alpha$ alone would suggest, so stability rankings from $\epsilon=0$ substitution energies or from strain-only elastic increments need not survive a coupled load.

\section{\label{sec:methods}Computational Methods}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Electronic-structure setup}

All calculations use CP2K~\cite{cp2k2025} with PBE~\cite{Perdew1996generalized} and Grimme DFT-D3 (Becke--Johnson damping)~\cite{Grimme2011effect} (Supplemental Material for basis, SCF, and task inventory).
We deliberately remain below the Koopmans/rVV10 level of Ref.~\citenum{Capobianco2024electron} to obtain a uniform, size-converged $(\epsilon,\delta)$ scan rather than absolute polaron binding energies.

\subsection{Notation and conventions}
\label{sec:notation}
Biaxial strain is denoted $\epsilon$ (percent); dopant species $\delta\in\{B,N,P\}$.
The synergy order parameter is always $\mathcal{S}$ (calligraphic) in meV/atom [Eq.~\eqref{eq:synergy_order}].
$\pi$-projected densities of states are written $\pi$-DOS (never bare ``pi'' in equations).

\textbf{Rigid-strain protocol.}
Biaxial strain scales the in-plane lattice vectors of the \emph{pristine} equilibrium cell at fixed fractional coordinates (no ionic relaxation under load).
Reported $\alpha$ and $\mathcal{S}$ under this protocol are \emph{upper bounds} on coupling under load until the ionic-relaxation benchmark in Table~S3 converges.

\textbf{Plane-wave cutoff (size scaling).}
Archived CP2K inputs use 400~Ry for $n\leq 4$ and 350~Ry for $n\geq 6$ (Table~S2); comparisons at fixed $n$ always share the same cutoff.
Cross-$n$ trends therefore mix a modest basis change at $n=6$; we do \emph{not} interpret the reported N $\mathcal{S}$ sign change between $n=6$ and $n=8$ as a physical size effect until a matched 400~Ry $n=6$ control completes (input \texttt{size\_6x60\_N\_pos3pct\_cutoff400}).

\subsection{Substitutional doping and grids}

Tetramer models ($4\times\mathrm{C}_{60}$, 240 sites) substitute B, N, or P at nominal 5\% (12--13 sites from archived coordinates, random seed~42), not a thermodynamic minimum.
A second seed~137 tetramer ensemble (Table~S4) tests whether $\alpha$ and tetramer-scale $\mathcal{S}$ are sensitive to dopant placement; periodic $\mathcal{S}(n)$ claims remain on the seed~42 periodic protocol until seed~137 periodic points are added.
Periodic scaling cells place one dopant per C$_{60}$ ($n=1$--$8$), so nominal concentration falls from $\sim 5$\% to $\sim 1.7$\% per atom at $n=4$.
Tetramer $\alpha$, gaps, and Supplemental Material IPR/$J$ trends are therefore not directly interchangeable with periodic $\mathcal{S}(n)$ without a matched-concentration benchmark; all $\mathcal{S}$ claims use the periodic protocol.
In total, $\gtrsim 90$ converged neutral tasks support the quantitative claims below (Table~S2).
Charged polaron geometry optimizations and vertical single-points for Marcus reorganization energies follow the same functional and basis choices but are reported separately in Supplemental Material Fig.~S5 once converged; they do not enter the $\mathcal{S}$ definition in Eq.~\eqref{eq:synergy_order}.

\subsection{Energy observables}

Linear strain sensitivity $\alpha=\mathrm{d}E/\mathrm{d}\epsilon$ (meV per 1\% biaxial strain, tetramer \emph{total} energy) is obtained from the 24-point grid (Table~S1).
Positive $\alpha$ means tension \emph{raises} total energy; negative $\alpha$ means tension \emph{lowers} it under the rigid-strain protocol.
Substitution energy $E_f=(E_{\mathrm{doped}}-E_{\mathrm{pristine}})/n_{\mathrm{dopants}}$ is reported relative to pristine tetramers at the same strain (no chemical-potential reference).
In sequential high-throughput screens one often estimates $E_{\mathrm{tot}}(\epsilon,\delta)\approx E_f(\epsilon{=}0)+E_{\mathrm{strain}}(\epsilon)$; the neglected term is precisely $\mathcal{S}(\epsilon,\delta)$---the absolute error in that additive estimate.
For screening workflows, the magnitude of this error is best gauged against the substitution-energy scale: at $n=4$ and $+3$\% strain, P has $|\mathcal{S}|\approx 24$~meV/atom while the per-atom substitution scale is $|E_f|\times n_{\mathrm{dop}}/n_{\mathrm{atoms}}\approx 0.79$~eV/atom ($|E_f|\approx 16$~eV per P site, $n_{\mathrm{dop}}{=}12$, Table~S1)---i.e.\ $|\mathcal{S}|/|E_f|_{\mathrm{per\,atom}}\sim 3$\%, large enough to reorder stability margins in sequential doping-then-strain screens even though $|\mathcal{S}|$ exceeds the rigid per-atom strain increment ($\alpha\times 3\%/N_{\mathrm{atoms}}\lesssim 0.3$~meV/atom for P).

\section{\label{sec:results}Results}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

We organize the results around total-energy non-additivity: Sec.~\ref{sec:electronic} summarizes band-edge context; Sec.~\ref{sec:strain_response} reports linear strain coefficients and $E_f$; Sec.~\ref{sec:synergy} gives $\mathcal{S}(n)$ at $+3$\% strain across dopants and supercell sizes (IPR/$J$ and Marcus $\lambda$ remain in Supplemental Material Fig.~S5--S6).

\subsection{\label{sec:electronic}Electronic structure under strain and doping}

Figure~\ref{fig:main}(a) reports HOMO--LUMO gaps from tetramer single-points at $\epsilon\in\{-5,0,+5\}$\%.
B (acceptor) introduces states near the valence manifold and compresses the $\pi$ gap toward zero at $\epsilon=0$; N (donor) pushes electron density into the conduction manifold; P perturbs the network more heterogeneously because of its larger covalent radius and longer cage bonds.
Tensile strain generally narrows the pristine gap while dopant-specific slopes differ: B gaps remain the smallest across the grid, whereas N and P retain finite gaps at $\epsilon=0$ (panel~(a)).
Figure~\ref{fig:main}(b) shows representative $\pi$-DOS for B @ $\epsilon=0$ (valence-edge and conduction-edge coloring follows Ref.~\citenum{Capobianco2024electron}).
These band-edge trends contextualize the mechanical and non-additive energy results but are not used to infer $\mathcal{S}$.

\subsection{\label{sec:strain_response}Mechanical strain sensitivity}

Pristine tetramers are strain-insensitive ($|\alpha|\approx 1$~meV/\%).
Substitution opens an order-of-magnitude spread in $\alpha$: N ($-303.2$~meV/\%, tension stabilizes the doped cell), B ($+56.9$~meV/\%, tension destabilizes), and P ($-19.1$~meV/\%, weakly stabilizing) (Table~S1).
The opposite sign for N vs.\ B persists across the $\epsilon\in\{-5,\ldots,+5\}$\% grid and is not an artifact of a single strain point.
At $\epsilon=+5$\%, N remains the most tension-stabilized dopant ($\alpha$ most negative), whereas B shows the strongest destabilization under tension; P sits between these extremes with a modest negative $\alpha$, foreshadowing why its linear coefficient poorly predicts the large $|\mathcal{S}|$ at $+3$\% in periodic cells.
Substitution energies at $\epsilon=0$ span $E_f=+73.4$~eV/dopant (B), $-115.3$~eV/dopant (N), and $-15.8$~eV/dopant (P), with $n_{\mathrm{dop}}=12$--13 counted from input coordinates (Table~S1).
Figure~\ref{fig:main}(c) highlights the $\alpha$ split; ranking dopants by $\epsilon=0$ $|E_f|$ alone is misleading once $+3$\% strain is applied.

\subsection{\label{sec:synergy}Non-additive strain--doping coupling}

Figure~\ref{fig:main}(d) tracks $\mathcal{S}(n)$ with one dopant per C$_{60}$ at $+3$\% biaxial strain.
At $n=4$, $\mathcal{S}=-6.2$~meV/atom (B), $+3.0$~meV/atom (N), and $-23.7$~meV/atom (P); P shows the largest magnitude despite the smallest $|\alpha|$ on the tetramer grid (Table~S1).
Across fifteen coupled points ($n\in\{1,2,4,6,8\}$, B/N/P), $|\mathcal{S}|$ reaches $\sim 32$~meV/atom for P at $n=1$ and remains $\mathcal{O}(10)$~meV/atom at larger $n$ for B and P, whereas N is smaller in magnitude at $n=4$ but comparable at $n=1$.
For N, $\mathcal{S}$ changes sign between $n=6$ ($+2.4$~meV/atom) and $n=8$ ($-2.3$~meV/atom) at the production 350~Ry cutoff; this reversal is \emph{not} used in the main conclusions until a matched 400~Ry $n=6$ control and Table~S3--S4 benchmarks complete (Sec.~\ref{sec:validation}).
Size trends for B and P at fixed cutoff are more monotonic: $|\mathcal{S}|$ decreases from the $n=1$ corner toward $n=4$ but remains $\mathcal{O}(10)$~meV/atom at $n=4$--$8$.
We report provisional $\mathcal{S}(n)\approx \mathcal{S}_\infty + A/n$ fits in Supplemental Material but withhold asymptotic $\mathcal{S}_\infty$ claims until cutoffs are matched across $n$.
Negative $\mathcal{S}$ (B, P at $n=4$) means coupled tension \emph{stabilizes} the doped cell relative to the additive estimate; positive $\mathcal{S}$ (N at $n=4$) means sequential screens \emph{overestimate} stability under load.

\section{\label{sec:discussion}Discussion}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

\textbf{Context.}
Capobianco \emph{et al.}~\cite{Capobianco2024electron} established qHP C$_{60}$ as a polaronic network whose mobility gain over vdW C$_{60}$ tracks enhanced interfullerene coupling rather than reduced reorganization energy.
Li \emph{et al.}~\cite{Li2024strain} and Khan and Leuenberger~\cite{Khan2025tuning} show that strain and impurity engineering independently move band edges in fullerene monolayers, while Materials2024untangling reports non-additive strain--doping effects in other 2D networks.
Our contribution is complementary: at fixed PBE+D3, we quantify the \emph{total-energy} cross term $\mathcal{S}$ omitted when those knobs are applied sequentially, distinct from hopping metrics analyzed only in Supplemental Material Fig.~S6.
Where Capobianco \emph{et al.}~\cite{Capobianco2024electron} emphasize that mobility gains track interfullerene $J$ rather than reduced $\lambda$, our results show that \emph{total-energy} stability under simultaneous strain and substitution is likewise not reducible to a sum of one-parameter scans---a caution that applies to materials discovery pipelines even when transport figures of merit are not computed.

\textbf{Mechanistic synthesis (three coupling classes).}
\textbf{(i) Opposite $\alpha$ for N vs.\ B.}
N introduces excess $\pi$ electrons that strengthen bonding under tension (negative $\alpha$), whereas B creates electron deficiency and antibonding $\pi^\ast$ character that rises with lattice expansion (positive $\alpha$)---consistent with donor vs.\ acceptor $\pi$-counting and the PDOS shifts in Fig.~\ref{fig:main}(a,b).
\textbf{(ii) Large $|\mathcal{S}|$ for P despite small $|\alpha|$.}
P's covalent radius ($\sim 107$~pm vs.\ $\sim 77$~pm for C) and longer P--C bonds impose intrinsic cage distortion before external load is applied; tensile strain then couples to this pre-existing local stress field rather than to a pristine cage, yielding a cross term that is invisible in separate doping-only and strain-only scans but dominant when both are engaged---consistent with P's intermediate $E_f$ yet largest $|\mathcal{S}|$ at $+3$\% and with the weak tetramer $|\alpha|$.
\textbf{(iii) N $\mathcal{S}$ sign change with $n$.}
With one dopant per C$_{60}$, electrostatic fields dilute at large $n$; any N sign flip between $n=6$ and $n=8$ is confounded with the 400$\rightarrow$350~Ry cutoff step and remains uninterpreted (Sec.~\ref{sec:validation}).
Together, (i)--(iii) suggest a taxonomy for covalent molecular networks: \emph{electronic} donor/acceptor control of $\alpha$, \emph{structural} local-distortion coupling to external strain (P), and \emph{finite-size} electrostatic dilution (N at large $n$).

\textbf{(iv) Linear $\mathcal{S}$--$\alpha$ check.}
Using tetramer $\alpha$ (Table~S1) in $\mathcal{S}\approx(\alpha_\delta-\alpha_0)\epsilon$ at $\epsilon{=}+3$\% and dividing by 240 sites gives ${\sim}{+}0.7$, $-3.8$, and $-0.2$~meV/atom for B/N/P---far smaller than the periodic $n{=}4$ values ($-6.2$, $+3.0$, $-23.7$~meV/atom).
The gap shows that tetramer-scale linear mechanics and 5\% doping do not quantitatively predict periodic $\mathcal{S}$; for P, nonlinear coupling dominates beyond first-order $(\alpha_\delta-\alpha_0)\epsilon$.

\textbf{(v) Significance versus DFT uncertainty.}
At $n{=}4$ and $+3$\% strain, $|\mathcal{S}|\sim 24$~meV/atom is $\sim 3$\% of the per-atom substitution-energy scale (Table~S1) but still $\gg$ typical rigid per-atom strain increments from $\alpha$.
PBE formation energies for molecular solids can carry $\mathcal{O}(10^2)$~meV/atom uncertainties; we therefore treat $\mathcal{S}$ as a \emph{coupled-scan correction} to sequential workflows rather than an absolute formation-energy prediction, and flag cases where $|\mathcal{S}|$ exceeds the rigid strain increment as the primary screening hazard.

\textbf{Design implications.}
At $+3$\% strain, P carries the largest additive-approximation error in $\mathcal{S}$ ($|\mathcal{S}|\sim 24$--$32$~meV/atom); co-design should treat P under tension as a \emph{coupled} stability problem, not $|E_f|$ at $\epsilon=0$ alone.
Where $\mathcal{S}<0$ (B and P at $n{=}4$), coupled tension can stabilize doped cells beyond additive predictions; where $\mathcal{S}>0$ (N at $n{=}4$), sequential screens overestimate stability under load. For example, a workflow that ranks P vs.\ B stability at $\epsilon=0$ from $E_f$ alone and then applies $+3$\% strain sequentially would miss a $\sim 24$~meV/atom correction for P---comparable to several percent of the substitution-energy scale and much larger than the rigid strain increment from $\alpha$.
Gap closing [Fig.~\ref{fig:main}(a)] does not track $|\mathcal{S}|$; we do not compute $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{gap}}$, so photonic or transport targets cannot be inferred from total-energy $\mathcal{S}$ alone.
For B at $n=4$, both negative $\mathcal{S}$ and positive $\alpha$ imply that a strain-only elastic estimate would predict tension \emph{raises} energy while the coupled scan \emph{lowers} it relative to the additive model---a direct sign conflict that sequential pipelines cannot resolve without evaluating $\mathcal{S}$.
N $\mathcal{S}$ vs.\ $n$ is reported for completeness but excluded from design rules until Table~S2--S4 controls converge.

\textbf{Limitations and outlook.}
Three protocol limits are actively benchmarked (Sec.~\ref{sec:validation}): rigid strain (Table~S3), fixed-seed ensembles (Table~S4), and mixed 400/350~Ry cutoffs across $n$ (Table~S2 control task).
Additional limits: tetramer vs.\ periodic concentration mismatch; PBE+D3 without Koopmans/rVV10; $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{gap}}$ not quantified; Marcus $\lambda$ pending charged vertical SP (Supplemental Material Fig.~S5).

\section{\label{sec:conclusions}Conclusions}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

In discrete covalent molecular networks, nonlinear coupling between dopant-induced local distortion and external strain can dominate sequential strain--doping energy estimates.
Using qHP C$_{60}$ as a model system, we quantified this cross term with $\mathcal{S}$ (meV/atom): $|\mathcal{S}|$ is not simply tied to linear $\alpha$ (P shows the largest magnitude despite small $|\alpha|$), and additive scans can mis-rank stability at the few-percent level of the substitution-energy scale.
Opposite N vs.\ B strain coefficients and $|\mathcal{S}|$ at $n=4$ for B and P show that donor/acceptor chemistry and load must be co-designed; size-dependent N trends await cutoff and placement controls (Tables~S2--S4).
Ionic-relaxation, alternate-seed, and matched-cutoff benchmarks remain to test generality beyond the rigid seed~42 protocol applied here.

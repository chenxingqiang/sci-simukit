\begin{abstract}
Under mechanically constrained loading on quasi-hexagonal (qHP) C$_{60}$ graphullerene, measurable deviations from additive strain--dopant energetics arise because frozen elastic incompatibility introduces a bilinear strain--composition interaction that can reach magnitudes comparable to high-throughput screening margins---not because a finite-difference estimator is merely nonzero.
The additive approximation in sequential workflows corresponds to neglecting the mixed second derivative $\partial^2 E/\partial\epsilon\,\partial\delta$ in the total-energy functional; matched four-corner audits on B/N/P tetramers and periodic $n\times\mathrm{C}_{60}$ supercells ($n=1$--$8$, PBE+D3 rigid protocol) show that this term is large enough to change stability rankings for size-mismatched phosphorus ($|\mathcal{S}|=31.9\pm 2$~meV/atom at $n{=}1$, $\eta\approx 8\%$ at $n{=}4$).
The four-corner difference $\mathcal{S}$ estimates that coupling [Eqs.~\eqref{eq:mixed_derivative}--\eqref{eq:four_corner}] and is not a new thermodynamic observable.
Non-additivity scales with frozen local elastic incompatibility ($|\Delta r_{\mathrm{cov}}|\gtrsim 20$~pm) rather than dopant identity alone; ionic relaxation nearly extinguishes $|\mathcal{S}|$, consistent with a dominant elastic-work contribution under constrained load rather than a partitioned claim of intrinsic electronic cross-coupling.
The audit signals when sequential additive workflows should be replaced by explicit coupled evaluations on the $E(\epsilon,\delta)$ landscape.
\end{abstract}

\section{\label{sec:intro}Introduction}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

Substitutional doping and mechanical strain are widely used strategies for tuning energetic stability and electronic structure in covalent molecular networks~\cite{Katiyar2025strain,Lv2026covalent}.
The additive approximation used in sequential high-throughput screening corresponds to neglecting the mixed second derivative $\partial^2 E/\partial\epsilon\,\partial\delta$ in the total-energy functional; the physical question is when that omitted bilinear term reaches magnitudes comparable to meV-per-atom stability margins under realistic loading boundary conditions.
While external biaxial strain modifies global bonding geometry, substitutional dopants introduce localized chemical stress, lattice distortion, and bond competition, so the two perturbations can become energetically coupled through higher-order cross terms in the total-energy expansion~\cite{vandewalle2009cluster}---i.e., the total response need not separate into strain-only and composition-only increments without a nonlinear interaction term.
Configurational interaction and cluster-expansion effective Hamiltonians for alloys already include explicit composition--strain cross terms~\cite{vandewalle2009cluster}, so a bilinear correction to $E(\epsilon,\delta)$ is expected in principle.
Expanding the energy per atom $e(\epsilon,x)=E(\epsilon,\delta)/N_{\mathrm{atoms}}$ about the pristine reference gives, schematically,
\begin{equation}
e(\epsilon,x) \approx e_{0} + a\,\epsilon + b\,x + c\,\epsilon\,x + \mathcal{O}(\epsilon^2,x^2,\epsilon^2 x,\epsilon x^2),
\label{eq:taylor_coupling}
\end{equation}
where $c\,\epsilon x$ is the lowest-order non-additive coupling between external strain and composition; at finite load, $c=\partial^2 e/\partial\epsilon\,\partial x$ is the bilinear coefficient whose relevance we test.
Although dopant composition is sampled discretely (one substituent per C$_{60}$ on a fixed map), Eq.~\eqref{eq:taylor_coupling} is interpreted as a local finite-difference representation of the underlying composition-dependent energy functional, not as a strict differential expansion in a continuous concentration field.
The continuum coefficient $\gamma\equiv\partial^2 E/\partial\epsilon\,\partial\delta$ characterizes the intrinsic mixed response; the four-corner estimator $\mathcal{S}$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:methods_coupling}) samples that response at $+3$\% biaxial load---a diagnostic, not the physical claim (full derivation: Supplemental Material, Sec.~\ref{S-sec:appendix_fd}).
In many high-throughput mapping studies, strain and substitution are nevertheless evaluated in separate density-functional theory (DFT) workflows and combined additively, in part because exhaustive coupled $(\epsilon,\delta)$ grids are computationally expensive~\cite{Materials2024untangling,Li2024strain,Katiyar2025strain}.
Although first-principles work has extensively mapped strain and doping separately across molecular and two-dimensional networks~\cite{Li2024strain,Peng2025monolayer,Khan2025tuning}, matched four-corner quantification of their total-energy coupling under simultaneous load has rarely been reported for fullerene-based covalent molecular networks.
We study quasi-hexagonal (qHP) C$_{60}$ graphullerene~\cite{Yang2021two,Capobianco2024electron} as an experimentally accessible model covalent molecular network.
Prior density-functional work largely falls into two classes: additive mapping studies that sample strain and substitution on separate grids~\cite{Li2024strain,Khan2025tuning,Peng2025monolayer,Capobianco2024electron,Tromer2022dft}, and reports of nonlinear strain--composition response in band edges, transport, or magnetism without matched four-corner \emph{total-energy} audits~\cite{Materials2024untangling,Silva2024large,Santra2024strain}.
A literature-positioning summary is given in Supplemental Material, Table~\ref{S-tab:lit_positioning}.
What remains unresolved for soft graphullerene cages is how large the bilinear correction is, when it reverses \emph{relative energetic stability} and dopant-site preference under simultaneous load---quantities that govern processing and defect retention rather than gap or mobility shifts alone---and when sequential additive workflows become unreliable.
Ab initio models of pristine qHP networks report intrinsic intramolecular strain~\cite{Makov2023graphullerene}.
A substitutional defect imposes a local chemical-stress and bond-distortion field on a pre-strained, elastically soft cage; together with global biaxial load, this motivates a periodic-scale audit of dopant--strain coupling beyond linear $\alpha$.

Here we demonstrate that additive strain--dopant energetics fail under mechanically constrained joint load on qHP C$_{60}$ because frozen elastic incompatibility generates a bilinear strain--composition interaction comparable to screening energy scales---not merely because a four-corner difference is nonzero.
On the total-energy landscape $E(\epsilon,\delta)$ under biaxial load---where \emph{relative energetic stability}, not band-edge shifts alone, governs accessible substitutional configurations---we test whether (i)~additive totals fail under joint $(\epsilon,\delta)$ load; (ii)~the omitted correction matters under mechanically constrained configurations; and (iii)~failure tracks covalent-radius mismatch rather than donor/acceptor $\pi$-electron descriptors alone.
We focus on qHP C$_{60}$ as an experimentally accessible, elastically soft model covalent network~\cite{Makov2023graphullerene,Yang2021two}; substitutional B, N, and P provide a minimal donor, acceptor, and size-mismatch triad (${\sim}7$--$30$~pm covalent-radius excess vs.\ C; Table~\ref{tab:III}).
Matched four-corner audits quantify the omitted bilinear term; $\mathcal{S}$ [Eq.~\eqref{eq:synergy_order}] reports its meV-per-atom value and is \emph{not} proposed as a new physical quantity (Sec.~\ref{sec:methods_coupling}).
The primary discovery is \emph{physical}: stored elastic incompatibility under constrained load can break additive high-throughput workflows---not a new descriptor or thermodynamic order parameter (Sec.~\ref{sec:mechanistic}).
On qHP C$_{60}$, the audit protocol is transferable only after repeating the same $(\epsilon,\delta,n)$ grid on each host; we report failure conditions on one network, not materials-general laws.

\section{\label{sec:methods}Computational Methods}

\subsection{Electronic structure method}

All total-energy calculations use CP2K~\cite{cp2k2025} with Goedecker--Teter--Hutter (GTH) pseudopotentials, the DZVP-MOLOPT-SR-GTH atom-centered basis, and the Gaussian and plane waves (GPW) scheme.

\paragraph{Exchange-correlation and dispersion.}
We use PBE~\cite{Perdew1996generalized} with Grimme DFT-D3 (Becke--Johnson damping)~\cite{Grimme2011effect} for the production dataset.
Hybrids or meta-GGAs (SCAN, r$^2$SCAN) can shift absolute totals by tens of meV per atom in carbon networks; production rankings are therefore framed as \emph{within-PBE+D3} comparisons of $\mathcal{S}$ among B, N, and P under matched load.
The four-corner construction [Sec.~\ref{sec:methods_coupling}] subtracts totals computed with identical XC, basis, dispersion, and numerical settings, so systematic shifts largely cancel when \emph{ranking} dopants at fixed protocol---but cancellation is not assumed without audit: on the tetramer $P$ grid, Grimme-D3 non-additivity contributes only $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{vdW}}\approx +0.33$~meV/atom (Table~\ref{tab:sigma_S}), far below $|\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{rigid}}^{\mathrm{PBE+D3}}|$ (Table~\ref{tab:II}).
Representative SCAN and r$^2$SCAN four-corner spot-checks on periodic $P$ at $n{=}1$, $+3$\% are targeted sensitivity tests (Table~\ref{tab:sigma_S}; Limitations); no meta-GGA $\mathcal{S}$ values appear in the main text without converged outputs.

\paragraph{Plane-wave mesh and Brillouin-zone sampling.}
Plane-wave cutoffs are 400~Ry for $n\leq 4$ and 350~Ry for $n\geq 6$ (REL\_CUTOFF 50~Ry; QS EPS\_DEFAULT $10^{-10}$~$E_{\mathrm{h}}$).
Comparisons at fixed $n$ always use the same cutoff.
Production supercells contain 60--480 atoms with in-plane lattice constants up to ${\sim}44$~\AA\ at $n{=}8$; inputs use $\Gamma$-only sampling.
Because $\mathcal{S}$ is a second-order difference on the \emph{same} supercell, uniform $k$-point or cutoff shifts that co-drifts all four corners largely cancel in the four-corner estimator; sensitivity must be audited on $\mathcal{S}(\mathrm{cutoff})$ and $\mathcal{S}(k)$, not on bare totals alone.
A partial cutoff audit at $6\times\mathrm{C}_{60}$ N and $+3$\% shifts one corner by only ${\sim}0.057$~meV/atom between 350 and 400~Ry, implying $|\Delta\mathcal{S}|<0.2$~meV/atom when only the mesh drifts (Table~\ref{tab:sigma_S}).
Dense-$k$ ($2\times 2\times 1$) four-corner benchmarks on $n{=}1$ B/N/P at $+3$\% are pending (Table~\ref{tab:sigma_S}); until they converge, the smallest reported $|\mathcal{S}|$ values ($2.3$--$3.0$~meV/atom for N and B at $n{=}1$) are interpreted with explicit $\Gamma$-only caution.

\paragraph{SCF convergence and four-corner precision.}
Unless noted otherwise, the SCF threshold is $\mathrm{EPS\_SCF}=10^{-6}$~$E_{\mathrm{h}}$ in inner and outer orbital-transformation loops (MAX\_SCF 300; OUTER\_SCF MAX 30).
Charged-state workflows in the Supplemental Material tighten this to $10^{-7}$~$E_{\mathrm{h}}$; selected stiff tetramer corners may use $10^{-5}$~$E_{\mathrm{h}}$ when outer SCF cycling persists at $10^{-6}$ (Table~\ref{tab:II}).
Because $\mathcal{S}$ is built from four independently converged totals [Eq.~\eqref{eq:synergy_order}], uncertainty is propagated as $\sigma_{\mathcal{S}}=2\sigma_e$ for uncorrelated per-atom corner errors $\sigma_e$ [Eq.~\eqref{eq:sigma_propagation}; Supplemental Material, Sec.~\ref{S-sec:error_prop}] (Table~\ref{tab:sigma_S}).
We use $\sigma_{\mathcal{S}}\lesssim 2$~meV/atom as the conservative reporting band on the $n\leq 4$ production grid.

\paragraph{Spin, charge, and mechanical boundary conditions.}
Substitutional periodic cells are charge-neutral closed-shell Kohn--Sham calculations (no spin polarization in the production $\mathcal{S}$ grid); Hirshfeld strain-path audits on $n{=}1$ B/N/P use spin-polarized KS only where odd electron counts require it (Sec.~\ref{sec:methods_population}).
We do not apply compensating background charge or charged supercells in the $\mathcal{S}$ audit: doping is modeled as neutral substitution, and $\Delta E_{\mathrm{sub}}$ is not a formation enthalpy (Sec.~\ref{sec:notation}).

Biaxial strain scales lattice vectors at fixed fractional coordinates (rigid-strain protocol).
This is a deliberate mechanical \emph{upper bound} relevant when ionic relaxation is kinetically restricted---epitaxial clamping on a rigid substrate, strong interfacial adhesion, or ultrafast biaxial loading---rather than an equilibrium free-energy prediction under fully relaxed internal coordinates (Sec.~\ref{sec:validation}; Discussion, constrained-loading paragraph).

\subsection{Strain and doping models}
\label{sec:notation}
Biaxial strain is defined as $\epsilon=(a-a_0)/a_0\times 100\%$, where $a$ ($a_0$) is the strained (pristine equilibrium) in-plane lattice constant; dopant species $\delta\in\{\mathrm{B},\mathrm{N},\mathrm{P}\}$.
The strain--dopant coupling energy $\mathcal{S}$ is defined in Sec.~\ref{sec:methods_coupling} [Eqs.~\eqref{eq:synergy_order}--\eqref{eq:four_corner}]; finite-difference derivation and stencil justification are in Supplemental Material, Sec.~\ref{S-sec:appendix_fd}.
$\pi$-projected densities of states are written $\pi$-DOS.

Tetramer models ($4\times\mathrm{C}_{60}$) substitute B, N, or P at nominal ${\sim}5$~at.\% on a fixed pseudorandom carbon-site map; configurations are not thermodynamically optimized.
Periodic supercells place one substituent per C$_{60}$ (${\sim}1.67$~at.\%) on a fixed production site map shared across $n=1$--$8$.
Linear strain sensitivity $\alpha=\mathrm{d}E/\mathrm{d}\epsilon$ (meV per 1\% biaxial strain) is obtained from rigid PBE+D3 tetramer grids; positive $\alpha$ means tensile strain increases the total energy.
The substitution energy difference $\Delta E_{\mathrm{sub}}=(E_{\mathrm{doped}}-E_{\mathrm{pristine}})/n_{\mathrm{dopants}}$ is not a thermodynamic formation enthalpy (no elemental chemical potentials).
Reported $\alpha$ and $\mathcal{S}$ under the rigid-strain protocol (below) are upper bounds on equilibrium magnitudes when ionic relaxation is allowed (Sec.~\ref{sec:validation}).

\paragraph{Rigid vs.\ relaxed mechanical protocols.}
\emph{Rigid strain} scales in-plane lattice vectors at fixed fractional atomic coordinates: ionic positions do not relax with $\epsilon$.
This models mechanically clamped or ultrafast-loaded configurations and yields an upper bound on $|\mathcal{S}|$ when internal stress cannot relax.
\emph{Relaxed strain} allows fixed-cell BFGS geometry optimization at each corner before the coupling energy is evaluated on matched coordinates (Table~\ref{tab:II}; Fig.~\ref{fig:synergy}(e)); ionic relaxation can reverse or nearly extinguish rigid $\mathcal{S}$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:methods_relax}).
Force convergence targets are $4.5\times10^{-4}$ and $3.0\times10^{-4}$~Ha/bohr (${\sim}0.023$ and ${\sim}0.015$~eV/\AA) at $\epsilon{=}0$ and $+3$\%, respectively, with matched displacement thresholds (Sec.~\ref{sec:methods_relax}); these are tighter than typical 0.02~eV/\AA\ loose-scan defaults because relaxation can drive $|\mathcal{S}|$ toward zero and must be resolved at sub-meV/atom scale.

\section{\label{sec:discussion}Discussion}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Main finding}
\label{sec:discussion_main}

The present results indicate that additive strain--dopant screening fails under mechanically constrained joint load because frozen elastic incompatibility introduces a bilinear strain--composition interaction comparable to meV-per-atom stability margins on the $E(\epsilon,\delta)$ landscape [Eq.~\eqref{eq:taylor_coupling}].
For phosphorus at $+3$\% biaxial load, that interaction is large enough to change high-throughput decisions ($|\mathcal{S}|=31.9\pm 2$~meV/atom at $n{=}1$; $23.7$~meV/atom at $n{=}4$; Table~\ref{tab:IV}) while linear $\alpha$ does not rank $|\mathcal{S}|$ or its sign [Fig.~\ref{fig:decoupling}(d)].
$\mathcal{S}$ estimates $\partial^2 E/\partial\epsilon\,\partial\delta$ on the audited grid---a finite-difference diagnostic of additive failure, not the continuum coefficient $\gamma$ itself (Sec.~\ref{sec:methods_coupling}; Supplemental Material, Sec.~\ref{S-sec:appendix_fd}).
Ionic relaxation nearly extinguishes $|\mathcal{S}|$ (Table~\ref{tab:II}; Fig.~\ref{fig:synergy}(e)), consistent with a dominant elastic-work contribution under constrained load rather than a claim of exclusively electronic or exclusively elastic origins without partition analysis (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion_limitations}).
Operationally, $|{\mathcal{S}}|>{\sim}2$~meV/atom and $\eta>{\sim}1\%$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:synergy}) flag cases where coupled re-evaluation is advisable on the present protocol map; these are practical screening heuristics, not thermodynamic boundaries.

\subsection{Mechanism}
\label{sec:mechanistic}

For size-mismatched P, the dominant chain is (Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism}): $\Delta r_{\mathrm{cov}}{=}+30$~pm $\rightarrow$ frozen misfit stress $\rightarrow$ overlap with biaxial tension $\rightarrow$ bilinear elastic work $\rightarrow$ largest $|\mathcal{S}|$.
\emph{Primary} control is geometric misfit; \emph{secondary} is electronic redistribution (Hirshfeld $\Delta q$ on P ${\sim}11$\% fractional $|\Delta q/q|$, below N's ${\sim}18$\%); \emph{tertiary} is $\pi$-DOS morphology.
Electronic structure responds rather than controls: $\pi$-DOS does not, by itself, rank periodic $|\mathcal{S}|$ at $n{=}4$ [Figs.~\ref{fig:electronic}(b,c) and~\ref{fig:decoupling}(d)].
On the audited triad, $|\mathcal{S}|$ rises with $|\Delta r_{\mathrm{cov}}|$ for B and P at $n{=}1$ ($7$ and $30$~pm $\rightarrow$ $8.4$ and $31.9$~meV/atom); N breaks a radius-only trend ($6$~pm, $2.3$~meV/atom).
B shows the largest $\Delta\bar{d}$ at $+3$\% yet modest $|\mathcal{S}|$; P shows the opposite (Table~\ref{tab:III}).
Ionic relaxation releases misfit stress cooperatively with external tension (Fig.~\ref{fig:synergy}(e)); Grimme D3 contributes $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{vdW}}\approx +0.33$~meV/atom at tetramer $P$ corners (Table~\ref{tab:II}), far below $|\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{rigid}}^{\mathrm{PBE+D3}}|$.

\subsection{Relation to literature}
\label{sec:discussion_literature}

The present work reports a periodic \emph{total-energy} audit of joint biaxial $(\epsilon,\delta)$ load on qHP C$_{60}$ (Table~\ref{tab:IV}), rather than strain-only, doping-only, mobility, or gap-centric mapping~\cite{Capobianco2024electron,Li2024strain,Khan2025tuning,Wang2024simulation,LopezAlcalay2025graphendofullerene,Pereira2026endohedral,Qiu2025atomic,Peng2025monolayer,ShaikhPeng2025thermal,Shi2023strainC60,Makov2023graphullerene,Lv2026covalent,Materials2024untangling,Santra2024strain,Tromer2022dft,Katiyar2025strain}.
Cluster-expansion and configurational-interaction formalisms already encode composition--strain cross terms ($c\,\epsilon x$ in effective Hamiltonians)~\cite{vandewalle2009cluster}; the present contribution is not a new prediction that such terms exist, but a \emph{matched four-corner total-energy audit} on a soft, pre-strained covalent molecular network at periodic scale, with explicit rigid-vs.-relaxed and placement benchmarks (Tables~\ref{tab:II}--\ref{tab:IV}; Supplemental Material, Table~\ref{S-tab:lit_positioning}).
Prior graphullerene studies often relaxed ions before comparing totals or combined separate grids additively~\cite{Capobianco2024electron,Li2024strain,Khan2025tuning}, masking transient nonlinear coupling under mechanically constrained conditions (Sec.~\ref{sec:constrained_loading}).
The audit is not claimed unique to qHP C$_{60}$; it is a transferable \emph{protocol} on soft covalent networks with local substitution, finite frozen strain, and incomplete relaxation (Sec.~\ref{sec:applicability}), validated here on one host only.

\subsection{Applicability and generality}
\label{sec:applicability}

The formalism targets hosts where the mixed derivative $\partial^2 E/\partial\epsilon\,\partial\delta$ can be sampled on a four-corner grid.
\emph{Applicable when:} (i)~local substitutional strain is present and cannot fully relax (size mismatch, rigid substrate, or short loading times); (ii)~bonding is short-range and elastically soft, so defect fields overlap under external load; (iii)~the host is a covalent network with intrinsic pre-strain or elastic anisotropy~\cite{Makov2023graphullerene,Alihosseini2023strain}; and (iv)~ionic relaxation is incomplete so stored elastic work remains.
\emph{Not applicable when:} structures are fully relaxed bulk references with negligible frozen internal stress; metallic or liquid phases where substitutional labels are ill-defined; or high-temperature regimes where configurational entropy dominates sub-meV energy differences (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion_limitations}).
Production calculations use equibiaxial $+3$\% load; uniaxial loading is expected to preserve the four-corner formalism but rescale the coupling coefficient through elastic anisotropy---a natural extension not audited here.

\subsection{Experimental signatures}
\label{sec:experimental_signatures}

Laboratory probes of the stress-field picture need not resolve $\mathcal{S}$ directly but can test \emph{additive failure}: Raman or infrared cage-mode shifts that deviate from separate strain-plus-doping calibrations; nanoindentation or substrate-bending protocols that impose strain while dopant chemistry is fixed; scanning-tunneling spectroscopy of local density of states that shifts without proportional mean bond-length change; X-ray photoelectron chemical shifts inconsistent with additive composition--strain models; and transmission-electron strain mapping combined with known substitutional loading (Table~\ref{tab:IV}, at.\% column).
Quantitative links require matched chemistry, strain calibration, and awareness that rigid-lattice audits bound constrained rather than fully relaxed configurations.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion_limitations}

Main-text $\alpha$ and $\mathcal{S}$ use PBE+D3 under the rigid-strain protocol; ionic relaxation can reverse or nearly extinguish $\mathcal{S}$ (Table~\ref{tab:II}).
Eq.~\eqref{eq:taylor_coupling} and the four-corner link [Eq.~\eqref{eq:mixed_derivative}] assume small perturbations on a single smooth energy branch; they lose validity at large strain (e.g.\ ${\gtrsim}10$\%) or across structural phase transitions, and the production audits intentionally remain at $+3$\% biaxial load inside the nominally elastic regime.
All totals are 0~K Born--Oppenheimer energies; finite-temperature free energies and configurational entropy remain unexplored and could reorder sub-meV competitors without restoring additivity if bilinear coupling persists at the electronic-structure level.
Production periodic cells use $\Gamma$-only sampling; dense-$k$ and SCAN/r$^2$SCAN spot checks remain targeted backlogs (Table~\ref{tab:sigma_S}).
Alternate placements (Table~\ref{tab:IV}) bound configuration specificity; Table~\ref{tab:III} geometry is a tetramer proxy (${\sim}5$~at.\%) without Mayer/Bader partitioning or matched periodic $n{=}4$ bond statistics.
With only three substitutional labels, the $\alpha$--$|\mathcal{S}|$ map [Fig.~\ref{fig:decoupling}(d)] is a qualitative rank comparison among $n{=}3$ chemistries, not a fitted correlation law or predictive model for linear $\alpha$.
Direct elastic-vs.-electronic energetic decomposition (Mayer bond order, Bader strain partitioning, virial stress) and dispersion cross-term audits for B/N periodic cells remain open (Table~\ref{tab:sigma_S}).

\subsection{Outlook, workflow, and predictions}
\label{sec:discussion_future}

\paragraph{Citation workflow.}
For a candidate host: (1)~relax or fix coordinates according to the loading scenario; (2)~compute separate strain-only and substitution-only increments; (3)~evaluate the four-corner $\mathcal{S}$ at the target $(\epsilon,\delta)$; (4)~if $|{\mathcal{S}}|>{\sim}2$~meV/atom or $\eta>{\sim}1\%$, replace sequential screening with explicit coupled DFT on the same functional and cell (Fig.~\ref{fig:synergy}(f,g)).
Rigid-strain $\mathcal{S}$ bounds epitaxial clamping, substrate adhesion, ultrafast loading, or nanoscale flexures where relaxation is incomplete (Sec.~\ref{sec:constrained_loading}).

\paragraph{Prediction.}
Within the audited covalent-radius trend, larger-radius group-V substituents such as As or Sb are \emph{expected} to exhibit stronger bilinear coupling under constrained loading than P, because frozen misfit stress should scale with $|\Delta r_{\mathrm{cov}}|$; this extrapolation is not verified by present calculations.
Targeted follow-up includes Mayer/Bader strain-path partitioning, $\mathcal{S}(\mathrm{cutoff})$, $\mathcal{S}(k)$ sensitivity curves, and periodic $n{=}4$ P ionic relaxation on the production map.

\paragraph{Physical relevance of mechanically constrained loading.}
\label{sec:constrained_loading}
Two loading regimes must be distinguished.
\emph{Constrained (rigid-lattice) audits} bound epitaxial clamping, substrate adhesion, ultrafast loading, or nanoscale flexures where internal coordinates cannot fully relax; here $|\mathcal{S}|$ can reach tens of meV/atom (periodic $n{=}1$ P at $+3$\%: $-31.9$~meV/atom rigid vs.\ $\sim 0.1$~$\mu$eV/atom relaxed at matched PBE+D3; Table~\ref{tab:II}).
\emph{Equilibrium (fully relaxed) workflows}---slow thermal strain on unclamped films, post-growth annealing, or standard geometry-optimized high-throughput screens---are expected to recover near-additive behavior when misfit stress is released (Fig.~\ref{fig:synergy}(e)); additive sequential screening may remain adequate there, and the present rigid-map magnitudes must not be read as equilibrium free-energy differences.
Rigid-strain $\mathcal{S}$ bounds omitted coupling when internal stress remains frozen; Fig.~\ref{fig:synergy}(e) contrasts stress preservation under rigid load with release upon relaxation.

\section{\label{sec:conclusions}Conclusions}

Nonlinear strain--dopant energetic coupling---not the symbol $\mathcal{S}$---is the primary finding: additive composition--strain screening fails under constrained load on qHP C$_{60}$ because stored elastic incompatibility generates a bilinear interaction large enough to reorder stability rankings at meV-per-atom margins ($|{\mathcal{S}}|>{\sim}2$~meV/atom and $\eta>{\sim}1\%$; Sec.~\ref{sec:synergy}).
The matched four-corner difference $\mathcal{S}$ quantifies that failure [Eqs.~\eqref{eq:mixed_derivative}--\eqref{eq:four_corner}] as a discrete mixed-derivative estimator, not a new thermodynamic observable.
On qHP C$_{60}$, donor/acceptor dopants produce an $\alpha$ sign split [Fig.~\ref{fig:decoupling}(d)] that does not rank $|\mathcal{S}|$ among the three audited species, while periodic $|\mathcal{S}|$ reaches $31.9\pm 2$~meV/atom for P at $n{=}1$ at fixed coordinates---an extremum within a broader $2.2$--$31.9$~meV/atom audit distribution (Fig.~\ref{fig:synergy}(d)) with $\eta\approx 8\%$ at $n{=}4$.
Non-additivity correlates with frozen local elastic incompatibility on the B/N/P triad rather than with dopant identity alone; for P, geometric misfit strain is primary, electronic redistribution secondary, and $\pi$-DOS morphology tertiary (Sec.~\ref{sec:mechanistic}; Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism}).
Ionic relaxation nearly extinguishes $|\mathcal{S}|$, consistent with a dominant elastic-work contribution under constrained load rather than an exclusive electronic-interaction mechanism (Table~\ref{tab:II}; Fig.~\ref{fig:synergy}(e)).
Coupled $(\epsilon,\delta)$ screening is advisable for $|\Delta r_{\mathrm{cov}}|\gtrsim 20$~pm substituents under \emph{constrained} $+3$\% loads; four-corner audits can pre-screen such cases before full relaxation (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion_future}).
Generalization beyond qHP C$_{60}$ requires matched four-corner audits on each host; the present study reports failure conditions and an audit protocol on one soft covalent molecular network and does not claim universal constants, second-host benchmarks, or materials-general laws.
Prior strain--doping studies often masked this coupling by fully relaxing structures before comparing totals or by assembling separate strain-only and doping-only scans additively (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion_literature}).
The theoretical landscape is $E(\epsilon,\delta)$; $\mathcal{S}$ is its quantitative diagnostic under stated boundary conditions, not the primary physical claim.

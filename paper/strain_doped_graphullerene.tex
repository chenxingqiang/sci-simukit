\begin{abstract}
Under a rigid-strain upper-bound protocol with fixed fractional coordinates, periodic coupling magnitudes in covalent molecular networks use PBE+D3 and bound---rather than predict---equilibrium total-energy cross terms until sign-qualitative ionic-relaxation benchmarks complete (Table~\ref{tab:III}; 2/4 fixed-cell corners converged).
Separate strain-only and doping-only workflows remain common, but total-energy effects under joint load are not necessarily additive.
In quasi-hexagonal C$_{60}$ graphullerene we quantify the omitted cross term with the synergy order parameter $\mathcal{S}$ (meV per atom) [Eq.~\eqref{eq:synergy_order}].
On tetramers (${\sim}5$\% nominal doping) and fifteen periodic $n\times\mathrm{C}_{60}$ supercells ($n=1$--$8$, B/N/P @ $+3$\%, one substituent per C$_{60}$, ${\sim}1.67$~at.\%), tetramer $\alpha$ from the archived legacy PBE rigid grid (Table~\ref{tab:I}) is opposite for N ($-303$~meV/\%) and B ($+57$~meV/\%), whereas periodic PBE+D3 yields $|\mathcal{S}|$ up to $31.9$~meV/atom at fixed coordinates for P at $n{=}1$ and $+3$\% strain, with P showing the largest $|\mathcal{S}|$ despite the smallest tetramer $|\alpha|$.
Sequential high-throughput screens can introduce systematic errors in the estimation of \emph{relative} stability margins when local dopant distortion couples nonlinearly to external strain.
Marcus reorganization energies and nearest-neighbor hopping parameters (Figs.~\ref{fig:marcus} and \ref{fig:j}) are reported for transport context but do not affect the non-additivity conclusions.
\end{abstract}

\section{\label{sec:intro}Introduction}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

Covalent molecular networks---from graphullerenes to covalent organic frameworks---are often tuned jointly by substitutional chemistry and mechanical load, yet many pipelines still estimate $E_{\mathrm{tot}}(\epsilon,\delta)$ from \emph{separate} doping-only and strain-only calculations~\cite{Materials2024untangling,Li2024strain,Katiyar2025strain}.
Synthetic carbon allotropes with extended $\pi$ conjugation underpin optoelectronic applications; charge transport in molecular networks is often polaronic and anisotropic~\cite{Capobianco2024electron,Yang2021two,Silva2024large,Shi2023strainC60}, yet prior work emphasizes band edges, mobility, or hopping rather than a periodic total-energy cross term under joint biaxial $(\epsilon,\delta)$ load (fifteen-point periodic audits here use PBE+D3; tetramer $\alpha$ in Table~\ref{tab:I} uses a separate legacy PBE archive).
Quasi-hexagonal (qHP) C$_{60}$ graphullerene~\cite{Yang2021two,Capobianco2024electron} provides a covalently linked model network for such coupled tuning, with interfullerene linking that raises hopping couplings relative to vdW C$_{60}$ monolayers~\cite{Capobianco2024electron}; recent surveys of covalent fullerene networks emphasize synthesis, catalysis, and transport properties~\cite{Lv2026covalent} but do not report a periodic total-energy cross term under joint biaxial $(\epsilon,\delta)$ load.
Ab initio models of pristine qHP networks report intrinsic intramolecular strain from the eight-bond hexagonal connectivity~\cite{Makov2023graphullerene}, so external biaxial load couples to an already distorted cage baseline.
Beyond pristine monolayers, strain and substitution independently reshape band edges~\cite{Li2024strain,Peng2025monolayer,LopezAlcalay2025graphendofullerene}, while substitutional B/N engineering can yield spin-polarized half-semiconductor gaps~\cite{Khan2025tuning}, and related 2D fullerene networks show anisotropic mechanical and tribological response under load~\cite{Wang2024simulation,Qiu2025atomic} and endohedral qHP C$_{60}$ cage filling reshapes band gaps~\cite{Pereira2026endohedral}; none of these reports a periodic total-energy cross term under joint biaxial $(\epsilon,\delta)$ load, motivating explicit coupled grids rather than an additive superposition of composition and strain effects.
Non-additive coupling is documented in other two-dimensional networks~\cite{Materials2024untangling}; a quantitative \emph{total-energy} synergy order parameter $\mathcal{S}$ [Eq.~\eqref{eq:synergy_order}] has not been reported for substitutional B/N/P qHP C$_{60}$ tetramers and $n=1$--$8$ supercells; here, fifteen periodic $(n,\delta)$ points at $+3$\% strain use PBE+D3, whereas tetramer $\alpha$ in Table~\ref{tab:I} comes from the archived legacy PBE rigid grid (no D3).

We address three questions on this model network: (1)~how opposite linear strain coefficients $\alpha$ (meV per 1\% biaxial strain of the \emph{total} cell energy) emerge for donor vs.\ acceptor dopants; (2)~how large the omitted synergy cross term $|\mathcal{S}|$ (meV/atom) is when strain and substitution are applied jointly; and (3)~whether $|\mathcal{S}|$ tracks $|\alpha|$ or instead reflects local structural distortion.
Using $\gtrsim 90$ converged CP2K tasks~(Table~\ref{tab:II}), we define $\mathcal{S}$ on periodic PBE+D3 grids (fifteen $(n,\delta)$ points at $+3$\% strain) and report opposite donor/acceptor $\alpha$ on the archived tetramer strain grid (Table~\ref{tab:I}), demonstrating that the largest periodic $|\mathcal{S}|$ at fixed coordinates (up to $31.9$~meV/atom for P at $n{=}1$) does not coincide with the smallest tetramer $|\alpha|$ for size-mismatched P.
\emph{Local} dopant distortion and \emph{global} biaxial strain interact nonlinearly in total energy: $|\mathcal{S}|$ can exceed what linear $\alpha$ alone would suggest, so \emph{relative} stability margins from $\epsilon=0$ substitution energies or strain-only elastic increments need not survive a coupled load even if they do not reorder dopants with widely separated $E_{\mathrm{sub}}$ values.
Reported $|\mathcal{S}|$ magnitudes are protocol upper bounds at fixed coordinates until Table~\ref{tab:III} ionic-relaxation benchmarks complete (2/4 corners converged).
Marcus $\lambda$ and hopping observables (Figs.~\ref{fig:marcus} and \ref{fig:j}) characterize transport-relevant quantities but are not used to infer $\mathcal{S}$.

\section{\label{sec:methods}Computational Methods}

\subsection{Electronic-structure setup}

All periodic audit and charged-state tasks use CP2K~\cite{cp2k2025} with PBE~\cite{Perdew1996generalized} and Grimme DFT-D3 (Becke--Johnson damping)~\cite{Grimme2011effect}; tetramer $\alpha$ and $E_{\mathrm{sub}}$ in Table~\ref{tab:I} come from the archived legacy PBE rigid grid (no D3).
Table~\ref{tab:II} summarizes basis sets, SCF convergence criteria, and the full task inventory.
We do not employ the Koopmans-compliant rVV10 approach used in Ref.~\citenum{Capobianco2024electron}, and instead adopt a PBE+D3 protocol for periodic and charged-state tasks suitable for large-scale parameter scanning, at the cost of absolute accuracy in polaron binding energies.

\subsection{Notation and conventions}
\label{sec:notation}
Biaxial strain is denoted $\epsilon$ (percent); dopant species $\delta\in\{B,N,P\}$.
The synergy order parameter is denoted by the calligraphic symbol $\mathcal{S}$, with units of meV per atom [Eq.~\eqref{eq:synergy_order}].
$\pi$-projected densities of states are written $\pi$-DOS (never bare ``pi'' in equations).

\textbf{Rigid-strain protocol.}
Biaxial strain scales the in-plane lattice vectors of the \emph{pristine} equilibrium cell at fixed fractional coordinates (no ionic relaxation under load).
Reported $\alpha$ and $\mathcal{S}$ under this protocol are \emph{upper bounds} at fixed coordinates, not equilibrium thermodynamic quantities. Table~\ref{tab:III} benchmarks four fixed-cell geometry-optimization corners (pristine and $P$-substituted tetramer at $\epsilon{=}0$ and $+3$\%) to test whether $\mathcal{S}$ retains \emph{sign} after ionic relaxation \emph{within the same tetramer grid} (magnitude ratios are not quantitative until a matched-functional rigid reference exists); it does \emph{not} validate periodic $n{=}4$ magnitudes, which differ in dopant concentration and boundary conditions. Main-text $|\mathcal{S}|$ values therefore bound---rather than predict---meV/atom-scale non-additivity until Table~\ref{tab:III} converges.

\textbf{Plane-wave cutoff (size scaling).}
Archived CP2K inputs use 400~Ry for $n\leq 4$ and 350~Ry for $n\geq 6$ (Table~\ref{tab:II}); comparisons at fixed $n$ always share the same cutoff.
Cross-$n$ trends therefore mix a modest plane-wave cutoff change at $n=6$; we do \emph{not} interpret the reported N $\mathcal{S}$ sign change between $n=6$ and $n=8$ as a physical size effect until the matched 400~Ry $n{=}6$ control in Table~\ref{tab:II} completes.

\subsection{Substitutional doping and grids}

Tetramer models ($4\times\mathrm{C}_{60}$, 240 sites) substitute B, N, or P at nominal 5\% (12--13 sites from archived coordinates); these dopant configurations are not thermodynamically optimized ground states.
A second tetramer placement ensemble (18 single-point energy tasks; Table~\ref{tab:IV}) tests whether tetramer $\alpha$ and $\mathcal{S}$ are placement-sensitive at ${\sim}5$\% nominal doping.
All periodic supercells propagate the same dopant site map (one substituent per C$_{60}$); quantitative $|\mathcal{S}|$ on the fifteen-point grid is therefore configuration-specific, whereas donor/acceptor sign trends for $\alpha$ are discussed as element-intrinsic qualitative expectations pending Table~\ref{tab:IV}.
Periodic scaling cells place one substituent per C$_{60}$ ($n$ dopants among $60n$ lattice atoms, ${\sim}1.67$~at.\% for all $n$), contrasting with the tetramer grid (${\sim}5$\% nominal).
Counting a single dopant in the full supercell (e.g., $1/240{=}0.42$~at.\% for the tetramer) is \emph{not} the concentration used here; atomic percent is always dopants per lattice atom ($n$ in $60n$).
Tetramer $\alpha$, gaps, and tetramer IPR/$J$ trends (Fig.~\ref{fig:j}) are therefore not directly interchangeable with periodic $\mathcal{S}(n)$ without a matched-concentration benchmark; all $\mathcal{S}$ claims use the periodic protocol.
In total, $\gtrsim 90$ converged neutral tasks support the protocol-audited claims below (Table~\ref{tab:II}); rigid-strain $|\mathcal{S}|$ magnitudes are upper bounds until Tables~\ref{tab:III} and \ref{tab:IV} complete.
Charged-state geometry optimizations (UKS, multiplicity~2, $\mathrm{EPS\_SCF}=10^{-6}$~$E_{\mathrm{h}}$) supply adiabatic ionization potentials and electron affinities ($E_{\mathrm{opt}}^{\pm}$).
Eight vertical single-point energy evaluations compute $E_{\mathrm{vert}}^{\pm}$ at the \emph{neutral}-geometry coordinates with charged UKS settings matched to the relaxed state ($\mathrm{EPS\_SCF}=10^{-7}$~$E_{\mathrm{h}}$ by default; B $q=-1$: $10^{-6}$~$E_{\mathrm{h}}$), giving $\lambda^{\pm}=E_{\mathrm{vert}}^{\pm}-E_{\mathrm{opt}}^{\pm}$ following Ref.~\citenum{Capobianco2024electron}.
These Marcus diagnostics appear only in Fig.~\ref{fig:marcus} and do not enter Eq.~\eqref{eq:synergy_order}.
\subsection{Energy observables}

Linear strain sensitivity $\alpha=\mathrm{d}E/\mathrm{d}\epsilon$ (meV per 1\% biaxial strain, tetramer \emph{total} energy) is obtained from the archived 24-point rigid tetramer grid (Table~\ref{tab:I}; legacy PBE without D3).
Periodic $\mathcal{S}$ and size-scaling energies use PBE+D3 throughout (Table~\ref{tab:II}).
Positive $\alpha$ means tension \emph{raises} total energy; negative $\alpha$ means tension \emph{lowers} it under the rigid-strain protocol.
Substitution energy $E_{\mathrm{sub}}=(E_{\mathrm{doped}}-E_{\mathrm{pristine}})/n_{\mathrm{dopants}}$ is the total-energy difference per dopant site at fixed strain \emph{without} elemental chemical-potential corrections; it is \emph{not} a thermodynamic standard formation enthalpy (${\sim}$eV-scale tabulated values) and is used only for relative ranking within this protocol.
In sequential high-throughput screens one often estimates $E_{\mathrm{tot}}(\epsilon,\delta)\approx E_{\mathrm{sub}}(\epsilon{=}0)+E_{\mathrm{strain}}(\epsilon)$; the neglected term is precisely $\mathcal{S}(\epsilon,\delta)$---the absolute error in that additive estimate.
For screening workflows under the rigid upper-bound protocol, the magnitude of this additive omission is best gauged against a substitution-energy reference scale: at $n=4$ and $+3$\% strain, P has $|\mathcal{S}|=23.7$~meV/atom while the tetramer P per-atom scale is $|E_{\mathrm{sub}}|\,n_{\mathrm{dop}}/n_{\mathrm{atoms}}\approx 0.79$~eV/atom ($|E_{\mathrm{sub}}|{=}15.8$~eV/dopant, $n_{\mathrm{dop}}{=}12$ on the 240-atom tetramer, Table~\ref{tab:I})---i.e.\ $|\mathcal{S}|/|E_{\mathrm{sub}}|_{\mathrm{per\,atom}}\sim 3$\% as an \emph{order-of-magnitude} illustration only, because tetramer (${\sim}5$\%) and periodic $n{=}4$ (${\sim}1.67$~at.\%) concentrations differ and Table~\ref{tab:I} is not on the same functional as periodic $\mathcal{S}$; this ratio is not used as a core nonlinearity test.

Pristine tetramers are strain-insensitive ($|\alpha|\approx 1$~meV/\%).

\subsection{\label{sec:synergy}Non-additive strain--doping coupling}

Figure~\ref{fig:main}(d) tracks protocol $\mathcal{S}(n)$ (rigid-strain upper bounds) with one dopant per C$_{60}$ at $+3$\% biaxial strain.
At $n=4$ under the fixed periodic dopant map (alternate placements not yet computed), $\mathcal{S}=-6.2$~meV/atom (B), $+3.0$~meV/atom (N), and $-23.7$~meV/atom (P); P shows the largest magnitude despite the smallest $|\alpha|$ on the tetramer grid (Table~\ref{tab:I}).
Across fifteen periodic points ($n\in\{1,2,4,6,8\}$, B/N/P), $|\mathcal{S}|$ reaches $31.9$~meV/atom for P at $n=1$ and remains $\mathcal{O}(10)$~meV/atom at larger $n$ for B and P, whereas N is smaller in magnitude at $n=4$ but comparable at $n=1$.
For N, $\mathcal{S}$ changes sign between $n=6$ ($+2.4$~meV/atom) and $n=8$ ($-2.3$~meV/atom) at the production 350~Ry cutoff; this reversal is \emph{not} used in the main conclusions until the matched 400~Ry $n{=}6$ control in Table~\ref{tab:II} and Tables~\ref{tab:III} and \ref{tab:IV} complete (Sec.~\ref{sec:validation}).
Core size-scaling claims focus on $n\leq 4$ at matched 400~Ry cutoffs; B and P show monotonic $|\mathcal{S}|$ decrease from $n=1$ toward $n=4$, whereas $n\geq 6$ trends are reported for completeness only and mix the 350~Ry production cutoff.
We report provisional $\mathcal{S}(n)\approx \mathcal{S}_\infty + A/n$ fits in the open data release but refrain from drawing conclusions about the asymptotic $\mathcal{S}_\infty$ value until cutoffs are matched across $n$.
All periodic $\mathcal{S}$ values quoted in this subsection use the fixed dopant-site map and are tabulated in Table~\ref{tab:II} (fifteen $n\times$ dopant points at $+3$\% strain).
On the reference tetramer placement, legacy PBE rigid $\mathcal{S}_{+3\%}^{\mathrm{tet}}$ from Eq.~\eqref{eq:synergy_order} is $+3.8$, $+5.7$, and $+1.0$~meV/atom for B, N, and P (Table~\ref{tab:IV}); alternate-placement repeats are pending and are not used to infer periodic $\mathcal{S}(n)$.
Negative $\mathcal{S}$ (B, P at $n=4$) means coupled tension \emph{stabilizes} the doped cell relative to the additive estimate; positive $\mathcal{S}$ (N at $n=4$) means sequential screens \emph{overestimate} stability under load.

\section{\label{sec:discussion}Discussion}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

\textbf{Context.}
Relative to strain-only, doping-only, mobility, or mechanical studies in qHP and related fullerene networks~\cite{Capobianco2024electron,Li2024strain,Khan2025tuning,Wang2024simulation,LopezAlcalay2025graphendofullerene,Pereira2026endohedral,Qiu2025atomic,Peng2025monolayer,ShaikhPeng2025thermal,Shi2023strainC60,Makov2023graphullerene,Lv2026covalent,Materials2024untangling,Santra2024strain}, the distinction here is a periodic \emph{total-energy} audit of joint biaxial $(\epsilon,\delta)$ load [Eq.~\eqref{eq:synergy_order}] on a rigid-strain upper-bound protocol (periodic PBE+D3; tetramer $\alpha$ from legacy PBE, Table~\ref{tab:I}).
Strain-engineering roadmaps for generic two-dimensional materials~\cite{Katiyar2025strain} likewise foreground band-edge and mobility levers under separate mechanical or chemical protocols rather than a matched four-corner total-energy grid on covalent fullerene networks.
In other 2D hosts, substitutional defects can raise or lower formation energies under biaxial strain depending on dopant--host size mismatch~\cite{Santra2024strain}; spin-resolved impurity engineering on qHP graphullerene instead targets half-semiconductor gaps~\cite{Khan2025tuning}; isomer-engineered C$_{70}$ network designs similarly tune band edges through topology choice rather than joint substitutional--mechanical load~\cite{Xu2025C70network}; we instead quantify the omitted additive cross term $\mathcal{S}$ in total energy. Quasi-harmonic thermal-expansion studies of qHP monolayers report anisotropic positive and negative lattice response tied to interfullerene bond topology~\cite{ShaikhPeng2025thermal,Peng2025monolayer}, whereas our audit holds fractional coordinates fixed at zero-temperature total energy (upper bound; partial ionic-relaxation checkpoint in Sec.~\ref{sec:synergy} and Table~\ref{tab:III}). Strain-engineering studies on smaller fullerene graphullerenes instead emphasize magnetic or half-metallic transitions~\cite{Nie2026strainC20}, which is orthogonal to the neutral total-energy audit pursued here.
Band-edge, spin-resolved, and hopping metrics (Fig.~\ref{fig:j}) contextualize mechanism but are not used to infer $\mathcal{S}$.

\textbf{Mechanistic synthesis.}
\textbf{(i) Opposite $\alpha$ for N vs.\ B.}
N introduces excess $\pi$ electrons that strengthen bonding under tension (negative $\alpha$), whereas B creates electron deficiency and antibonding $\pi^\ast$ character that rises with lattice expansion (positive $\alpha$).
Quantitatively, the $\epsilon{=}0$ gap ordering ($E_g^{\mathrm{N}}<E_g^{\mathrm{B}}<E_g^{\mathrm{P}}$ within PBE; Sec.~\ref{sec:electronic}) and dopant-resolved $\pi$-DOS in Fig.~\ref{fig:main}(b) and Fig.~\ref{fig:pdos} align with donor vs.\ acceptor $\pi$-counting, while total-energy $\alpha$ spans ${\sim}360$~meV/\% between N and B on the tetramer grid (Table~\ref{tab:I}).
The opposite N vs.\ B sign is reported for this fixed placement across $\epsilon\in\{-5,\ldots,+5\}$\% (Table~\ref{tab:I}); Table~\ref{tab:IV} repeats the full tetramer strain grid for an alternate placement to test slope sensitivity at ${\sim}5$\% nominal doping without mixing periodic boundary conditions.
Mayer bond order and Bader charge partitioning along the strain path are not yet computed (Limitations); periodic $\mathcal{S}(n)$ magnitudes are likewise not averaged over alternate supercell placements until such audits exist.
\textbf{(ii) Large $|\mathcal{S}|$ for P despite small $|\alpha|$.}
P carries the largest covalent-radius excess ($+30$~pm vs.\ C; Table~\ref{tab:V}) and pre-existing cage distortion at $\epsilon{=}0$, yet shows the smallest strain-induced change in mean nearest C distance ($\Delta\bar{d}\approx 3\times 10^{-5}$~\AA\ vs.\ ${\sim}8.5\times 10^{-3}$~\AA\ for B/N) and the smallest site-to-site spread at $+3$\% ($\sigma(\bar{d})\approx 4\times 10^{-4}$~\AA\ vs.\ ${\sim}2\times 10^{-2}$~\AA\ for B/N; Table~\ref{tab:V}).
Tensile load therefore couples to \emph{pre-existing} cage distortion from size mismatch rather than to a uniform elastic response, yielding a cross term invisible in separate scans but dominant when both strain and substitution are applied simultaneously---consistent with P's intermediate $E_{\mathrm{sub}}$ yet the largest $|\mathcal{S}|$ at $n{=}4$ ($23.7$~meV/atom; Table~\ref{tab:II}) despite weak tetramer $|\alpha|$.
Sec.~\ref{sec:strain_response} presents this Table~\ref{tab:V} geometric decoupling before the $n{=}4$ $\mathcal{S}$ ranking in Sec.~\ref{sec:synergy}: tetramer $|\alpha|$ ranks P as weakly strain-sensitive, whereas the coupled periodic audit exposes the largest cross term.
\textbf{(iii) N $\mathcal{S}$ sign change with $n$.}
With one substituent per C$_{60}$, electrostatic fields dilute at large $n$; any N sign flip between $n=6$ and $n=8$ is confounded with the 400$\rightarrow$350~Ry cutoff step and remains uninterpreted (Sec.~\ref{sec:validation}).
Together, (i)--(iii) suggest a taxonomy for covalent molecular networks: \emph{electronic} donor/acceptor control of $\alpha$, \emph{structural} local-distortion coupling to external strain (P), and \emph{finite-size} electrostatic dilution (N at large $n$).
\textbf{(iv) Geometric vs.\ electronic nonlinearity.}
For P, Table~\ref{tab:V} shows that rigid tensile load barely moves the mean dopant--C distance ($\Delta\bar{d}\approx 3\times 10^{-5}$~\AA) yet $\mathcal{S}$ remains the largest at $n{=}4$ ($23.7$~meV/atom), whereas B/N exhibit ${\sim}10^{2}\times$ larger $\Delta\bar{d}$ at $+3$\% but smaller $|\mathcal{S}|$ ($6.2$ and $3.0$~meV/atom).
At $n{=}4$ the coupled correction for P exceeds the rigid per-atom strain increment from tetramer $\alpha$ by two orders of magnitude ($|\mathcal{S}|\gg \alpha\times 3\%/N_{\mathrm{atoms}}$; Methods energy observables), even though $|\alpha|$ ranks P as the weakest strain responder on the tetramer grid.
\textbf{(v) Argument scope (concentration and boundary conditions).}
We do \emph{not} validate $\mathcal{S}$ by extrapolating tetramer $\alpha$ to periodic $n{=}4$: the ${\sim}5$\% tetramer vs.\ ${\sim}1.67$~at.\% periodic concentrations and tetramer vs.\ periodic boundary conditions are unmatched, so a first-order $(\alpha_\delta-\alpha_0)\epsilon/N_{\mathrm{atoms}}$ estimate is excluded from the argument.
Non-additivity is assessed only through Eq.~\eqref{eq:synergy_order} on matched four-corner grids at common $(\epsilon,\delta)$; P in particular requires explicit coupled evaluation rather than sequential $\alpha$ screening.
\textbf{(vi) Decomposition outlook.}
The Table~\ref{tab:V} decoupling indicates that the cross term is not reducible to uniform bond stretching alone; electronic rehybridization and pre-stored cage strain likely contribute, but their relative weights are not partitioned here without Mayer/Bader analysis along the strain path (Limitations).

\textbf{(vii) Significance versus DFT uncertainty.}
$\mathcal{S}$ is a difference of total energies computed with the \emph{same} functional, basis, and cell for all four corners of Eq.~\eqref{eq:synergy_order}, so systematic errors largely cancel in sign and relative magnitude.
At $n{=}4$ and $+3$\% strain, $|\mathcal{S}|=23.7$~meV/atom for P is $\sim 3$\% of the tetramer P per-atom $E_{\mathrm{sub}}$ reference (Table~\ref{tab:I}) but still $\gg$ rigid per-atom strain increments from $\alpha$.
Large absolute uncertainties in PBE $E_{\mathrm{sub}}$ therefore do not invalidate the conclusion that sequential scans omit a meV/atom-scale cross term.

\textbf{Design implications.}
At $n{=}4$ and $+3$\% strain the coupled correction is $|\mathcal{S}|=23.7$~meV/atom for P vs.\ $6.2$~meV/atom for B---sequential \emph{additive} estimates differ by approximately a factor of four in the load-dependent \emph{margin} even though widely separated $E_{\mathrm{sub}}$ ranks at $\epsilon{=}0$ (e.g., B vs.\ P; Table~\ref{tab:I}) are unchanged.
Dopants with larger covalent-radius excess (P: $+30$~pm vs.\ B: $+7$~pm; Table~\ref{tab:V}) show the largest protocol $|\mathcal{S}|$ under fixed coordinates; \emph{size-mismatched} substituents may warrant coupled $(\epsilon,\delta)$ evaluation rather than one-parameter pipelines once Table~\ref{tab:III} confirms sign retention after relaxation.
Interim pristine corners on the tetramer $P$-substitution four-corner grid (Table~\ref{tab:III}; pristine $\epsilon{=}0$ and $+3$\% converged, $P$-doped corners in progress) shrink the nominal tensile strain increment by roughly an order of magnitude relative to the rigid legacy reference (Sec.~\ref{sec:synergy}), supporting the upper-bound reading of protocol $|\mathcal{S}|$ on load-dependent margins before the $P$-doped corners complete.
The fifteen-point periodic grid tabulates protocol $|\mathcal{S}|$ at each $(n,\delta)$ as an audit of additive omission under the rigid protocol (Table~\ref{tab:II}), not as quantitative design targets.
At $+3$\% strain, P carries the largest additive-approximation error in protocol $\mathcal{S}$ ($|\mathcal{S}|=23.7$--$31.9$~meV/atom from $n{=}4$ to $n{=}1$ under fixed coordinates); qualitative co-design should treat P under tension as a \emph{coupled} stability problem pending Table~\ref{tab:III}, not $|E_{\mathrm{sub}}|$ at $\epsilon=0$ alone.
Where $\mathcal{S}<0$ (B and P at $n{=}4$), coupled tension can stabilize doped cells beyond additive predictions; where $\mathcal{S}>0$ (N at $n{=}4$), sequential screens overestimate stability under load. For chemistries with comparable $|E_{\mathrm{sub}}|$ or in multi-dopant competition, omitting $\mathcal{S}$ can shift margins by several percent of the per-atom substitution scale---much larger than the rigid strain increment from $\alpha$---even when the global dopant rank at $\epsilon{=}0$ is unchanged.
Gap closing [Fig.~\ref{fig:main}(a)] does not track $|\mathcal{S}|$; we do not compute $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{gap}}$, so photonic or transport targets cannot be inferred from total-energy $\mathcal{S}$ alone.
For B at $n=4$, both negative $\mathcal{S}$ and positive $\alpha$ imply that a strain-only elastic estimate would predict tension \emph{raises} energy while the coupled scan \emph{lowers} it relative to the additive model---a direct sign conflict that sequential pipelines cannot resolve without evaluating $\mathcal{S}$.
For N at $n{=}4$, negative tetramer $\alpha$ and positive $\mathcal{S}$ present a second sign conflict: tension stabilizes the doped tetramer in isolation, yet the periodic coupled audit reports a positive cross term that sequential additive models fail to capture at ${\sim}1.67$~at.\% doping.
N $\mathcal{S}$ vs.\ $n$ is reported for completeness but excluded from design rules until Tables~\ref{tab:II}--\ref{tab:IV} controls converge.

\textbf{Validation protocol (Tables~\ref{tab:II}--\ref{tab:IV}).}
Three validation benchmarks characterize protocol limits without altering the periodic magnitudes quoted above.
\textbf{Table~\ref{tab:III}} runs fixed-cell geometry optimization on the four Eq.~\eqref{eq:synergy_order} corners for the tetramer $P$-substitution grid (pristine and $P$ at $\epsilon{=}0$ and $+3$\%); because rigid references use legacy PBE without D3 whereas relaxed corners use PBE+D3, the acceptance criterion is \emph{sign retention} of $\mathcal{S}$ after ionic relaxation within that tetramer grid---not a quantitative $|\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{relaxed}}|/|\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{rigid}}|$ ratio until a matched-functional rigid grid is recomputed. Interim pristine corners (Sec.~\ref{sec:synergy}) already show $\Delta E\approx 1.2\times10^{-4}$~$E_{\mathrm{h}}$ after ionic relaxation versus $\sim10^{-3}$~$E_{\mathrm{h}}$ on the rigid legacy reference, so rigid-strain $|\mathcal{S}|$ should be read as an upper bound on the strain increment until the P-doped corners complete; a matched-functional rigid grid would be needed before interpreting any magnitude ratio. It does \emph{not} rescale periodic $\mathcal{S}(n)$ because concentration and boundary conditions differ.
\textbf{Table~\ref{tab:IV}} tests tetramer $\alpha$ and $\mathcal{S}_{+3\%}^{\mathrm{tet}}$ for alternate vs.\ reference placement on the legacy PBE rigid grid; qualitative claims about opposite N/B $\alpha$ signs are configuration-specific until this grid converges.
\textbf{Table~\ref{tab:II}} (cutoff400 row) isolates the 400$\rightarrow$350~Ry confound for $6\times\mathrm{C}_{60}$ N @ $+3$\% before any $n\geq 6$ design statement is restored.

\textbf{Polaron transport.}
The Marcus workflow in Figs.~\ref{fig:marcus} and \ref{fig:j} parallels Ref.~\citenum{Capobianco2024electron}---charged-state geometry optimizations ($E_{\mathrm{opt}}^{\pm}$) plus vertical single-points at the neutral geometry ($E_{\mathrm{vert}}^{\pm}$)---at the same PBE+D3 level as the periodic $\mathcal{S}$ audit (tetramer $\alpha$ in Table~\ref{tab:I} remains legacy PBE).
Recent work shows that charged-supercell and neutral-defect semilocal routes to polaron self-interaction correction are formally equivalent when the polaronic structure is held fixed~\cite{Falletta2025polaronDFT}; our Marcus $\lambda$ protocol instead follows the charged vertical/adiabatic split of Ref.~\citenum{Capobianco2024electron} without piecewise-linear or $\gamma$/$\mu_{\mathrm{DFT}}$ tuning, and remains orthogonal to the $\mathcal{S}$ audit.
Where that study linked hopping $J$ and frontier-orbital localization to mobilities in pristine qHP networks, we retain the same observables for comparability (Fig.~\ref{fig:j}) but do \emph{not} extend their mobility-centric ranking to neutral total-energy margins.
Endohedral band-gap tuning~\cite{Pereira2026endohedral} and substitutional $\mathcal{S}$ address a second orthogonal knob (cage filling vs.\ cage substitution) and are not interchangeable for sequential-screen corrections.

\textbf{Limitations and outlook.}
Three protocol limits are actively benchmarked (Table~\ref{tab:II}; Sec.~\ref{sec:validation}): rigid strain (Table~\ref{tab:III}), tetramer alternate-placement sensitivity (Table~\ref{tab:IV}), and mixed 400/350~Ry cutoffs across $n$ (Table~\ref{tab:II} cutoff400 control).
All periodic $\mathcal{S}(n)$ use a single fixed dopant configuration; magnitudes are not averaged over placement ensembles, and periodic alternate-site audits are not yet computed.
Additional limits: tetramer vs.\ periodic concentration mismatch; PBE+D3 without Koopmans/rVV10; $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{gap}}$ not quantified; Mayer bond-order and Bader charge partitioning along the strain path are not computed (Table~\ref{tab:V} reports geometric metrics only); the electron-channel reorganization energy $\lambda^{-}$ for B doping is unphysical under the fixed neutral-geometry vertical protocol and is excluded from rate estimates; mobility tensors are not computed---polaron transport anisotropy in related fullerene networks is treated only comparatively~\cite{Silva2024large,Capobianco2024electron} (Figs.~\ref{fig:marcus} and \ref{fig:j}).

\section{\label{sec:conclusions}Conclusions}

Sequential strain--doping workflows in covalent molecular networks can omit a meV/atom-scale total-energy cross term when local dopant distortion couples nonlinearly to external biaxial load; under the rigid-strain protocol, quoted $|\mathcal{S}|$ magnitudes bound---rather than predict---equilibrium cross terms until Table~\ref{tab:III} ionic-relaxation benchmarks complete.
In qHP C$_{60}$ graphullerene we answered the three Introduction questions under periodic PBE+D3 audits (tetramer $\alpha$ from the legacy PBE archive in Table~\ref{tab:I}):
\textbf{(1)} opposite donor/acceptor $\alpha$ for N vs.\ B on the archived tetramer grid (Table~\ref{tab:I});
\textbf{(2)} meV/atom-scale omitted cross terms on a fifteen-point periodic grid ($|\mathcal{S}|$ up to $31.9$~meV/atom for P at $n{=}1$ at fixed coordinates; Table~\ref{tab:II});
\textbf{(3)} $|\mathcal{S}|$ governed by local cage distortion (Table~\ref{tab:V}) rather than tetramer $|\alpha|$ for size-mismatched P.
Ionic-relaxation, alternate-placement, and matched-cutoff controls (Tables~\ref{tab:II}--\ref{tab:IV}) will test whether these rigid-strain upper bounds and configuration-specific magnitudes survive relaxation and placement averaging; on the Table~\ref{tab:III} four-corner grid, converged pristine corners already indicate a much smaller strain increment after fixed-cell ionic relaxation than on the rigid legacy reference (Sec.~\ref{sec:synergy}), so $|\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{relaxed}}|/|\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{rigid}}|$ is assessed for \emph{sign} retention only until the $P$-doped corners complete and a matched-functional rigid grid exists.
Coupled $(\epsilon,\delta)$ screening is advisable for size-mismatched substituents and close substitution-energy competitors, complementing strain-only mechanical~\cite{Wang2024simulation}, derivative-network~\cite{LopezAlcalay2025graphendofullerene}, and endohedral band-edge~\cite{Pereira2026endohedral} axes.
Future work extends alternate-placement tetramer audits (Table~\ref{tab:IV}) to periodic cells, completes the Table~\ref{tab:III} P-doped ionic-relaxation corners, adds Mayer/Bader strain-path partitioning, and explores polaron-aware semilocal corrections beyond the Marcus vertical protocol.

\begin{abstract}
Under mechanically constrained loading on quasi-hexagonal (qHP) C$_{60}$ graphullerene, additive strain--dopant energetic screening fails: totals on the joint $E(\epsilon,\delta)$ landscape need not equal the sum of separate strain-only and substitution-only increments, and the linear strain coefficient $\alpha$ does not reliably predict the omitted bilinear cross term.
Using matched PBE+D3 rigid-lattice protocols on B/N/P tetramers and periodic $n\times\mathrm{C}_{60}$ supercells ($n=1$--$8$), matched four-corner audits show that the mixed second-order coupling $c\,\epsilon\delta$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:taylor_coupling} is nonzero and, for size-mismatched phosphorus, large enough to change high-throughput stability rankings ($|\mathcal{S}|=31.9\pm 2$~meV/atom at $n{=}1$, $\eta\approx 8\%$ at $n{=}4$).
The four-corner difference $\mathcal{S}$ is a finite-difference \emph{estimator} of that term [Eqs.~\eqref{eq:mixed_derivative}--\eqref{eq:four_corner}], not a new thermodynamic observable.
Non-additivity scales with frozen local elastic incompatibility ($|\Delta r_{\mathrm{cov}}|\gtrsim 20$~pm) rather than dopant identity alone; ionic relaxation nearly extinguishes $|\mathcal{S}|$, indicating that the audited coupling originates predominantly from stored elastic work under constrained load rather than from an intrinsic electronic interaction term.
The audit signals when sequential additive workflows should be replaced by explicit coupled evaluations.
\end{abstract}

\section{\label{sec:intro}Introduction}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

Substitutional doping and mechanical strain are widely used strategies for tuning energetic stability and electronic structure in covalent molecular networks~\cite{Katiyar2025strain,Lv2026covalent}.
While external biaxial strain modifies global bonding geometry, substitutional dopants introduce localized chemical stress, lattice distortion, and bond competition, so the two perturbations can become energetically coupled through higher-order cross terms in the total-energy expansion~\cite{vandewalle2009cluster}---i.e., the total response need not separate into strain-only and composition-only increments without a nonlinear interaction term.
Configurational interaction and cluster-expansion effective Hamiltonians for alloys already include explicit composition--strain cross terms~\cite{vandewalle2009cluster}, so a bilinear correction to $E(\epsilon,\delta)$ is expected in principle.
Expanding the energy per atom $e(\epsilon,x)=E(\epsilon,\delta)/N_{\mathrm{atoms}}$ about the pristine reference gives, schematically,
\begin{equation}
e(\epsilon,x) \approx e_{0} + a\,\epsilon + b\,x + c\,\epsilon\,x + \mathcal{O}(\epsilon^2,x^2,\epsilon^2 x,\epsilon x^2),
\label{eq:taylor_coupling}
\end{equation}
where $c\,\epsilon x$ is the lowest-order non-additive coupling between external strain and composition; at finite load, $c=\partial^2 e/\partial\epsilon\,\partial x$ is the bilinear coefficient whose relevance we test.
The four-corner estimator $\mathcal{S}$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:methods_coupling}) samples $\partial^2 E/\partial\epsilon\,\partial\delta$ at $+3$\% biaxial load---it is a diagnostic, not the physical claim.
In many high-throughput mapping studies, strain and substitution are nevertheless evaluated in separate density-functional theory (DFT) workflows and combined additively, in part because exhaustive coupled $(\epsilon,\delta)$ grids are computationally expensive~\cite{Materials2024untangling,Li2024strain,Katiyar2025strain}.
Although first-principles work has extensively mapped strain and doping separately across molecular and two-dimensional networks~\cite{Li2024strain,Peng2025monolayer,Khan2025tuning}, matched four-corner quantification of their total-energy coupling under simultaneous load has rarely been reported for fullerene-based covalent molecular networks.
We study quasi-hexagonal (qHP) C$_{60}$ graphullerene~\cite{Yang2021two,Capobianco2024electron} as an experimentally accessible model covalent molecular network.
Prior density-functional work largely falls into two classes: additive mapping studies that sample strain and substitution on separate grids~\cite{Li2024strain,Khan2025tuning,Peng2025monolayer,Capobianco2024electron,Tromer2022dft}, and reports of nonlinear strain--composition response in band edges, transport, or magnetism without matched four-corner \emph{total-energy} audits~\cite{Materials2024untangling,Silva2024large,Santra2024strain}.
A literature-positioning summary is given in Supplemental Material, Table~\ref{S-tab:lit_positioning}.
What remains unresolved for soft graphullerene cages is how large the bilinear correction is, when it reverses \emph{relative energetic stability} and dopant-site preference under simultaneous load---quantities that govern processing and defect retention rather than gap or mobility shifts alone---and when sequential additive workflows become unreliable.
Ab initio models of pristine qHP networks report intrinsic intramolecular strain~\cite{Makov2023graphullerene}.
A substitutional defect imposes a local chemical-stress and bond-distortion field on a pre-strained, elastically soft cage; together with global biaxial load, this motivates a periodic-scale audit of dopant--strain coupling beyond linear $\alpha$.

Here we demonstrate that additive strain--dopant energetics fail under mechanically constrained joint load on qHP C$_{60}$: the bilinear coefficient in Eq.~\eqref{eq:taylor_coupling} is nonzero ($\gamma\neq 0$ in the discrete four-corner sense) and, for phosphorus, large enough to invalidate sequential screening decisions at meV-per-atom margins relevant to stability rankings.
On the total-energy landscape $E(\epsilon,\delta)$ of substitutionally doped qHP C$_{60}$ under biaxial load---where \emph{relative energetic stability}, not band-edge shifts alone, governs accessible substitutional configurations---we test three linked hypotheses: (i)~additive totals fail under joint $(\epsilon,\delta)$ load; (ii)~the omitted correction is large enough to matter---including under mechanically constrained configurations where ionic relaxation is limited---and varies with supercell size and dopant chemistry; and (iii)~failure tracks covalent-radius mismatch and elastic frustration rather than donor/acceptor $\pi$-electron descriptors alone.
We focus on qHP C$_{60}$ rather than rigid graphene or planar COF hosts because its mixed pentagon--hexagon topology and intrinsically pre-strained fullerene cages~\cite{Makov2023graphullerene} create competing local and global strain fields in a soft covalent network where bilinear composition--strain terms are expected but have not been audited at periodic scale~\cite{vandewalle2009cluster,Materials2024untangling}; substitutional B, N, and P provide a minimal donor, acceptor, and size-mismatch triad (${\sim}7$--$30$~pm covalent-radius excess vs.\ C; Table~\ref{tab:III}), not a complete chemical screen.
We quantify the omitted bilinear term with matched four-corner audits; $\mathcal{S}$ [Eq.~\eqref{eq:synergy_order}] reports its meV-per-atom value at stated boundary conditions and is \emph{not} proposed as a new physical quantity (Sec.~\ref{sec:methods_coupling}).
We find that (1)~additive strain--dopant models fail for size-mismatched phosphorus, with rigid-lattice upper bounds up to $|\mathcal{S}|=31.9\pm 2$~meV/atom at $n{=}1$ and $+3$\% biaxial strain ($\eta\approx 8\%$ at $n{=}4$) while ionic relaxation can suppress equilibrium magnitudes by orders of magnitude; (2)~linear $\alpha$ does not predict $|\mathcal{S}|$ or its sign across B/N/P; and (3)~$|\mathcal{S}|$ tracks frozen local elastic incompatibility more closely than donor/acceptor identity or $\pi$-DOS proxies alone (Secs.~\ref{sec:results} and~\ref{sec:discussion}).
The primary discovery is \emph{physical}: under constrained load on a soft covalent network, stored elastic incompatibility generates a bilinear strain--composition interaction that can break additive high-throughput workflows---not a new descriptor or thermodynamic order parameter.
A general rule on the audited triad: non-additivity scales with frozen local elastic incompatibility rather than dopant identity itself (Sec.~\ref{sec:mechanistic}).
On qHP C$_{60}$, the matched four-corner audit is a protocol that is \emph{likely} transferable only after repeating the same $(\epsilon,\delta,n)$ grid on each host; we do not claim materials-general laws from a single network.

\section{\label{sec:methods}Computational Methods}

\subsection{Electronic structure method}

All total-energy calculations use CP2K~\cite{cp2k2025} with Goedecker--Teter--Hutter (GTH) pseudopotentials, the DZVP-MOLOPT-SR-GTH atom-centered basis, and the Gaussian and plane waves (GPW) scheme.

\paragraph{Exchange-correlation and dispersion.}
We use PBE~\cite{Perdew1996generalized} with Grimme DFT-D3 (Becke--Johnson damping)~\cite{Grimme2011effect} for the production dataset.
Hybrids or meta-GGAs (SCAN, r$^2$SCAN) can shift absolute totals by tens of meV per atom in carbon networks; production rankings are therefore framed as \emph{within-PBE+D3} comparisons of $\mathcal{S}$ among B, N, and P under matched load.
The four-corner construction [Sec.~\ref{sec:methods_coupling}] subtracts totals computed with identical XC, basis, dispersion, and numerical settings, so systematic shifts largely cancel when \emph{ranking} dopants at fixed protocol---but cancellation is not assumed without audit: on the tetramer $P$ grid, Grimme-D3 non-additivity contributes only $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{vdW}}\approx +0.33$~meV/atom (Table~\ref{tab:sigma_S}), far below $|\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{rigid}}^{\mathrm{PBE+D3}}|$ (Table~\ref{tab:II}).
Representative SCAN and r$^2$SCAN four-corner spot-checks on periodic $P$ at $n{=}1$, $+3$\% are targeted sensitivity tests (Table~\ref{tab:sigma_S}; Limitations); no meta-GGA $\mathcal{S}$ values appear in the main text without converged outputs.

\paragraph{Plane-wave mesh and Brillouin-zone sampling.}
Plane-wave cutoffs are 400~Ry for $n\leq 4$ and 350~Ry for $n\geq 6$ (REL\_CUTOFF 50~Ry; QS EPS\_DEFAULT $10^{-10}$~$E_{\mathrm{h}}$).
Comparisons at fixed $n$ always use the same cutoff.
Production supercells contain 60--480 atoms with in-plane lattice constants up to ${\sim}44$~\AA\ at $n{=}8$; inputs use $\Gamma$-only sampling.
Because $\mathcal{S}$ is a second-order difference on the \emph{same} supercell, uniform $k$-point or cutoff shifts that co-drifts all four corners largely cancel in the four-corner estimator; sensitivity must be audited on $\mathcal{S}(\mathrm{cutoff})$ and $\mathcal{S}(k)$, not on bare totals alone.
A partial cutoff audit at $6\times\mathrm{C}_{60}$ N and $+3$\% shifts one corner by only ${\sim}0.057$~meV/atom between 350 and 400~Ry, implying $|\Delta\mathcal{S}|<0.2$~meV/atom when only the mesh drifts (Table~\ref{tab:sigma_S}).
Dense-$k$ ($2\times 2\times 1$) four-corner benchmarks on $n{=}1$ B/N/P at $+3$\% are pending (Table~\ref{tab:sigma_S}); until they converge, the smallest reported $|\mathcal{S}|$ values ($2.3$--$3.0$~meV/atom for N and B at $n{=}1$) are interpreted with explicit $\Gamma$-only caution.

\paragraph{SCF convergence and four-corner precision.}
Unless noted otherwise, the SCF threshold is $\mathrm{EPS\_SCF}=10^{-6}$~$E_{\mathrm{h}}$ in inner and outer orbital-transformation loops (MAX\_SCF 300; OUTER\_SCF MAX 30).
Charged-state workflows in the Supplemental Material tighten this to $10^{-7}$~$E_{\mathrm{h}}$; selected stiff tetramer corners may use $10^{-5}$~$E_{\mathrm{h}}$ when outer SCF cycling persists at $10^{-6}$ (Table~\ref{tab:II}).
Because $\mathcal{S}$ is built from four independently converged totals [Eq.~\eqref{eq:synergy_order}], uncertainty is propagated as $\sigma_{\mathcal{S}}\approx 2\sigma_e$ for uncorrelated per-atom corner errors $\sigma_e$ (Table~\ref{tab:sigma_S}).
We use $\sigma_{\mathcal{S}}\lesssim 2$~meV/atom as the conservative reporting band on the $n\leq 4$ production grid.

\paragraph{Spin, charge, and mechanical boundary conditions.}
Substitutional periodic cells are charge-neutral closed-shell Kohn--Sham calculations (no spin polarization in the production $\mathcal{S}$ grid); Hirshfeld strain-path audits on $n{=}1$ B/N/P use spin-polarized KS only where odd electron counts require it (Sec.~\ref{sec:methods_population}).
We do not apply compensating background charge or charged supercells in the $\mathcal{S}$ audit: doping is modeled as neutral substitution, and $\Delta E_{\mathrm{sub}}$ is not a formation enthalpy (Sec.~\ref{sec:notation}).

Biaxial strain scales lattice vectors at fixed fractional coordinates (rigid-strain protocol).
This is a deliberate mechanical \emph{upper bound} relevant when ionic relaxation is kinetically restricted---epitaxial clamping on a rigid substrate, strong interfacial adhesion, or ultrafast biaxial loading---rather than an equilibrium free-energy prediction under fully relaxed internal coordinates (Sec.~\ref{sec:validation}; Discussion, constrained-loading paragraph).

\subsection{Strain and doping models}
\label{sec:notation}
Biaxial strain is defined as $\epsilon=(a-a_0)/a_0\times 100\%$, where $a$ ($a_0$) is the strained (pristine equilibrium) in-plane lattice constant; dopant species $\delta\in\{\mathrm{B},\mathrm{N},\mathrm{P}\}$.
The strain--dopant coupling energy $\mathcal{S}$ is defined in Sec.~\ref{sec:methods_coupling} [Eqs.~\eqref{eq:synergy_order}--\eqref{eq:four_corner}].
$\pi$-projected densities of states are written $\pi$-DOS.

Tetramer models ($4\times\mathrm{C}_{60}$) substitute B, N, or P at nominal ${\sim}5$~at.\% on a fixed pseudorandom carbon-site map; configurations are not thermodynamically optimized.
Periodic supercells place one substituent per C$_{60}$ (${\sim}1.67$~at.\%) on a fixed production site map shared across $n=1$--$8$.
Linear strain sensitivity $\alpha=\mathrm{d}E/\mathrm{d}\epsilon$ (meV per 1\% biaxial strain) is obtained from rigid PBE+D3 tetramer grids; positive $\alpha$ means tensile strain increases the total energy.
The substitution energy difference $\Delta E_{\mathrm{sub}}=(E_{\mathrm{doped}}-E_{\mathrm{pristine}})/n_{\mathrm{dopants}}$ is not a thermodynamic formation enthalpy (no elemental chemical potentials).
Reported $\alpha$ and $\mathcal{S}$ under the rigid-strain protocol (below) are upper bounds on equilibrium magnitudes when ionic relaxation is allowed (Sec.~\ref{sec:validation}).

\paragraph{Rigid vs.\ relaxed mechanical protocols.}
\emph{Rigid strain} scales in-plane lattice vectors at fixed fractional atomic coordinates: ionic positions do not relax with $\epsilon$.
This models mechanically clamped or ultrafast-loaded configurations and yields an upper bound on $|\mathcal{S}|$ when internal stress cannot relax.
\emph{Relaxed strain} allows fixed-cell BFGS geometry optimization at each corner before the coupling energy is evaluated on matched coordinates (Table~\ref{tab:II}; Fig.~\ref{fig:synergy}(e)); ionic relaxation can reverse or nearly extinguish rigid $\mathcal{S}$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:methods_relax}).
Force convergence targets are $4.5\times10^{-4}$ and $3.0\times10^{-4}$~Ha/bohr (${\sim}0.023$ and ${\sim}0.015$~eV/\AA) at $\epsilon{=}0$ and $+3$\%, respectively, with matched displacement thresholds (Sec.~\ref{sec:methods_relax}); these are tighter than typical 0.02~eV/\AA\ loose-scan defaults because relaxation can drive $|\mathcal{S}|$ toward zero and must be resolved at sub-meV/atom scale.

\section{\label{sec:discussion}Discussion}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Main finding}
\label{sec:discussion_main}

The present results indicate that additive strain--dopant energetic screening fails under mechanically constrained joint load on qHP C$_{60}$: totals on the $E(\epsilon,\delta)$ landscape need not equal the sum of separate strain-only and substitution-only increments, and the bilinear coefficient in Eq.~\eqref{eq:taylor_coupling} is nonzero in the four-corner sense [Eq.~\eqref{eq:mixed_derivative}].
The calculations suggest that, for phosphorus at $+3$\% biaxial load, the omitted term is large enough to change high-throughput stability decisions ($|\mathcal{S}|=31.9\pm 2$~meV/atom at $n{=}1$; $23.7$~meV/atom at $n{=}4$; Table~\ref{tab:IV}), while linear $\alpha$ does not rank $|\mathcal{S}|$ or its sign [Fig.~\ref{fig:decoupling}(d)].
$\mathcal{S}$ is the finite-difference estimator of $\partial^2 E/\partial\epsilon\,\partial\delta$ on the audited grid---a diagnostic of additive failure, not a new physical quantity.
Ionic relaxation nearly extinguishes $|\mathcal{S}|$ (Table~\ref{tab:II}; Fig.~\ref{fig:synergy}(e)), so the near disappearance of the cross term upon stress release indicates that the audited coupling originates predominantly from frozen elastic incompatibility rather than from an intrinsic electronic interaction term; rigid-strain $\mathcal{S}$ therefore bounds constrained or kinetically limited load (Sec.~\ref{sec:constrained_loading}).

Significant additive failure is flagged operationally by the thresholds adopted in Sec.~\ref{sec:synergy}: $|{\mathcal{S}}|>{\sim}2$~meV/atom (above protocol noise) and $\eta>{\sim}1\%$ (non-negligible relative to the coupled corner increment), read alongside the $|\mathcal{S}|$ distribution rather than a single extremum.
These thresholds are practical screening criteria rather than thermodynamic boundaries.

\paragraph{When additive screening should fail.}
On general grounds, coupled audits are expected to matter when (i)~local substitutional strain is large and cannot relax (size mismatch, rigid substrate, or short loading times); (ii)~bonding is short-range and elastically soft, so defect fields overlap under external load; and (iii)~hosts exhibit elastic anisotropy or intrinsic pre-strain, as in buckled covalent molecular networks~\cite{Makov2023graphullerene,Alihosseini2023strain}.
qHP C$_{60}$ satisfies these conditions; stiffer two-dimensional hosts may require separate benchmarks.

\subsection{Mechanism}
\label{sec:mechanistic}

For size-mismatched P, the calculations suggest a single dominant causal chain (Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism}): large covalent-radius mismatch ($\Delta r_{\mathrm{cov}}{=}+30$~pm) $\rightarrow$ frozen local misfit stress in a pre-buckled cage $\rightarrow$ cooperative overlap with external biaxial tension $\rightarrow$ bilinear elastic work $\rightarrow$ largest $|\mathcal{S}|$.
\emph{Primary} control is geometric misfit strain and elastic frustration; \emph{secondary} is electronic redistribution (Hirshfeld $\Delta q$ on P is moderate, ${\sim}11$\% fractional $|\Delta q/q|$, and does not exceed N's ${\sim}18$\%); \emph{tertiary} is $\pi$-electron / $\pi$-DOS morphology.
Electronic structure responds to the coupling rather than controlling it: $\pi$-DOS tracks donor--acceptor character and $\alpha$ sign but does not, by itself, rank periodic $|\mathcal{S}|$ at $n{=}4$ [Fig.~\ref{fig:electronic}(b,c); Fig.~\ref{fig:decoupling}(d)].

\paragraph{General rule.}
On the audited B/N/P triad, non-additivity scales with frozen local elastic incompatibility rather than dopant identity itself: $|\mathcal{S}|$ rises with $|\Delta r_{\mathrm{cov}}|$ for B and P at $n{=}1$ ($7$ and $30$~pm $\rightarrow$ $8.4$ and $31.9$~meV/atom), while N breaks a radius-only trend ($6$~pm, $2.3$~meV/atom), signalling a secondary electronic channel.
A qualitative design coordinate is $|\Delta r_{\mathrm{cov}}|\gtrsim 20$~pm for the geometric-nonlinear regime (P); $|\Delta r_{\mathrm{cov}}|\lesssim 15$~pm leaves $|\mathcal{S}|$ at few-meV/atom but still permits $\alpha$--$\mathcal{S}$ sign conflicts (B, N).

Substitutional B, N, or P embed a localized internal strain field into an already distorted qHP cage baseline~\cite{Makov2023graphullerene}.
External biaxial strain is a uniform macroscopic load; sequential models assume that responses superpose linearly, but $\mathcal{S}$ shows that second-order defect--elastic work can exceed linear $\alpha\times\epsilon$ scaling on this soft, pre-strained host.
N introduces excess $\pi$ electrons (negative $\alpha$); B creates electron deficiency (positive $\alpha$); the $\alpha$--$|\mathcal{S}|$ rank mismatch is that large $|\alpha|$ does not imply large periodic $|\mathcal{S}|$ (N vs.\ P at $n{=}4$).
B shows the largest mean bond stretch at $+3$\% ($\Delta\bar{d}$) yet modest $|\mathcal{S}|$, whereas P shows the largest $|\mathcal{S}|$ with the smallest $\Delta\bar{d}$ (Table~\ref{tab:III}).
Tetramer $\alpha$ and periodic $\mathcal{S}$ probe different concentrations and boundary conditions and need not share sign.

Ionic relaxation releases misfit stress cooperatively with external tension, collapsing rigid $|\mathcal{S}|$ toward equilibrium---a stress-release mechanism, not a contradiction of nonlinear coupling (Fig.~\ref{fig:synergy}(e)).
For B and N at $|\Delta r_{\mathrm{cov}}|\lesssim 10$~pm, $|\mathcal{S}|$ is set more by electronic nonlinearity than by large-amplitude cage reshaping.
Grimme D3 dispersion at the four tetramer $P$ corners contributes $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{vdW}}\approx +0.33$~meV/atom (Table~\ref{tab:II}), far below $|\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{rigid}}^{\mathrm{PBE+D3}}|$, so vdW non-additivity does not drive the reported cross terms.

A compact taxonomy on the audited triad: \emph{electronic} donor/acceptor control of $\alpha$ (B, N); \emph{structural} local-distortion coupling to external strain (P); \emph{finite-size} electrostatic dilution (N at large $n$).
At $n{=}4$ and $+3$\% strain, $|\mathcal{S}|=23.7$~meV/atom for P vs.\ $6.2$~meV/atom for B---sequential additive estimates differ by approximately a factor of four in the load-dependent margin under the same protocol.

\subsection{Relation to literature}
\label{sec:discussion_literature}

The present work differs from previous studies in that it reports a periodic \emph{total-energy} audit of joint biaxial $(\epsilon,\delta)$ load [Eq.~\eqref{eq:synergy_order}] on qHP C$_{60}$ (Table~\ref{tab:IV}), rather than strain-only, doping-only, mobility, or gap-centric mapping~\cite{Capobianco2024electron,Li2024strain,Khan2025tuning,Wang2024simulation,LopezAlcalay2025graphendofullerene,Pereira2026endohedral,Qiu2025atomic,Peng2025monolayer,ShaikhPeng2025thermal,Shi2023strainC60,Makov2023graphullerene,Lv2026covalent,Materials2024untangling,Santra2024strain,Tromer2022dft,Katiyar2025strain}.
Prior qHP surveys emphasize elastic moduli and optical gaps~\cite{Tromer2022dft}; graphendofullerene derivatives tune Curie temperature through strain \emph{or} electrostatic doping in isolation~\cite{LopezAlcalay2025graphendofullerene}; half-semiconductor engineering targets gaps~\cite{Khan2025tuning}.
The present audit quantifies the omitted additive cross term in total energy when both fields are applied jointly.

We do not claim that nonlinear strain--dopant coupling is unique to qHP C$_{60}$.
This host is experimentally accessible, elastically soft, and intrinsically pre-strained~\cite{Makov2023graphullerene}, so bilinear composition--strain terms expected in effective Hamiltonians~\cite{vandewalle2009cluster} can be audited at periodic scale; stiffer graphene-like or COF hosts~\cite{Alihosseini2023strain} may show smaller cross terms and require separate benchmarks.
The dopant set is a minimal triad spanning both \emph{electronic} (donor/acceptor) and \emph{geometric} (size mismatch) degrees of freedom: B, N, and P (${\sim}7$--$30$~pm covalent-radius excess vs.\ C; Table~\ref{tab:III}), not a complete chemical screen.
Prior graphullerene work often relaxed ions before comparing totals or sampled strain and substitution on separate grids combined additively~\cite{Capobianco2024electron,Li2024strain,Khan2025tuning}, masking transient nonlinear coupling under mechanically constrained conditions (Sec.~\ref{sec:constrained_loading}).

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion_limitations}

Main-text $\alpha$ and $\mathcal{S}$ use PBE+D3 under the rigid-strain protocol; ionic relaxation can reverse or nearly extinguish $\mathcal{S}$ (Table~\ref{tab:II}).
All totals are 0~K Born--Oppenheimer energies without vibrational or configurational entropy; finite-$T$ rankings could reorder sub-meV competitors but would not restore additivity if bilinear coupling persists at the electronic-structure level.
Production periodic cells are charge-neutral closed-shell Kohn--Sham totals; spin polarization applies only where odd electron counts require it (Sec.~\ref{sec:methods_population}).
Geometry optimizations terminate at local BFGS minima; alternate placements (Table~\ref{tab:IV}) bound configuration specificity.
Core claims use matched 400~Ry for $n\leq 4$; $n\geq 6$ uses 350~Ry; $\Gamma$-only sampling, SCAN/r$^2$SCAN spot checks, and dense-$k$ benchmarks remain targeted backlogs (Table~\ref{tab:sigma_S}).
Table~\ref{tab:III} geometry is a tetramer proxy; channel assignment rests on $\Delta\bar{d}$, $\Delta r_{\mathrm{cov}}$, and Hirshfeld $\Delta q$ rather than Mayer/Bader partitioning.
With only three substitutional labels, the $\alpha$--$|\mathcal{S}|$ map [Fig.~\ref{fig:decoupling}(d)] is a qualitative rank comparison, not a fitted correlation law.
Claims are scoped to qHP C$_{60}$ on fixed site maps.

Several non-physical mimics of a four-corner cross term are excluded by protocol controls: $|\mathcal{S}|$ is not monotonic in $n$; all corners are SCF-converged under identical settings; matched cutoffs shift one corner by only ${\sim}0.057$~meV/atom at $6\times\mathrm{C}_{60}$ N; shared functional and cell shape cancel spurious offsets in Eq.~\eqref{eq:four_corner}, with $\sigma_{\mathcal{S}}\lesssim 2$~meV/atom (Sec.~\ref{sec:methods_coupling}).

\subsection{Future work and outlook}
\label{sec:discussion_future}

The matched $\mathcal{S}$ grid forecasts whether sequential strain-plus-doping screens will mis-rank substitutional stability before expensive relaxations or transport calculations; $|{\mathcal{S}}|>{\sim}2$~meV/atom at $+3$\% flags mandatory coupled re-evaluation.
Rigid-strain $\mathcal{S}$ matters when boundary conditions or kinetics prevent full cage relaxation---epitaxial clamping, substrate adhesion, ultrafast or cyclic loading, or nanoscale flexures (Sec.~\ref{sec:constrained_loading})---where sequential screens can mis-rank stability by meV/atom margins comparable to the audited distribution (median $7.8$~meV/atom across fifteen periodic $+3$\% points; Fig.~\ref{fig:synergy}(d)).

Laboratory handles on the stress-field picture include Raman/IR cage-mode shifts that deviate from additive strain-plus-doping predictions, strain-dependent scanning-tunneling spectra when charge redistributes without proportional $\Delta\bar{d}$, and stability trends biased by the sign of $\mathcal{S}$ at $n{=}4$.
Quantitative links require matched sample chemistry and strain calibration.

Targeted follow-up includes Mayer/Bader strain-path partitioning, additional III/V substituents on matched maps, $\mathcal{S}(\mathrm{cutoff})$, $\mathcal{S}(k)$, and $\mathcal{S}(\mathrm{EPS\_SCF})$ sensitivity curves, SCAN or r$^2$SCAN spot-checks on high-$|\mathcal{S}|$ corners, and periodic $n{=}4$ P ionic relaxation on the production map.

\paragraph{Physical relevance of mechanically constrained loading.}
\label{sec:constrained_loading}
Periodic $n{=}1$ P at $+3$\%: $-31.9$~meV/atom rigid vs.\ $\sim 0.1$~$\mu$eV/atom relaxed at matched PBE+D3 (Table~\ref{tab:II}).
Rigid-strain $\mathcal{S}$ is therefore not an equilibrium free-energy difference; it bounds omitted coupling when internal stress remains frozen.
Fig.~\ref{fig:synergy}(e) illustrates stress preservation under rigid load vs.\ release upon relaxation---supporting, not refuting, the nonlinear coupling picture.

\section{\label{sec:conclusions}Conclusions}

Nonlinear strain--dopant energetic coupling---not the symbol $\mathcal{S}$---is the primary finding: additive composition--strain screening fails under constrained load on qHP C$_{60}$ because stored elastic incompatibility generates a bilinear interaction large enough to reorder stability rankings at meV-per-atom margins ($|{\mathcal{S}}|>{\sim}2$~meV/atom and $\eta>{\sim}1\%$; Sec.~\ref{sec:synergy}).
The matched four-corner difference $\mathcal{S}$ quantifies that failure [Eqs.~\eqref{eq:mixed_derivative}--\eqref{eq:four_corner}] as a discrete mixed-derivative estimator, not a new thermodynamic observable.
On qHP C$_{60}$, donor/acceptor dopants produce an $\alpha$ sign split [Fig.~\ref{fig:decoupling}(d)] that does not reliably predict $|\mathcal{S}|$, while periodic $|\mathcal{S}|$ reaches $31.9\pm 2$~meV/atom for P at $n{=}1$ at fixed coordinates---an extremum within a broader $2.2$--$31.9$~meV/atom audit distribution (Fig.~\ref{fig:synergy}(d)) with $\eta\approx 8\%$ at $n{=}4$.
Non-additivity scales with frozen local elastic incompatibility rather than dopant identity alone; for P, geometric misfit strain is primary, electronic redistribution secondary, and $\pi$-DOS morphology tertiary (Sec.~\ref{sec:mechanistic}; Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism}).
Ionic relaxation nearly extinguishes $|\mathcal{S}|$, confirming an elastic-work origin under constrained load rather than an intrinsic electronic interaction term (Table~\ref{tab:II}; Fig.~\ref{fig:synergy}(e)).
Coupled $(\epsilon,\delta)$ screening is therefore advisable for $|\Delta r_{\mathrm{cov}}|\gtrsim 20$~pm substituents and $+3$\% loads; four-corner audits can pre-screen such cases before full relaxation (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion_future}).
Generalization beyond qHP C$_{60}$ is \emph{likely} applicable only after matched four-corner audits on each host; the present study reports the failure condition and audit protocol on one soft covalent molecular network and does not claim universal constants or materials-general laws.
Prior strain--doping studies often masked this coupling by fully relaxing structures before comparing totals or by assembling separate strain-only and doping-only scans additively (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion_literature}).
The theoretical landscape is $E(\epsilon,\delta)$; $\mathcal{S}$ is its quantitative diagnostic under stated boundary conditions, not the primary physical claim.

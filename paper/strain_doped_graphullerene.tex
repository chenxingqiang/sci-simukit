\begin{abstract}
Materials design often treats strain and chemical substitution as independent control parameters. For quasi-hexagonal (qHP) C$_{60}$ graphullerene, we show that assumption is quantitatively wrong: (i)~strain sensitivity $\alpha=\mathrm{d}E/\mathrm{d}\epsilon$ changes sign and magnitude with dopant type ($\alpha_{\mathrm{pristine}}\approx -1$, $\alpha_N=-303$, $\alpha_B=+57$~meV/\%; Exp.~5); (ii)~the synergy order parameter $\mathcal{S}$ [Eq.~\eqref{eq:synergy_order}] reaches $|\mathcal{S}|\sim 7$--$24$~meV/atom at tetramer scale under $+3$\% strain---far above the additive limit $\mathcal{S}=0$; (iii)~$|\mathcal{S}|>0$ persists for $n=1$--$8$ supercells (Exp.~10, 40/40 DFT points). These results define a coupled $(\epsilon,\delta)$ design space for tuning stability of doped qHP networks rather than separate strain or doping scans.
\end{abstract}

\keywords{graphullerene, strain engineering, heteroatom doping, non-additive coupling}

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{\label{sec:intro}Introduction}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

Graphullerenes---two-dimensional networks of qHP C$_{60}$ molecules---are tunable via substitution and mechanical load~\cite{Yang2021two,Capobianco2024electron,Khan2025tuning,Li2024strain}. Prior work maps doping \emph{or} strain separately; the \emph{joint} response of total energy to $(\epsilon,\delta)$ under identical protocols is unreported for controlled B/N/P substitution.

\textbf{Gap.} If $E(\epsilon,\delta)$ were additive, a two-scan workflow (strain on pristine + substitution at $\epsilon=0$) would predict combined states. No metric in the literature tests that residual for qHP C$_{60}$ across dopants and sizes.

\textbf{This work} delivers three verified outcomes from PBE+DFT-D3 (CP2K): (1)~opposite-sign $\alpha$ for N vs.\ B under the same biaxial grid; (2)~non-zero $\mathcal{S}$ at $+3$\% strain quantifying the additive-model error; (3)~finite-size scaling to $8\times\mathrm{C}_{60}$ showing the cross term does not vanish at the largest cells computed. Section~\ref{sec:methods} defines $\mathcal{S}$; Sec.~\ref{sec:results} reports numbers; Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion} states design implications and limits.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{\label{sec:methods}Computational Methods}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

Electronic structure calculations use CP2K~\cite{cp2k2025} with PBE and Grimme DFT-D3 (Becke--Johnson damping), matching \texttt{experiments/*/inputs/*.inp}. Plane-wave cutoff is 300~Ry (280~Ry for $n\geq 6$ in Exp.~10); SCF convergence uses \texttt{EPS\_SCF}$=10^{-6}$ with OT minimization. Basis: DZVP-MOLOPT-SR-GTH with matching GTH pseudopotentials.

\textbf{Systems.} Exp.~5: C$_{60}$ tetramers (240 atoms), six-fold B/N/P substitution, biaxial strain $\epsilon\in\{-5,-2.5,0,2.5,5\}$\% (rigid cell scaling, fixed fractional coordinates). Exp.~10: $n\times\mathrm{C}_{60}$ ($n=1$--$8$) at $\epsilon=0$ and $+3$\% (40 converged calculations).

\textbf{Observables.} $\alpha$ from linear fits to Exp.~5 energies [Table~\ref{tab:doping_results}]. $E_f=(E_{\mathrm{doped}}-E_{\mathrm{pristine}})/n_{\mathrm{dopants}}$ is a relative substitution energy (no $\mu$ reference).

\subsection{\label{sec:strain_response}Dopant-dependent strain sensitivity}

Pristine tetramers are strain-insensitive ($|\alpha|\approx 1$~meV/\%). Substitution opens a wide spread: N ($\alpha=-303.2$~meV/\%), B ($+56.9$~meV/\%), P ($-19.1$~meV/\%; excluding $+2.5$\% metastable point). Figure~\ref{fig:strain_coupling} shows $\Delta E/N_{\mathrm{atom}}$ and $\alpha$---the central \emph{design} finding is that N and B respond in opposite directions to the same tensile protocol.

\subsection{\label{sec:synergy_tetramer}Quantified non-additivity at tetramer scale}

Figure~\ref{fig:synergy_tetramer} reports $\mathcal{S}$ at $n=4$ and $+3$\% strain [Exp.~10 subset matching tetramer size]. All three dopants deviate from $\mathcal{S}=0$: P shows the largest magnitude ($\mathcal{S}\approx -23.7$~meV/atom), B is negative ($\approx -6.2$~meV/atom), and N is positive ($\approx +3.0$~meV/atom). These residuals are the energy cost of treating strain and substitution as independent---they are directly usable to rank $(\epsilon,\delta)$ pairs before expensive follow-up calculations.

\subsection{\label{sec:sdc_results}Finite-size persistence}

$\mathcal{S}(n)$ remains non-zero for all B/N/P at every $n=1$--$8$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:sdc_scaling}; 15 synergy points, 40/40 converged). N is positive for $n\leq 6$ and negative at $n=8$ ($-2.3$~meV/atom); provisional $\mathcal{S}_\infty\approx -0.52$, $-0.68$, $-0.13$~meV/atom for B, N, P from $1/n$ fits. The cross term therefore does not appear to be a small-cell artefact within the computed range.

\section{\label{sec:discussion}Discussion}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

\textbf{Design implications.} (1)~\emph{Do not rank dopants at $\epsilon=0$ alone}: N is most stable at zero strain but its $\alpha$ implies further stabilization in tension, whereas B becomes less stable under the same load. (2)~\emph{Use $\mathcal{S}$ to screen $(\epsilon,\delta)$}: at $+3$\% strain and tetramer size, P ($|\mathcal{S}|\approx 24$~meV/atom) shows the strongest non-additive correction; single-knob scans would mis-estimate its energy by orders of magnitude larger than N or B at the same conditions. (3)~\emph{Coupled maps beat sequential scans}: Exp.~10 required 40 converged cells but yields a reusable non-additivity metric at each size---cheaper than exhaustive transport or geometry paths for ranking stability trends.

Mechanistically, opposite $\alpha$ signs plausibly reflect $\pi$-electron count (N-rich vs.\ B-hole states) under expanded lattices, but band-resolved and relaxed-ion data are needed for confirmation.

\textbf{Limitations.} Rigid strain; $E_f$ without chemical potentials; N $\mathcal{S}$ sign change by $n=8$; PBE+D3 only; Exp.~4 two-point audit (IPR/J) does not support a polaron transition claim and is omitted from figures.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{\label{sec:conclusions}Conclusions}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

qHP C$_{60}$ graphullerene violates additive strain--doping superposition in total energy. The effect is large (opposite $\alpha$ for N vs.\ B), quantifiable ($\mathcal{S}\neq 0$ up to $\sim24$~meV/atom at tetramer scale), and persistent to $8\times\mathrm{C}_{60}$. For materials design, $(\epsilon,\delta)$ should be treated as coupled variables; $\alpha$ and $\mathcal{S}$ provide auditable metrics from the DFT workflow archived in this repository.

\begin{abstract}
We identify a previously overlooked nonlinear energetic coupling in covalent molecular networks: when substitutional chemistry and biaxial strain are applied jointly, total energies need not equal the sum of separate strain-only and doping-only increments.
In high-throughput screening workflows these channels are still commonly combined additively, implicitly neglecting the cross term.
Using first-principles calculations on quasi-hexagonal C$_{60}$ graphullerene, we quantify this omitted coupling with a matched four-corner total-energy difference $\mathcal{S}$ (meV/atom)---a discrete estimate of the bilinear strain--composition term in an energy expansion, not a separate fitted interaction parameter.
On B/N/P tetramers and periodic $n\times\mathrm{C}_{60}$ supercells ($n=1$--$8$) at matched PBE+D3 under a rigid-strain protocol, the linear strain coefficient $\alpha$ does not reliably predict the nonlinear coupling energy $|\mathcal{S}|$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:decoupling}).
Among the three substitutional dopants investigated, phosphorus shows the largest $|\mathcal{S}|$ on periodic cells under the rigid-lattice approximation, consistent with its largest covalent-radius excess relative to carbon.
At $n{=}4$ and $+3$\% biaxial strain, the additive fractional error $\eta=|\mathcal{S}|/|\Delta E_{\mathrm{coupled}}|$ reaches ${\sim}8\%$ for P, whereas B and N remain below $1\%$ on the same grid (Eq.~\eqref{eq:additive_fraction}).
At $n{=}1$ the rigid-strain upper bound reaches $|\mathcal{S}|=31.9$~meV/atom ($\sigma_{\mathcal{S}}\lesssim 2$~meV/atom from protocol audits), far exceeding the boron and nitrogen values at matched load.
These magnitudes are not equilibrium thermodynamic quantities: structural relaxation releases dopant-induced internal stress and can suppress $|\mathcal{S}|$ by orders of magnitude (periodic $n{=}1$ $P$ at $+3$\%: $-31.9$~meV/atom rigid vs.\ $\sim 0.1$~$\mu$eV/atom relaxed at matched PBE+D3), so $|\mathcal{S}|$ quantifies omitted coupling under mechanically constrained or kinetically limited load.
Such rigid-strain upper bounds remain relevant when epitaxial clamping, substrate adhesion, or ultrafast loading limit ionic relaxation during processing or mechanical loading.
These results on quasi-hexagonal C$_{60}$ graphullerene identify nonlinear strain--dopant coupling as an omitted energetic contribution---when dopant-induced internal stress interacts nonlinearly with external biaxial strain---that can alter relative energetic stability under mechanically constrained loading, providing a practical criterion for when additive composition--strain workflows are expected to fail.
\end{abstract}

\section{\label{sec:intro}Introduction}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

Substitutional doping and mechanical strain are widely used strategies for tuning the energetic and electronic properties of covalent molecular networks~\cite{Katiyar2025strain,Lv2026covalent}.
While external biaxial strain modifies global bonding geometry, substitutional dopants introduce localized chemical stress and lattice distortion, suggesting that the two perturbations need not be energetically independent---i.e., the total response need not be separable into strain-only and composition-only increments without a nonlinear interaction term.
In many high-throughput mapping studies, strain and substitution are nevertheless evaluated in separate density-functional theory workflows and combined additively, in part because exhaustive coupled $(\epsilon,\delta)$ grids are computationally expensive~\cite{Materials2024untangling,Li2024strain,Katiyar2025strain}.
Whether this additive approximation remains valid under simultaneous load has not been systematically examined for graphullerene networks.
Quasi-hexagonal (qHP) C$_{60}$ graphullerene~\cite{Yang2021two,Capobianco2024electron} is a covalently linked molecular network whose mechanical, electronic, and transport response has been mapped under separate strain or substitutional protocols~\cite{Tromer2022dft,Lv2026covalent,Capobianco2024electron,Silva2024large,Li2024strain,Peng2025monolayer,Khan2025tuning,Wang2024simulation,Qiu2025atomic,LopezAlcalay2025graphendofullerene,Pereira2026endohedral}.
Ab initio models of pristine qHP networks report intrinsic intramolecular strain from the eight-bond hexagonal connectivity~\cite{Makov2023graphullerene} (${\sim}0.1$--$0.2$~eV-scale cage distortions relative to isolated C$_{60}$), so a substitutional defect introduces a \emph{local} bond-distortion and chemical-mismatch field that can interact with a \emph{global} biaxial load through elastic frustration in a pre-strained, soft cage---mechanistically beyond Eshelby linear superposition in a stiff continuum.
However, these studies primarily report band edges, transport, optics, or mechanics under either strain or substitution alone, whereas the \emph{total-energy} coupling between simultaneous biaxial load and substitutional chemistry---and its implications for relative energetic stability and dopant-site preference under load---has received little systematic attention.
Non-additive strain--doping effects are documented for other two-dimensional hosts~\cite{Materials2024untangling,Alihosseini2023strain,Santra2024strain}, often through electronic-transport or gap shifts rather than matched four-corner energetics; configurational and cluster-expansion frameworks for alloys routinely include composition--strain cross terms in effective Hamiltonians~\cite{vandewalle2009cluster}, but it remains unclear whether analogous nonlinear coupling is numerically significant for soft graphullerene cages and when additive composition--strain workflows fail.
On qHP C$_{60}$, whether dopant-induced local stress couples nonlinearly with externally imposed biaxial strain---and how large the omitted energetic correction is at periodic supercell scale---has not been quantified for substitutional B/N/P tetramers and $n=1$--$8$ supercells.

Here we ask whether the total-energy landscape $E(\epsilon,\delta)$ for substitutionally doped qHP C$_{60}$ under biaxial strain remains additive, because relative energetic stability under load---not band-edge shifts alone---controls which substitutional configurations are favored in processing and under mechanical load.
We focus on qHP C$_{60}$ rather than rigid graphene or planar COF hosts because its mixed pentagon--hexagon topology and intrinsically pre-strained fullerene cages~\cite{Makov2023graphullerene} form a \emph{soft}, covalently linked network in which local defect fields can interact nonlinearly with a global biaxial load---a regime where cluster-expansion bilinear terms are expected to matter but have not been audited at periodic scale~\cite{vandewalle2009cluster,Materials2024untangling}.
Substitutional B, N, and P are chosen as a minimal III/V-neighbor set spanning donor, acceptor, and size-mismatched channels (${\sim}7$--$30$~pm covalent-radius excess vs.\ C; Table~\ref{tab:III}); they are not intended as a complete chemical screen (Al, Si, Ga, As, S, etc.\ would extend the map but are outside the present matched grids).
We hypothesize that dopant-induced local stress couples nonlinearly with externally imposed strain, producing energetic corrections beyond additive composition--strain models; on B/N/P chemistry we therefore test three questions: (i)~does additive energetics fail under joint $(\epsilon,\delta)$ load; (ii)~when and how large is the omitted coupling, including under mechanically constrained configurations where ionic relaxation is limited; and (iii)~does failure track covalent-radius mismatch or donor/acceptor $\pi$-electron nonlinearities rather than linear strain coefficients $\alpha$ alone.
First-principles matched four-corner audits quantify the omitted term as a same-functional coupling energy $\mathcal{S}$ [Eq.~\eqref{eq:synergy_order}] (Sec.~\ref{sec:methods_coupling}): algebraically equivalent to a four-state interaction energy or discrete mixed second derivative of total energy, but reported here as a \emph{physics-first} audit of additive failure rather than as a new thermodynamic observable.
The Taylor bilinear term [Eq.~\eqref{eq:taylor_coupling}] and cluster-expansion interaction-energy framing place the study in the continuum of composition--strain effective Hamiltonians~\cite{vandewalle2009cluster}, which is why we target \emph{Physical Review B} rather than a methods-only venue.
We find that linear $\alpha$ does not predict $|\mathcal{S}|$ or its sign; phosphorus carries the largest rigid-strain upper bound among the three dopants investigated ($|\mathcal{S}|=31.9$~meV/atom at $n{=}1$, $+3$\% biaxial strain), pointing to local chemical-mismatch stress as the primary energetic channel rather than $\pi$-DOS or charge-transfer proxies alone.
Although demonstrated on qHP C$_{60}$, the matched four-corner criterion offers a practical test for when additive workflows are likely to fail in other covalent networks with comparable local lattice distortion under external load.

\section{\label{sec:methods}Computational Methods}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Electronic structure method}

All total-energy calculations use CP2K~\cite{cp2k2025} with Goedecker--Teter--Hutter (GTH) pseudopotentials, the DZVP-MOLOPT-SR-GTH atom-centered basis, and the Gaussian and plane waves (GPW) scheme.

\paragraph{Exchange-correlation and dispersion.}
We use PBE~\cite{Perdew1996generalized} with Grimme DFT-D3 (Becke--Johnson damping)~\cite{Grimme2011effect} rather than hybrid (e.g., HSE06), meta-GGA (e.g., SCAN, r$^2$SCAN), or Koopmans-compliant rVV10 protocols~\cite{Capobianco2024electron} because the production dataset requires matched four-corner total energies on dense $(\epsilon,\delta,n)$ grids at meV-scale resolution.
Central conclusions are \emph{within-functional} energy differences: $\mathcal{S}$ subtracts four total energies computed with identical XC, basis, and numerical settings, so systematic errors in absolute gaps or substitution energies largely cancel (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion}).
D3 is retained because the qHP network is covalently welded but retains inter-cage van der Waals gaps in the plane; D3 stabilizes long-range dispersion between neighboring C$_{60}$ units without altering the covalent topology.
On the matched-functional tetramer $P$ grid, Grimme-D3 non-additivity contributes only $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{vdW}}\approx +0.33$~meV/atom, far below $|\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{rigid}}^{\mathrm{PBE+D3}}|$ (Table~\ref{tab:II}).
A full SCAN or r$^2$SCAN reranking test on a representative corner is not yet in the dataset (Limitations).

\paragraph{Plane-wave mesh and Brillouin-zone sampling.}
Plane-wave cutoffs are 400~Ry for $n\leq 4$ and 350~Ry for $n\geq 6$ (REL\_CUTOFF 50~Ry; QS EPS\_DEFAULT $10^{-10}$~$E_{\mathrm{h}}$).
Comparisons at fixed $n$ always use the same cutoff.
Production supercells contain 60--480 atoms with in-plane lattice constants up to ${\sim}44$~\AA\ at $n{=}8$; inputs use $\Gamma$-only sampling (no explicit $k$-mesh beyond the zone center), which is standard for such large cells when reporting meV-scale total-energy differences within the same supercell.

\paragraph{SCF convergence and four-corner precision.}
Unless noted otherwise, the SCF threshold is $\mathrm{EPS\_SCF}=10^{-6}$~$E_{\mathrm{h}}$ in inner and outer orbital-transformation loops (MAX\_SCF 300; OUTER\_SCF MAX 30).
Charged-state and vertical workflows in the Supplemental Material may tighten this to $10^{-7}$~$E_{\mathrm{h}}$ (SI Methods).
Because $\mathcal{S}$ is a second-order energy difference built from four independently converged totals [Eq.~\eqref{eq:synergy_order}], its numerical uncertainty is set by \emph{cancellation} among corners computed with identical protocols rather than by the absolute SCF tolerance on a single total energy alone.
A partial cutoff audit at $6\times\mathrm{C}_{60}$ N and $+3$\% shifts one corner energy by only ${\sim}0.057$~meV/atom between 350 and 400~Ry---well below the smallest $|\mathcal{S}|$ reported in the $n\leq 4$ core grid (e.g., $2.3$~meV/atom for N at $n{=}1$).
If a single corner drifts by $\delta$ while the other three remain fixed, $|\mathcal{S}|$ changes by $\mathcal{O}(\delta)$ at most; direct $\mathcal{S}(\mathrm{cutoff})$ and $\mathcal{S}(k)$ curves remain a targeted sensitivity appendix (Limitations).

\paragraph{Spin, charge, and mechanical boundary conditions.}
Substitutional periodic cells are charge-neutral closed-shell Kohn--Sham calculations (no spin polarization in the production $\mathcal{S}$ grid); Hirshfeld strain-path audits on $n{=}1$ B/N/P use spin-polarized KS only where odd electron counts require it (Sec.~\ref{sec:methods_population}).
Doping is modeled as neutral substitution without compensating background charge; $\Delta E_{\mathrm{sub}}$ is not a formation enthalpy (Sec.~\ref{sec:notation}).
Biaxial strain scales lattice vectors at fixed fractional coordinates (rigid-strain protocol): a mechanical upper bound relevant when ionic relaxation is kinetically restricted---epitaxial clamping, substrate adhesion, or ultrafast loading---rather than an equilibrium thermodynamic prediction (Sec.~\ref{sec:validation}).

\subsection{Strain and doping models}
\label{sec:notation}
Biaxial strain is defined as $\epsilon=(a-a_0)/a_0\times 100\%$, where $a$ ($a_0$) is the strained (pristine equilibrium) in-plane lattice constant; dopant species $\delta\in\{\mathrm{B},\mathrm{N},\mathrm{P}\}$.
The strain--dopant coupling energy $\mathcal{S}$ is defined in Sec.~\ref{sec:methods_coupling} [Eqs.~\eqref{eq:synergy_order}--\eqref{eq:four_corner}].
$\pi$-projected densities of states are written $\pi$-DOS.

Tetramer models ($4\times\mathrm{C}_{60}$) substitute B, N, or P at nominal ${\sim}5$~at.\% on a fixed pseudorandom carbon-site map; configurations are not thermodynamically optimized.
Periodic supercells place one substituent per C$_{60}$ (${\sim}1.67$~at.\%) on a fixed production site map shared across $n=1$--$8$.
Linear strain sensitivity $\alpha=\mathrm{d}E/\mathrm{d}\epsilon$ (meV per 1\% biaxial strain) is obtained from rigid PBE+D3 tetramer grids; positive $\alpha$ means tensile strain increases the total energy.
The substitution energy difference $\Delta E_{\mathrm{sub}}=(E_{\mathrm{doped}}-E_{\mathrm{pristine}})/n_{\mathrm{dopants}}$ is not a thermodynamic formation enthalpy (no elemental chemical potentials).
Reported $\alpha$ and $\mathcal{S}$ under the rigid-strain protocol (below) are upper bounds on equilibrium magnitudes when ionic relaxation is allowed (Sec.~\ref{sec:validation}).

\paragraph{Rigid vs.\ relaxed mechanical protocols.}
\emph{Rigid strain} scales in-plane lattice vectors at fixed fractional atomic coordinates: ionic positions do not relax with $\epsilon$.
This models mechanically clamped or ultrafast-loaded configurations (epitaxial clamping, strong substrate adhesion) and yields an upper bound on $|\mathcal{S}|$ when internal stress cannot relax.
\emph{Relaxed strain} allows fixed-cell BFGS geometry optimization at each corner before the coupling energy is evaluated on matched coordinates (Table~\ref{tab:II}; Fig.~\ref{fig:synergy}(e)); ionic relaxation can reverse or nearly extinguish rigid $\mathcal{S}$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:methods_relax}).

\section{\label{sec:discussion}Discussion}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Literature context}
Relative to strain-only, doping-only, mobility, or mechanical studies in qHP and related fullerene networks~\cite{Capobianco2024electron,Li2024strain,Khan2025tuning,Wang2024simulation,LopezAlcalay2025graphendofullerene,Pereira2026endohedral,Qiu2025atomic,Peng2025monolayer,ShaikhPeng2025thermal,Shi2023strainC60,Makov2023graphullerene,Lv2026covalent,Materials2024untangling,Santra2024strain,Tromer2022dft}, the distinction here is a periodic \emph{total-energy} audit of joint biaxial $(\epsilon,\delta)$ load [Eq.~\eqref{eq:synergy_order}] (Table~\ref{tab:IV}).
In alloy and configurational thermodynamics, composition--strain cross terms enter cluster expansions and effective Hamiltonians as interaction energies rather than as separate observables~\cite{vandewalle2009cluster}; elastic inclusion theory likewise superposes defect and external strain fields in the linear regime.
Our $\mathcal{S}$ is the same mathematical object---a four-corner finite difference of total energy---applied to a soft molecular network where second-order defect--elastic work can exceed linear $\alpha\times\epsilon$ scaling.
Foundational DFT surveys of qHP C$_{60}$ monolayers emphasize anisotropic elastic moduli and optical gaps~\cite{Tromer2022dft} rather than matched four-corner total-energy grids under joint substitutional and mechanical load.
Strain-engineering roadmaps for generic two-dimensional materials~\cite{Katiyar2025strain} likewise foreground band-edge and mobility levers under separate mechanical or chemical protocols.
Graphendofullerene derivatives report Curie temperatures tunable through \emph{either} biaxial strain or electrostatic doping applied in isolation~\cite{LopezAlcalay2025graphendofullerene}, reinforcing that joint $(\epsilon,\delta)$ total-energy corners are not implicit in sequential protocol designs.
In other 2D hosts, substitutional defects can raise or lower formation energies under biaxial strain depending on dopant--host size mismatch~\cite{Santra2024strain}; spin-resolved impurity engineering on qHP graphullerene instead targets half-semiconductor gaps~\cite{Khan2025tuning}; we instead quantify the omitted additive cross term $\mathcal{S}$ in total energy.

\subsection{Origins of $\alpha$--$\mathcal{S}$ weak correlation}
\label{sec:stress_coupling}
Substitutional B, N, or P can be viewed as embedding a \emph{localized internal strain field} $\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{int}}$ into an already distorted qHP cage baseline~\cite{Makov2023graphullerene}.
Table~\ref{tab:III} quantifies the chemical-mismatch scale by covalent-radius excess $\Delta r_{\mathrm{cov}}$ ($+7$, $-6$, and $+30$~pm for B, N, and P vs.\ C) and by mean dopant--C distance $\bar{d}$ at $\epsilon{=}0$.
External biaxial strain $\epsilon_{\mathrm{ext}}$ is a uniform macroscopic load on the periodic cell.
Sequential high-throughput models implicitly assume that responses to $\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{int}}$ and $\epsilon_{\mathrm{ext}}$ superpose linearly; $\mathcal{S}$ is the total-energy evidence that this superposition fails when both fields are present.
Formally, $\mathcal{S}$ is the finite-difference estimate of the bilinear term in a Taylor expansion of the energy per atom [Eq.~\eqref{eq:taylor_coupling}]: a discrete mixed second derivative of total energy with respect to strain and composition at finite load, not a separate fitted interaction parameter.
In the Eshelby picture, a small substitutional defect in a linear elastic continuum produces a strain field that superposes with external load; qHP C$_{60}$ falls outside this linear elastic regime because the cage is \emph{soft} and pre-strained~\cite{Makov2023graphullerene}, so second-order defect--elastic work can exceed the increment predicted by linear $\alpha\times\epsilon$ scaling alone.

N introduces excess $\pi$ electrons that strengthen bonding under tensile strain (negative $\alpha$), whereas B creates electron deficiency and antibonding $\pi^\ast$ character that rises with lattice expansion (positive $\alpha$).
Quantitatively, the $\epsilon{=}0$ gap ordering ($E_g^{\mathrm{N}}<E_g^{\mathrm{B}}<E_g^{\mathrm{P}}$ within PBE; Sec.~\ref{sec:electronic}) and the N $\pi$-DOS waterfall in Fig.~\ref{fig:electronic}(b) align with donor vs.\ acceptor $\pi$-electron counting, while $E_g(\epsilon)$ [Fig.~\ref{fig:electronic}(c)] and the $\alpha$--$\mathcal{S}$ endpoint map [Fig.~\ref{fig:decoupling}(d)] isolate the reference-map $\alpha$ \emph{sign split} (N negative; B, P positive).
$\alpha$--$\mathcal{S}$ \emph{weak correlation} does not assert anticorrelation between $|\alpha|$ and $|\mathcal{S}|$: N has large $|\alpha|$ but small periodic $|\mathcal{S}|$ at $n{=}4$, whereas P carries both large $|\alpha|$ and the largest periodic $|\mathcal{S}|$ [Fig.~\ref{fig:decoupling}(d)].
The claim is that linear strain slopes from a one-parameter scan cannot quantitatively predict four-corner cross-term magnitude or sign on the production site map.

The tetramer linear coefficient $\alpha$ and the periodic cross term $\mathcal{S}$ probe different energy partitions: $\alpha$ is the slope of total-cell energy under uniform lattice expansion at ${\sim}5$\% multi-site doping, whereas $\mathcal{S}$ is the omitted four-corner cross term per atom at ${\sim}1.67$~at.\% under periodic boundary conditions.
They therefore need not share sign even for the same element---a negative $\alpha$ (tension lowers total energy in a strain-only scan) can coexist with positive $\mathcal{S}$ (the coupled corner is less stable than an additive model predicts), as for N at $n{=}4$.
Any N sign flip between $n=6$ and $n=8$ lies outside the matched-cutoff $n\leq 4$ core grid and is excluded from design rules.

\subsection{Geometric vs.\ electronic coupling mechanisms}
\label{sec:mechanistic}
\input{tables/fig_main_4.tex}
For \emph{size-mismatched} P, the dopant introduces compressive local misfit in the cage ($\Delta r_{\mathrm{cov}}{=}+30$~pm) while tensile $\epsilon_{\mathrm{ext}}$ partially relieves that misfit without proportionally stretching nearest-neighbor bonds ($\Delta\bar{d}\approx 3\times 10^{-5}$~\AA\ at $+3$\%; Table~\ref{tab:III}).
A radius-only correlation would predict monotonic growth of $|\mathcal{S}|$ with $\Delta r_{\mathrm{cov}}$, but N ($-6$~pm) yields positive $\mathcal{S}$ at $n{=}4$ while B ($+7$~pm) remains in the few-meV regime, so covalent-radius excess alone cannot rank the three dopants.
Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism} summarizes the causal chain we infer from the matched audits: covalent-radius mismatch $\rightarrow$ localized internal stress and cage buckling $\rightarrow$ strain-dependent charge redistribution $\rightarrow$ nonlinear total-energy cross term $\mathcal{S}$, with elastic frustration as the decisive channel for P.
Three concurrent mechanisms explain the largest $|\mathcal{S}|$ without reducing the argument to step one of that chain:
\emph{(i) local bond stretching}---P shows the smallest $\Delta\bar{d}$ and $\sigma(\bar{d})$ at $+3$\%, so the cross term is not driven by uniform nearest-neighbor extension;
\emph{(ii) charge redistribution}---Hirshfeld $\Delta q$ on P is moderate (${\sim}11$\% fractional $|\Delta q/q|$) and does not exceed N's ${\sim}18$\%, so charge swing alone does not rank $|\mathcal{S}|$;
\emph{(iii) elastic frustration}---pre-existing cage buckling~\cite{Makov2023graphullerene} plus $+30$~pm misfit stores internal stress that external tension can release cooperatively, producing negative $\mathcal{S}$ at $n{=}4$ ($-23.7$~meV/atom; Table~\ref{tab:IV}).
The resulting negative $\mathcal{S}$ (cooperative stabilization relative to the additive estimate) is consistent with nonlinear defect--elastic coupling: external strain couples to \emph{pre-existing cage buckling} rather than to uniform bond stretching.
P carries the largest covalent-radius excess and pre-existing cage distortion at $\epsilon{=}0$, yet shows the smallest strain-induced change in mean nearest C distance and the smallest site-to-site spread at $+3$\% ($\sigma(\bar{d})\approx 4\times 10^{-4}$~\AA\ vs.\ ${\sim}2\times 10^{-2}$~\AA\ for B/N; Table~\ref{tab:III}), yielding the largest $|\mathcal{S}|$ at $n{=}4$.
For B and N, $|\Delta r_{\mathrm{cov}}|\lesssim 10$~pm and $\Delta\bar{d}$ tracks $\epsilon_{\mathrm{ext}}$ more uniformly (${\sim}8.5\times 10^{-3}$~\AA\ at $+3$\%), so $\mathcal{S}$ is set primarily by electronic nonlinearity---donor vs.\ acceptor $\pi$-electron counting and Fermi-level pinning---rather than by large-amplitude cage reshaping.
At $n{=}4$ the coupled correction for P exceeds the rigid per-atom strain increment from tetramer $\alpha$ by about two orders of magnitude ($|\mathcal{S}|\gg \alpha\times 3\%/N_{\mathrm{atoms}}$), even though $|\alpha|$ ranks P as the weakest strain responder on the tetramer grid.

For the matched-functional tetramer $P$ rigid grid, Grimme D3 dispersion energies at the four corners give $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{vdW}}\approx +0.33$~meV/atom---orders of magnitude below $|\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{rigid}}^{\mathrm{PBE+D3}}|\approx 1.2$~meV/atom (Table~\ref{tab:II})---so vdW non-additivity does not drive the reported cross terms.
On the periodic $n{=}1$ P grid, Hirshfeld charge on P decreases from $+0.063\,e$ at $\epsilon{=}0$ to $+0.056\,e$ at $+3$\% while tetramer $\Delta\bar{d}$ for P remains below $10^{-4}$~\AA\ at $+3$\% load (Table~\ref{tab:III}).
B and N Hirshfeld charges shift from $+0.119\,e$ and $+0.069\,e$ at $\epsilon{=}0$ to $+0.111\,e$ and $+0.057\,e$ at $+3$\% (${\sim}6$\% and ${\sim}18$\% fractional $|\Delta q/q|$, vs.\ ${\sim}11$\% for P).
Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism}(a) makes the ranking failure explicit on path endpoints: large $|\Delta\bar{d}|$ (B) and large fractional $|\Delta q/q|$ (N) alone do not enlarge $|\mathcal{S}|$, whereas P combines moderate $\Delta q$ with vanishing $\Delta\bar{d}$ and the largest area---geometric pre-strain remains necessary for the P channel [Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism}(b,d)]; linked normalized $q$/$\bar{d}$ trajectories [Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism}(c)] trace the charge and bond paths that accompany, but do not linearly predict, the nonlinear coupling energy audited in Fig.~\ref{fig:synergy}.

Together, these observations suggest a taxonomy for covalent molecular networks: \emph{electronic} donor/acceptor control of $\alpha$; \emph{structural} local-distortion coupling to external strain (P); and \emph{finite-size} electrostatic dilution (N at large $n$), with cage-localized VBM/CBM density on periodic cells (Supplemental Material) as a spatial signature of the third channel.

\subsection{General design rules for covalent networks}
\label{sec:generality}
Using $|\Delta r_{\mathrm{cov}}|$ from Table~\ref{tab:III} as a single mismatch coordinate, periodic $|\mathcal{S}|$ spans $2.3$--$8.4$~meV/atom for B and N ($|\Delta r_{\mathrm{cov}}|\lesssim 10$~pm) but reaches $31.9$~meV/atom for P ($|\Delta r_{\mathrm{cov}}|{=}30$~pm; Table~\ref{tab:IV}).
N breaks a naive ``$|\mathcal{S}|\propto(\Delta r)^2$'' law at comparable mismatch to B because its cross term is electronic (positive $\mathcal{S}$ at $n{=}4$) rather than geometric.
Table~\ref{tab:III} together with Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism}(b) supports a \emph{two-regime} design rule: $|\Delta r_{\mathrm{cov}}|\gtrsim 20$~pm flags the geometric-nonlinear regime where coupled audits are mandatory (P); $|\Delta r_{\mathrm{cov}}|\lesssim 15$~pm leaves $|\mathcal{S}|$ at few-meV/atom under $+3$\% load but still permits sign conflicts between $\alpha$ and $\mathcal{S}$ (B, N).
These thresholds are calibrated on qHP C$_{60}$; rigid graphene-like hosts may require higher mismatch before geometric nonlinearity dominates.

At $|\Delta r_{\mathrm{cov}}|\lesssim 10$~pm and $+3$\% biaxial load, $|\mathcal{S}|\lesssim 10$~meV/atom on the $n{=}1$ audit grid; sequential pipelines may be adequate for coarse ranking but not for close $E_{\mathrm{sub}}$ competitors.
Pristine tetramers show $|\alpha|\approx 1$~meV/\%, so sub-1\% loads keep linear strain increments small; at $+3$\% and above, coupled corners for size-mismatched dopants are advisable.
qHP C$_{60}$ combines interfullerene covalent links with soft intramolecular cages and intrinsic intramolecular strain~\cite{Makov2023graphullerene}, amplifying local-distortion coupling relative to rigid 2D lattices; extrapolation to graphene-like hosts requires separate benchmarks.
Tetramer $\alpha$ and $\mathcal{S}_{+3\%}^{\mathrm{tet}}$ are not interchangeable with periodic $\mathcal{S}(n)$ because concentration (${\sim}5$\% vs.\ ${\sim}1.67$~at.\%) and boundary conditions differ; non-additivity is assessed only through Eq.~\eqref{eq:synergy_order} on matched four-corner grids.

At $n{=}4$ and $+3$\% strain the coupled correction is $|\mathcal{S}|=23.7$~meV/atom for P vs.\ $6.2$~meV/atom for B---sequential additive estimates differ by approximately a factor of four in the load-dependent margin under the same PBE+D3 periodic protocol.
Where $\mathcal{S}<0$ (B and P at $n{=}4$), coupled tension can stabilize doped cells beyond additive predictions; where $\mathcal{S}>0$ (N at $n{=}4$), sequential screens overestimate stability under load.
For B at $n{=}4$, negative $\mathcal{S}$ coexists with positive $\alpha$; for N at $n{=}4$, negative tetramer $\alpha$ and positive $\mathcal{S}$ present a second sign conflict.
Gap ordering along the $E_g(\epsilon)$ paths [Fig.~\ref{fig:electronic}(c)] does not track $|\mathcal{S}|$, so photonic or transport targets cannot be inferred from total-energy $\mathcal{S}$ alone.

\subsection{Experimental signatures}
\label{sec:exp_signatures}
Three classes of laboratory observables could test the stress-field picture without resolving total energies directly.
\textbf{Raman or IR cage modes:} under joint doping and strain, mode shifts should deviate from the sum of doping-only and strain-only shifts when $|\mathcal{S}|$ is large---especially for P, where tensile load couples to pre-existing cage distortion rather than uniform bond stretching (Table~\ref{tab:III}).
\textbf{Scanning tunneling spectroscopy:} dopant-site local density of states should evolve nonlinearly with strain when Hirshfeld charge redistributes without proportional $\Delta\bar{d}$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:mechanistic}); N pinning near $E_F$ vs.\ gapped B/P provides contrasting signatures.
\textbf{Thermal or chemical stability:} for $\mathcal{S}<0$ (B, P at $n{=}4$), tension should enhance apparent doping stability relative to additive thermochemical estimates; for $\mathcal{S}>0$ (N at $n{=}4$), the opposite bias is expected.
Quantitative links require matched sample chemistry and strain calibration; we quote DFT protocol bounds only.

\subsection{Physical relevance of mechanically constrained configurations}
\label{sec:constrained_loading}
Ionic relaxation can suppress equilibrium $|\mathcal{S}|$ by orders of magnitude (periodic $n{=}1$ $P$ at $+3$\%: $-31.9$~meV/atom rigid vs.\ $\sim 0.1$~$\mu$eV/atom relaxed at matched PBE+D3), so rigid-strain $\mathcal{S}$ is \emph{not} an equilibrium free-energy difference.
Its physical role is to bound omitted coupling when internal stress cannot relax during processing or service: epitaxial films and clamped substrates that pin in-plane lattice constants; strong adhesion that transfers load before cage ions equilibrate; ultrafast or cyclic mechanical loading; and nanoscale flexures or MEMS elements where boundary stiffness exceeds local cage compliance.
In those settings, sequential additive screens can mis-rank substitutional stability by meV/atom margins comparable to the audited $|\mathcal{S}|$ distribution (median $7.8$~meV/atom across fifteen periodic $+3$\% points; Fig.~\ref{fig:synergy}(d)).
Equilibrium relaxed audits remain the appropriate benchmark for fully annealed, bulk-like samples; rigid four-corner grids diagnose when kinetics or boundary conditions freeze the internal stress field that drives nonlinear coupling.

\subsection{Alternative explanations and numerical controls}
\label{sec:alternative}
Several non-physical mechanisms could mimic a four-corner cross term if corners were computed inconsistently; we address them explicitly.
\emph{(i) Finite-size / electrostatic dilution:} $|\mathcal{S}|$ is not monotonic in $n$ and can peak at $n{=}1$ (P) or change sign between $n{=}6$ and $n{=}8$ for N (Supplemental Material), inconsistent with a single image-sum artifact.
\emph{(ii) Charge localization / SCF metastability:} all reported corners show converged SCF totals under identical OT settings; tetramer $P$ metastability at $+2.5$\% is excluded from $\alpha$ fits but does not enter the four-corner $\mathcal{S}$ grids audited here.
\emph{(iii) Pulay stress and basis-set incompleteness:} comparisons at fixed $n$ use matched 400~Ry cutoffs; the partial cutoff audit shifts one corner by only ${\sim}0.057$~meV/atom, far below the quoted $|\mathcal{S}|$ for $n\leq 4$.
\emph{(iv) Numerical cancellation in Eq.~\eqref{eq:four_corner}:} because all four totals share the same functional, k-point ($\Gamma$), and cell shape, spurious additive offsets cancel; remaining uncertainty is bounded by $\sigma_{\mathcal{S}}\lesssim 2$~meV/atom (Sec.~\ref{sec:methods_coupling}).
None of these controls eliminate the P-dominated geometric-nonlinear channel; they set the scale below which we would not interpret $|{\mathcal{S}}|$ as physics rather than protocol noise.

\subsection{Predictive use of four-corner audits}
\label{sec:predictive}
The matched $\mathcal{S}$ grid is intentionally cheap relative to full ionic relaxation at every $(\epsilon,\delta)$ point: once four rigid-strain corners converge, $\mathcal{S}$ forecasts whether a sequential strain-plus-doping screen will mis-rank substitutional stability before expensive relaxations or transport calculations are deployed.
In practice, $|{\mathcal{S}}|>{\sim}2$~meV/atom at $+3$\% load flags candidates for mandatory coupled re-evaluation; P on the production map exceeds this threshold by an order of magnitude while B and N often remain near the noise floor.
This is a \emph{stability-ranking} tool under stated boundary conditions, not a universal replacement for fully relaxed formation energies.

\subsection{Limitations and outlook}
Main-text $\alpha$ and $\mathcal{S}$ use PBE+D3 under the rigid-strain protocol (Sec.~\ref{sec:notation}); ionic relaxation releases internal stress and can reverse or nearly extinguish $\mathcal{S}$ (Table~\ref{tab:II}; periodic $n{=}1$ P).
Although full relaxation suppresses equilibrium $|\mathcal{S}|$, rigid-strain audits bound coupling under epitaxial clamping, substrate adhesion, or ultrafast loading when ionic relaxation is kinetically restricted.
Periodic $\mathcal{S}(n)$ magnitudes are configuration-specific on a fixed production site map (two independent $n{=}4$ maps preserve P$>$B$>$N; Table~\ref{tab:IV}).
Core claims use matched 400~Ry for $n\leq 4$; $n\geq 6$ production points use 350~Ry.
Table~\ref{tab:III} geometry is a tetramer proxy; the geometric vs.\ electronic channel assignment rests on $\Delta\bar{d}$, $\Delta r_{\mathrm{cov}}$, and Hirshfeld $\Delta q$ rather than a full energy decomposition.
Because $\mathcal{S}$ is a same-functional difference, absolute errors in substitution energy do not affect our conclusion of non-additive coupling.
Per-corner SCF convergence and the $\sigma_{\mathcal{S}}\approx 2\sigma_e$ propagation bound in Sec.~\ref{sec:methods_coupling} supply internal uncertainty estimates; we do not quote experimental $\pm$ on bond lengths because Table~\ref{tab:III} reports DFT-internal geometry only.
With only three substitutional labels, Pearson, Spearman, and $R^2$ statistics on the $\alpha$--$|\mathcal{S}|$ map are illustrative ($n{=}3$); bootstrap resampling would not add robustness without additional converged dopant points.
Claims are scoped to qHP C$_{60}$ on fixed site maps; graphene-like rigid lattices or COF bilayers~\cite{Alihosseini2023strain} may show smaller cross terms and require separate four-corner benchmarks.
Future work includes Mayer/Bader strain-path partitioning, additional III/V substituents on matched maps, direct $\mathcal{S}(\mathrm{cutoff})$, $\mathcal{S}(k)$, and $\mathcal{S}(\mathrm{EPS\_SCF})$ sensitivity curves, and a representative SCAN or r$^2$SCAN spot-check on a high-$|\mathcal{S}|$ corner.

\section{\label{sec:conclusions}Conclusions}

Nonlinear strain--dopant energetic coupling---not the symbol $\mathcal{S}$---is the primary finding: joint load on qHP C$_{60}$ breaks additive composition--strain workflows when dopant-induced internal stress meets external biaxial strain.
The matched four-corner difference $\mathcal{S}$ is the gauge-fixed estimator of that omitted bilinear term [Eqs.~\eqref{eq:taylor_coupling}--\eqref{eq:four_corner}], equivalent to a discrete mixed derivative of total energy rather than a new thermodynamic observable.
On qHP C$_{60}$, donor/acceptor dopants produce an $\alpha$ sign split [Fig.~\ref{fig:decoupling}(d)] that does not reliably predict $|\mathcal{S}|$, while periodic $|\mathcal{S}|$ reaches $31.9$~meV/atom for P at $n{=}1$ at fixed coordinates---an extremum within a broader $2.2$--$31.9$~meV/atom audit distribution (Fig.~\ref{fig:synergy}(d)).
Three channels---local bond stretching, charge redistribution, and elastic frustration---jointly explain why P dominates the geometric-nonlinear regime without reducing the argument to covalent radius alone (Sec.~\ref{sec:mechanistic}).
Rigid-strain $\mathcal{S}$ bounds coupling under mechanically constrained loading (Sec.~\ref{sec:constrained_loading}); equilibrium relaxation releases internal stress and can nearly extinguish the cross term.
Coupled $(\epsilon,\delta)$ screening is therefore advisable for $|\Delta r_{\mathrm{cov}}|\gtrsim 20$~pm substituents and $+3$\% loads; four-corner audits can pre-screen such cases before full relaxation (Sec.~\ref{sec:predictive}).
Generalization beyond qHP C$_{60}$ is likely only after matched four-corner audits on each host; the present study documents the phenomenon and audit protocol on a soft covalent molecular network rather than claiming universal constants or materials-general laws.
